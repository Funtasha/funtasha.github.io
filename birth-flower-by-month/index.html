Where the list comes from

No official body keeps the birth flower list. The list on this page is the US list most sources follow, from The Old Farmer's Almanac, and it comes from the Victorian language of flowers — a fashion for giving each flower a symbolic meaning, so a bunch of flowers could carry a message. That is why every flower above has a meaning attached to it: the meanings are part of the tradition, not a discovery about the flowers themselves.

Because the list is a custom rather than a rule, different lists do not always agree. UK lists differ for some months, and November is a good example of the variation: this list gives Chrysanthemum as the month's flower, while some lists add Peony as a secondary flower. If a list you have seen elsewhere names a different flower for your month, neither list is wrong — they are just different versions of the same tradition.

How to use a birth flower

A birth flower is an easy way to make a birthday gift feel personal. A small bouquet of the month's flower works on its own, and the flower's colours can set the palette for party decor — table flowers, a cake topper, or a printed pattern on invitations and cards. For a gift that lasts longer than fresh flowers, the flower can appear as a print, an embroidered motif, or a pressed flower in a frame.

Birth flowers follow the calendar month, not the star sign, so the flower depends only on the month of the birthday. Fresh flowers stay at their best when kept cool and in water.

Questions people ask

How do I find my birth flower?

Pick your birth month in the finder above. It shows the month's primary flower and its secondary flower, with the traditional meaning of each.

Why does my birth month have two flowers?

Most months on this list have a primary flower and a secondary one. Either one counts as the month's flower, so you can choose the one you like best.

Do birth flowers follow my star sign?

No. Birth flowers follow the calendar month you were born in, not your star sign. A person born on 15 March has the March flowers whatever their sign.

Are the meanings of the flowers real?

The meanings come from the Victorian language of flowers, a tradition of giving flowers symbolic meanings. They are folklore, not facts, so enjoy them as a story rather than a rule.

What if my birth flower is out of season?

Any flower makes a fine gift, whatever the month. If your birth flower is hard to find, a florist can suggest a lookalike, or you can use the flower as a colour or a pattern on a card instead.
