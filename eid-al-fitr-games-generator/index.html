How the games generator works

Choose who is playing from the list, and press New game. The generator picks a game that suits your group, and shows you its name, how many players it needs, and how to play it. Every game uses things you already have at home: paper, pens, cushions, balloons, and sweets.

If a game does not suit your family, press the button again for another one. There are eighteen games in all, split between children, adults, and everyone together, so you can keep the afternoon going for as long as anyone wants to play.

Games for children

The children's games are active, simple, and full of small surprises. Scavenger hunts, musical cushions, and pass the parcel all work because they give every child a turn and a chance to win something small. Nobody needs to read well, and nobody is left out.

Keep the rules short and the rounds quick. Young children lose interest in a long game, but they will happily play five short ones in a row. Let the winner of one round choose the next game, and praise effort as loudly as victory.

Games for adults

The adult games are quieter and more talkative: trivia, storytelling, and guessing games that let people show what they know and remember. They work best after the meal, when everyone is sitting together and nobody wants to move.

Two truths and a lie is a favourite at family gatherings, because it turns out that everyone has a surprising story. Story chain is just as good: the tale grows sillier with every sentence, and by the end of the round the whole room is laughing.

Games the whole family can play

The games marked for everyone are the heart of the generator. Pictionary, balloon pop quiz, and would-you-rather all work across ages, because they do not depend on speed or strength. A grandparent and a grandchild can play as equals, and often the grandchild wins.

Mixed-age games also bring the family together in a way that separate games cannot. When everyone plays the same game, the table becomes one conversation, and the youngest and oldest members of the family share the same laughter.

Running a games afternoon

A good games afternoon needs very little planning. Pick three or four games in advance, gather the things you need in one place, and let the generator fill the gaps. Start with an active game while everyone has energy, then move to quieter ones as the afternoon goes on.

Appoint one person to keep score if the games need it, and let someone else be in charge of the music and the snacks. The host should play too: a games afternoon works best when nobody is stuck in the kitchen the whole time.

Leave gaps between games for food, conversation, and rest. A games afternoon is not a race, and the best moments often happen between the games, when people are laughing about the last round and choosing the next one.

Games for a rainy Eid day

If the weather keeps everyone indoors on Eid day, games become even more important. A rainy day with a full house can feel long, but a few good games turn it into a day people remember fondly. The generator is made for exactly this: press the button, read the instructions, and play.

Indoor games need a little space and a lot of goodwill. Move the breakables out of the way, lay a rug on the floor for the children, and keep the noisy games to the biggest room. A house full of people playing together is the sound of a happy Eid, whatever the weather outside.

Prizes that cost nothing

The best prizes at a family games afternoon cost nothing at all. The winner chooses the next game, picks the music, or gets the first serving of dessert. A paper crown, a silly title, or a round of applause works just as well as anything bought.

If you do want small prizes, keep them tiny and equal: a sweet for every player, and a slightly bigger sweet for the winner. Nobody should go home empty-handed, and nobody should feel that losing mattered.

Games for guests who arrive late

Guests often arrive at different times on Eid day, and a good host has a game ready for latecomers. Pick a game that people can join in the middle, such as trivia or would-you-rather, rather than one that needs everyone from the start.

The generator helps here too: press New game when a new guest arrives, and let them join the fresh round. It is a kind way to welcome someone, and it gives them a role in the gathering straight away.

Keeping the peace: fair play at Eid

Games are meant to be fun, and the fastest way to spoil one is to argue about the rules. Decide the rules before you start, keep them simple, and let one person settle disputes. If a game starts to upset someone, stop it and choose another.

Children take losing hard, and adults sometimes do too. Praise good play, congratulate the winner, and move on to the next game. The point of the afternoon is togetherness, and a kind game is worth more than a clever one.

All the games on this page are for fun, with no betting and no prizes of money. Keep play kind: if a game upsets someone, stop and choose another.

Questions people ask

How many games are there?

There are eighteen games in the generator: six for children, six for adults, and six for everyone together. Choosing Everyone draws from all eighteen at once.

Do the games need special equipment?

No. Every game uses things most homes already have: paper, pens, cushions, balloons, sweets, and a few household items. Nothing needs to be bought specially.

Are the games suitable for young children?

Yes, when you choose the Children group. Those games are simple, active, and need no reading. Younger children may still need an adult to explain the rules.

Can I play with a large group?

Yes. Each game shows how many players it suits, and most of them work for groups of up to fifteen or twenty. For very large gatherings, split into two teams.

What if a game does not suit our family?

Press New game again for another one. The generator never picks the same game twice in a row, and you can keep pressing until you find one that fits.

Does the generator work on a phone?

Yes. The generator runs entirely in your browser with no sign-up and no download, and it works on phones, tablets, and computers.
