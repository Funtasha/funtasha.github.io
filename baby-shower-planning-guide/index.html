Baby Shower Planning Guide

This guide walks you through planning a baby shower, from the timeline and guest list to food, games, gifts and thank-you notes. Use the checklist to keep track of every task, and pick a theme to get decoration and food ideas in one place.

Start with a simple timeline

A baby shower is usually held a few weeks before the due date, when the parent-to-be can still move around comfortably and enjoy the day. Pick the date first, then work backwards: invitations go out a few weeks before the party, the venue and theme are settled before that, and the guest list comes first of all. A short written timeline keeps the whole plan calm, because every decision has a place to land.

Keep the day itself short and relaxed. Two or three hours is plenty for a shower: guests arrive, everyone eats, a game or two is played, gifts are opened, and the parent-to-be rests. Build in a quiet moment for photos and a proper goodbye, and do not pack the schedule so tightly that nobody has time to chat.

Build the guest list with the parent-to-be

The guest list is the parent-to-be's call, so sit down together and write it out. Close family and friends come first, then anyone else the parent-to-be would love to see. Ask about plus-ones early, and agree on a number the venue and the food can comfortably handle. A smaller, warmer gathering beats a crowded room where the guest of honour cannot talk to anyone.

Once the list is set, collect addresses or contact details in one place. If some guests live far away, ask the parent-to-be whether a video call during the party would be welcome — it is a kind way to include someone who cannot travel, and it takes only a few minutes to arrange.

Choose a venue that fits the guest list

A home or garden suits a small shower and keeps the mood relaxed. A community room, cafe or restaurant works better for a bigger group, because the space, seating and clean-up are already handled. Whichever you choose, check the practical details: enough chairs, a table for food and gifts, somewhere quiet for the parent-to-be to sit, and easy access for anyone with limited mobility.

Visit the venue if you can, and picture the day there. Where will guests arrive? Where does the food go? Is there shade or shelter if the weather turns? Answering these questions in advance means no scrambling on the day.

Pick a theme and keep it simple

A theme gives the party a look without much effort. Woodland animals, storybooks, a tea party, rainbows or a garden theme all work well, and a simple colour palette does most of the work. Choose two or three colours and repeat them in the tablecloth, balloons and napkins — the room will look put together even with very few decorations.

Decorate in the morning, not the night before, and keep the parent-to-be out of the lifting and climbing. A welcome sign, a few bunches of balloons and a dressed table are enough. The best decoration at a baby shower is a relaxed guest of honour.

Food and non-alcoholic drinks

Finger food suits a shower best: small sandwiches, fruit platters, pastries and a cake are easy to serve and easy to eat while chatting. A drinks station with sparkling water, fruit juice and a simple punch lets guests help themselves, and keeping every drink non-alcoholic means everyone at the party can join in. Ask about allergies and dietary needs on the invitation, and label anything that contains common allergens.

Plan the food in waves so the table stays fresh: put out half the food at the start and refill it midway through. Keep the cake for the last half hour, when everyone is settled and the parent-to-be is ready for the photos.

Games that everyone can join

Two or three short games are plenty, and the best ones need no special supplies. Baby bingo, guessing the due date, and a quick quiz about the parent-to-be all work for mixed ages. Keep the rules short, let people sit out if they prefer, and hand out small prizes so nobody leaves empty-handed.

If the parent-to-be is not keen on games, skip them entirely and let the conversation carry the day. A wishing tree, where guests write a short note for the baby, is a gentle alternative that doubles as a keepsake.

Gifts and etiquette

A gift list makes life easier for guests and for the parent-to-be. If the parents have made one, share it with the invitation; if not, suggest practical items in a range of sizes, from small everyday things to bigger pieces. Group gifts are a kind option for larger items, and a few guests chipping in together can give something genuinely useful.

Opening gifts in front of everyone is traditional but not required. If the parent-to-be would rather open them later, say so in advance so nobody is surprised. Either way, keep a list of who gave what — it makes the thank-you notes much easier.

Thank-you notes

Thank-you notes are the parent-to-be's job, but the host can help by keeping the gift list and noting down the giver of each present. Notes should go out within a few weeks of the party, and a few warm sentences are enough: name the gift, say how it will be used, and thank the giver for coming.

If writing notes feels like a lot in the final weeks before the baby arrives, short handwritten cards or even a thoughtful message each work well. What matters is that every guest feels thanked, not that every note is long.

Questions people ask

When should a baby shower be held?

A few weeks before the due date usually works best, when the parent-to-be can still enjoy the day comfortably. Pick a date together and work backwards from there.

Who hosts a baby shower?

A close friend or family member usually hosts, sometimes with a few helpers. The host plans the day, and the parent-to-be simply enjoys it.

Do I invite people who live far away?

Ask the parent-to-be first. If someone cannot travel, a short video call during the party is a kind way to include them.

What if the parent-to-be does not want games?

Skip them. A wishing tree or a simple message book gives guests something to do without putting anyone on the spot.

How soon should thank-you notes go out?

Within a few weeks of the party is the usual timing. A few warm sentences naming the gift are enough.
