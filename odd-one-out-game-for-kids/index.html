Odd One Out Game for Kids

Tap the odd one out from 44 original sets across Animals, Food, Objects and Nature, made for children aged 5 to 10. Every answer comes with a short explanation, and there is no timer and no pressure.

This game is made for relaxed play. There are no timers, no penalties for wrong taps, and no scores that can run out — children earn stars for correct answers and can try again as often as they like.

Stars: 0

How to play

Tap "New set" to see four things. Three of them belong together and one is the odd one out. Tap the one you think does not belong. If you are right, you earn a star and learn why. If you are not, just try again — there is no timer and no pressure.

The game is made for children aged 5 to 10. Younger players can play with a grown-up reading the words aloud, and older children can try to explain the reason in their own words before tapping an answer.

Why odd one out helps learning

Spotting the odd one out builds sorting and thinking skills. Children learn to group things by what they share — where they live, what they are made of, or what they are used for — and to notice the one thing that breaks the pattern. Talking about the reason matters more than getting the answer quickly.

Questions people ask

What age is the odd one out game for?

The sets are written for children aged 5 to 10. Younger children can play with a grown-up reading the words aloud, and older children can try to explain the reason before tapping an answer.

Is there a timer or a score that can run out?

No. There is no timer and no pressure, and a wrong tap never costs a star. Stars are only earned for correct answers, so children can take their time and try again.

What if my child taps the wrong answer?

The game simply says to have another try, with no penalty. The explanation appears once the odd one out is found, so every set ends with the reason why.

How many sets are there?

There are 44 sets across four groups: Animals, Food, Objects and Nature. Use the group menu to play one group at a time, or play all of them mixed together.

Can I use this game in a classroom?

Yes. The sets work well on a shared screen, with children taking turns to tap an answer and explain their thinking to the class.
