ChatGPT Shortcuts

By the Funtasha team · Published 20 November 2026 · Updated 20 November 2026

A ChatGPT shortcut is a short reusable instruction that does a job in one line — summarise this, rewrite it shorter, turn it into a plan. This guide collects the shortcuts people use every day, and shows how to save your own so you never type them twice.

Shortcuts save typing, but they do not check facts. Read the answer before you use it — especially for anything with dates, numbers, or advice you will act on.

What a ChatGPT shortcut is

A ChatGPT shortcut is a short instruction you reuse instead of typing the same long request every time. Instead of writing a paragraph about what you want, you keep a one-line version that does the job: Summarise this in five bullet points. Rewrite this shorter. Turn this into a plan. Paste your text after it, send it, and the answer comes back in the shape you asked for.

Shortcuts matter because ChatGPT is used so much. It is the most-searched term in the United States, with about 93.8 million US searches a month and about 124 million worldwide. Millions of people type the same kinds of requests every day — summarise this, explain that, help me plan — and the regulars are the ones who stopped typing them out longhand. A shortcut is simply the request, trimmed down to the words that do the work.

A shortcut is not a magic spell. It is a clear instruction, and it works for the same reason any clear instruction works: it tells ChatGPT exactly what to do, in what shape, and what to leave out. The picker above gives you one for each everyday job, ready to copy.

The everyday shortcuts worth knowing

Five shortcuts cover most of what people ask ChatGPT to do. Learn these and the rest is variation.

  • Summarise this. Paste a long text and ask for the main ideas in five short bullet points. It turns a wall of words into something you can read in a minute.
  • Explain it like I am five. Ask for a simple explanation with one everyday example. It is the fastest way into a topic you know nothing about.
  • Rewrite it shorter. Paste a draft and ask for the same meaning in fewer words. It tightens emails, notes and messages without changing what you meant.
  • Make a plan. State a goal and ask for steps, with the first step being something you can do today. A plan you can start is worth more than a perfect plan you cannot.
  • Turn this into a list. Paste a rambling paragraph and ask for a checklist. It turns thinking into doing.

Each of these is one line, and each does one job. That is the whole secret: one job, one line, and the words pasted after it.

How to save instructions so you type them once

Some instructions you want in every chat: answer in short sentences, keep it simple, use bullet points, never use jargon. Typing those every time defeats the purpose of a shortcut, so ChatGPT lets you save instructions that it uses in new chats. Look for the saved instructions or personalisation area in the settings, and write a few short lines about how you like answers.

Keep saved instructions short — three or four lines is plenty. Say how you like answers shaped (short, simple, bullet points), and add anything that is always true, like the language you write in or the fact that you are planning a trip. Long lists of instructions tend to fight each other, and ChatGPT follows short ones more reliably.

Saved instructions apply to new chats, not to ones you already started. And they are not set in stone: check them now and then, and change them when your needs change. A shortcut you saved for exam season may not suit a holiday plan.

Chaining shortcuts together

One shortcut does one job. Two in a row do something better. Chaining means using the answer from one shortcut as the starting point for the next, and it is how simple shortcuts turn into real work.

Take a long article. First shortcut: summarise it in five bullet points. Second shortcut: turn those points into a checklist of things to do. The summary gives you understanding; the checklist gives you action. Or take a rough draft: first rewrite it shorter, then ask for a friendlier version of the shorter text. Each step improves the result, and each step is easy to check.

The trick is to check the answer between steps. If the summary is wrong, the checklist built on it will be wrong too. Read each result, fix what is off, and only then move to the next shortcut. Chaining saves time because each step is small — and small steps are easy to trust.

The shortcuts that waste your time

Not every short instruction is a good shortcut. The ones that waste time are the vague ones: make it better, fix this, write something good. They sound like shortcuts, but they ask ChatGPT to guess what you want — and a guess is rarely what you meant.

The fix is to say what better means. Better how? Shorter? Simpler? Funnier? More formal? Pick one and say it: make this shorter and simpler. Give it a shape: five bullet points, under a hundred words, as a checklist. A shortcut with a shape gets an answer with a shape; a shortcut without one gets whatever ChatGPT feels like.

The other time-waster is asking for several jobs at once: summarise this, rewrite it, translate it and make a plan. Split it. One job per message is faster in the end, because you can check each answer and fix it before the next step.

Shortcuts for work and study

Shortcuts earn their keep at work and in study, where the same jobs come around every week. Meeting notes become an action list. A long report becomes five key points. A rough email becomes a polished one. Study notes become quiz questions you can test yourself with.

The pattern is always the same: paste the material, name the job, name the shape. Summarise these notes into five action points with a name next to each one. Turn this chapter into ten quiz questions with the answers at the end. The more specific the shape, the more useful the answer.

Keep the human in charge. A shortcut can draft, summarise and organise, but it cannot know what matters to you. Read the answer, fix the mistakes, add your own judgement, and never send something you have not read. The shortcut does the typing; the thinking stays yours.

Getting ChatGPT to follow the shortcut

Sometimes ChatGPT ignores a shortcut — it answers in paragraphs when you asked for bullets, or writes an essay when you asked for five points. Usually the request was vague, or it asked for several things at once. Shorten it to one job, name the shape you want, and try again.

Repeating the key words helps. If the answer comes back too long, say so plainly: shorter — five points only. If it misses the point, paste the important part again and ask for that alone. ChatGPT follows the last clear instruction best, so make the last thing you say the thing that matters most.

If a conversation has gone off track, start a fresh chat. Long chats collect old instructions that fight the new ones, and a clean start with a single shortcut often fixes what three corrections could not.

Questions people ask

What is a ChatGPT shortcut?

A ChatGPT shortcut is a short, reusable instruction that does one job in a single line — for example, summarise this in five bullet points. You paste your text after it and send it, instead of typing out a long request every time.

How do I save instructions in ChatGPT?

ChatGPT has a place for saved instructions in its settings, where you can write a few short lines about how you like answers — short sentences, bullet points, simple words. They apply to new chats, so keep them short and update them when your needs change.

What is the best shortcut for summarising?

Summarise this in five short bullet points, keeping only the main ideas. The number keeps it tight and the word only keeps out the detail. Change the number to fit the text — three points for a short note, ten for a long report.

Can shortcuts write my homework?

They can help you understand a topic and plan your work, but handing in AI-written work as your own is not honest, and most schools and colleges treat it as cheating. Use shortcuts to learn the topic, then write the work in your own words.

Why does ChatGPT ignore my shortcut sometimes?

Usually because the request is vague or asks for several things at once. Shorten it to one job, name the shape you want — bullets, five points, under a hundred words — and try again. A fresh chat helps when the conversation has gone off track.

Do shortcuts work on the free plan?

Yes. A shortcut is just words you type, so it works on any plan, free or paid. The free plan may have limits on how much you can use in a day, but the shortcuts themselves cost nothing to use.
