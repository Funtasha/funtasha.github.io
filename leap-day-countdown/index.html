Why leap days exist

A year on the calendar has 365 days, but the Earth takes a little longer than that to travel once around the Sun — about 365 days and a quarter. That leftover quarter of a day is small, but it adds up: after four years it has grown into a whole extra day.

If we ignored it, the calendar would slowly drift away from the seasons. After a century, the months would sit weeks away from the weather they used to match, and after a few centuries summer would fall in what the calendar called winter. The leap day stops that drift by adding one day, 29 February, every four years.

The fix is not perfect, because the Earth's journey is slightly shorter than 365 and a quarter days. That is where the century rule comes in: century years skip the leap day unless they divide by 400, which keeps the calendar in step for thousands of years.

The leap-year rule, step by step

To decide whether a year is a leap year, work through the rule in order:

  1. Divide by four. If the year does not divide evenly by four, it is an ordinary year with 365 days.
  2. Check the century. If the year ends in two noughts, such as 1900 or 2100, it is a century year, and the rule changes.
  3. Divide the century by 400. A century year is a leap year only if it divides evenly by 400. The year 2000 was a leap year; the year 2100 will not be.

The countdown above applies all three steps, so it never mistakes a skipped century year for a leap year. A leap year has 366 days, and the extra day is always 29 February.

Questions people ask

When is the next Leap Day?

The next Leap Day is the next 29 February in a leap year. The countdown at the top of this page shows it and the time left until it begins.

Why do we need leap years?

The Earth takes about 365 and a quarter days to orbit the Sun. Without an extra day every four years, the calendar would drift away from the seasons.

Is every fourth year a leap year?

Almost. Century years, such as 1900 and 2100, skip the leap day unless they divide evenly by 400 — the year 2000 was a leap year because it divides by 400.

What happens to people born on 29 February?

People born on 29 February celebrate their birthday in ordinary years on 28 February or 1 March, whichever they prefer. Their birthday still comes around every year — only the date 29 February is rare.

How long is a leap year?

A leap year has 366 days. The extra day is 29 February, added to February, which has 29 days instead of 28 in a leap year.
