Kling is an AI video tool known for realistic people and smooth camera moves. This guide covers the free daily credits, the watermark on free exports, the camera words that steer a shot, and eight prompts you can copy.

What Kling is good at

Kling is an AI video tool with a reputation for two things: realistic people and smooth camera moves. Where some tools turn a walking person into a wobbling shape, Kling keeps the walk steady and the body believable. Where some tools ignore camera words, Kling follows them: ask for a slow orbit and you get a slow orbit.

That is why creators search for it. Searches for 'kling ai' run at around 1.1 million a month, as of November 2026, and most of those searchers want the same two things: a person who moves like a person, and a camera that moves like a camera. Kling is built for both.

Kling works from text or from an image. Type a prompt and it builds a clip from words, or upload a photo and it animates the photo into motion. Image-to-video is the stronger starting point for products and portraits, because the tool keeps what your photo already got right.

Getting started: your first clip

Start with an account and a simple idea. Pick one subject, one action, and one camera move — a dog walking toward the camera, a slow push-in — and write it as one sentence. Simple prompts fail less often than busy ones, and your first clip should teach you how the tool responds.

  1. Open Kling and start a new text-to-video or image-to-video clip.
  2. Write one sentence: subject, action, camera move.
  3. Pick the shape you need: 9:16 for TikTok and Shorts, 16:9 for a wide screen.
  4. Generate the clip and watch it all the way through.
  5. If it warps, change one thing — the camera move, or the action — and try again.

Keep your first five clips simple on purpose. Each one teaches you a word or two that Kling responds to, and by the fifth you will know how much detail your prompts need.

Camera moves explained, with examples

Camera words are the steering wheel of a Kling prompt. They tell the tool how the viewpoint moves, and they change the feel of a clip more than any other single word. Five moves cover most jobs.

  • Dolly — the camera moves closer to or further from the subject. 'Slow dolly in on a baker kneading dough.'
  • Orbit — the camera circles the subject. 'Slow orbit around a handmade vase on a wooden table.'
  • Pan — the camera turns left or right. 'Slow pan across a busy market street at dawn.'
  • Crane — the camera rises or falls. 'Crane shot rising over a garden at sunrise.'
  • Tracking — the camera follows a moving subject. 'Tracking shot following a cyclist along a quiet road.'

Use one camera move per clip. Two moves in one prompt fight each other, and the tool usually picks one and ignores the other — or mixes them into a wobble. If you want two moves, make two clips and stitch them.

The free-tier math

Kling's free tier gives free daily credits, as of November 2026. That covers a small number of short clips each day — enough to test ideas and to make a clip or two for a post, but not enough to produce a whole video in one sitting. Plan your clips before you spend credits, and keep the best takes.

Free exports carry a watermark. That is the honest price of the free tier: your clips will show a Kling mark. If a clean clip matters, Flow leaves only an invisible SynthID mark on most free clips, and Wan 2.2 has no watermark at all because it runs on your own hardware.

Paid plans start from ~$7–10/mo. The paid tier removes the watermark and raises the limits. Whether it is worth it depends on how many clips you make: a few clips a month fits the free tier, and a clip a day is where the paid tier starts to make sense.

Fixing warps and wobbles

Every AI video tool warps sometimes: a hand melts, a face shifts, a background ripples. Kling warps less than most on people, but it still warps, especially on fast motion and small details. The fixes are simple, and they work on every tool.

First, shorten the prompt. A prompt with one subject, one action, and one camera move gives the tool less to juggle. Second, slow the motion down: 'slow orbit' fails less than 'fast orbit'. Third, start from an image when you can — image-to-video keeps the subject steady because the tool is animating something real.

If a clip still fails, change one thing and try again. Never re-run the exact same prompt hoping for luck: the same words usually give the same warp. Change the camera move, or the action, or the light, and the next try has a real chance.

When Kling beats the others

Kling beats the others when the shot needs a believable person or a deliberate camera move. A product walk-around, a portrait turning to camera, a tracking shot down a street — these are Kling's home ground, and the results show it.

Flow beats Kling when the clip must be clean. Flow's 50 free credits a day buy five 8-second Veo 3.1 Lite clips with no visible watermark, only an invisible SynthID mark, which matters for a product demo or a clip a client will see.

Runway's place is the trial: 125 credits, once, to see whether the tool suits you. Use the trial to compare, then spend your daily effort where the results are best. The honest answer for most creators is a mix: Kling for motion, Flow for clean clips, and CapCut to finish.

Questions people ask

Is Kling free?

Kling has a free tier with free daily credits, as of November 2026. Free exports carry a watermark, and paid plans start from ~$7–10/mo.

Does Kling put a watermark on free videos?

Yes. Free Kling exports carry a watermark. If you need a clean clip, Flow leaves only an invisible SynthID mark on most free clips, and Wan 2.2 has no watermark at all because it runs on your own hardware.

How long are Kling clips?

Like most free generators, Kling makes short clips of 5 to 15 seconds. Longer videos are made by stitching several clips together and adding narration.

What happened to Sora?

Sora shut down on 26 April 2026, and the Sora API shut down on 24 September 2026. OpenAI announced the shutdown on 24 March 2026 and named no successor. Guides that still list Sora as a working tool are out of date.

Is Kling better than Flow?

It depends on the job. Kling is stronger for realistic people and camera moves. Flow is stronger when you need a clip with no visible watermark, with 50 free credits a day.

Can I use Kling clips commercially?

Check Kling's terms on the day you use it. Free tiers often limit commercial use, and the rules change. Keep your own photos and words in the clip, and read the terms before you sell with it.
