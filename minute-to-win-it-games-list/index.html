Minute to Win It Games

Pick from 24 minute-to-win-it challenges across Balance, Speed, Memory and Team, each using household items only and lasting exactly 60 seconds. Every challenge has setup, rules and scoring, plus a built-in timer.

Clear the playing area before active games, use soft items for throwing challenges, and skip any challenge that does not suit a player's age or mobility. A quick tidy between rounds keeps everyone safe.

60 seconds on the clock

How minute to win it works

Each challenge lasts exactly 60 seconds and uses household items only — cups, coins, socks, spoons and paper. Play one challenge at a time, with everyone taking a turn, or run several players at once and compare scores. Keep a scoreboard across five or six challenges and crown the overall winner at the end.

For mixed ages, pair younger players with an older partner, or give children a simpler version of the challenge — a shorter distance, fewer items, or a helping hand. The aim is laughter, not perfection.

Safety note for active games

Clear the playing area of anything sharp or breakable before you start, and keep the floor dry. Use soft items where the game involves throwing or balancing, and skip any challenge that does not suit a player's age or mobility. A quick tidy between rounds keeps everyone safe.

Balance

Cookie Face

Setup: Each player leans their head back with a biscuit on their forehead.

Rules: Move the biscuit to your mouth using only your face muscles, with no hands.

Scoring: One point for each biscuit eaten in 60 seconds.

Cup Stack Pyramid

Setup: Give each player 15 plastic cups.

Rules: Stack the cups into a pyramid, then take it down into a single stack.

Scoring: The fastest complete pyramid and take-down wins.

Book Balance Walk

Setup: Mark a start line and a finish line a few steps apart.

Rules: Walk to the finish and back with a book balanced on your head, without touching it.

Scoring: One point for each completed trip; start again if the book falls.

Spoon and Cotton Ball Race

Setup: Give each player a spoon and a cotton ball.

Rules: Carry the cotton ball on the spoon from one end of the room to the other and back.

Scoring: One point per completed trip; the cotton ball must stay on the spoon.

Pencil Balance

Setup: Each player holds a pencil upright on the back of one hand.

Rules: Balance the pencil for as long as you can without touching it with the other hand.

Scoring: One point for every ten seconds balanced.

Paper Plate Spin

Setup: Give each player a paper plate.

Rules: Spin the plate on one finger and keep it spinning.

Scoring: One point for every five seconds it keeps spinning.

Speed

Sock Sort Sprint

Setup: Mix ten pairs of socks in a pile.

Rules: Match the socks into pairs as fast as you can.

Scoring: One point for each correct pair in 60 seconds.

Coin Stack

Setup: Give each player 20 coins.

Rules: Stack the coins one by one into a single tower.

Scoring: The tallest standing tower when time ends wins; a fallen tower scores nothing.

Straw Pickup

Setup: Scatter 20 straws on a table.

Rules: Pick up the straws one at a time using only one straw held in your mouth.

Scoring: One point for each straw collected.

Ping Pong Bounce

Setup: Place six cups in a row and give each player a ping pong ball.

Rules: Bounce the ball into the cups, one cup at a time.

Scoring: One point for each ball that lands in a cup.

Paper Clip Chain

Setup: Give each player a pile of paper clips.

Rules: Link the clips into the longest chain you can.

Scoring: One point for each clip in the longest unbroken chain.

Dice Stack

Setup: Give each player five dice and a small stick or ruler.

Rules: Balance the dice one on top of another using the stick.

Scoring: The tallest standing stack when time ends wins.

Memory

Tray Memory

Setup: Place 15 small household items on a tray and cover it.

Rules: Uncover the tray for 20 seconds, cover it again, then write down everything you remember.

Scoring: One point for each correct item.

Card Match Dash

Setup: Lay out 12 cards face down in a grid.

Rules: Turn over two cards at a time to find matching pairs.

Scoring: One point for each pair found in 60 seconds.

Shopping List Recall

Setup: The host reads a list of 12 items once.

Rules: Players write down as many items as they remember.

Scoring: One point for each correct item.

Colour Sequence

Setup: The host shows a sequence of five colours.

Rules: Players repeat the sequence from memory, then the host adds one colour each round.

Scoring: One point for each correct round.

Name That Tune Humming

Setup: One player hums a well-known tune.

Rules: The other players guess the tune within 60 seconds.

Scoring: One point for each correct guess; the hummer changes each round.

Picture Puzzle Recall

Setup: Show a photo or picture for 20 seconds, then hide it.

Rules: Players answer questions about details in the picture.

Scoring: One point for each correct answer.

Team

Human Knot Relay

Setup: Teams stand in a circle and join hands across the group.

Rules: Untangle the knot without letting go of hands.

Scoring: The first team untangled wins.

Cup Pass Relay

Setup: Teams line up with a cup of water each.

Rules: Pass the cup over your head to the person behind you; the last person pours it into a jug.

Scoring: The team with the most water in the jug wins.

Balloon Keep-Up

Setup: Give each team a balloon.

Rules: Keep the balloon in the air using only gentle taps, with no holding.

Scoring: One point for every ten seconds the balloon stays up.

Paper Plane Flight

Setup: Each team folds a paper plane.

Rules: Throw the planes from a line and measure the distance.

Scoring: The longest flight wins; teams get three throws and keep their best.

Tower Build

Setup: Give each team 20 sheets of newspaper and tape.

Rules: Build the tallest free-standing tower you can.

Scoring: The tallest tower standing when time ends wins.

Word Chain Team Race

Setup: Teams take turns naming words in a category.

Rules: Each word must start with the last letter of the previous word, with no repeats.

Scoring: One point per word; a repeat or a pause longer than five seconds ends the turn.

Questions people ask

How does a minute to win it game work?

Each challenge lasts exactly 60 seconds and uses household items only. Players take turns or play at the same time, and the best score in the time wins the round.

What do we need to play?

Only everyday household items: plastic cups, coins, socks, spoons, paper and balloons. Every challenge on this page lists what you need in its setup.

How many challenges should we play?

Five or six challenges make a great game night of about an hour. Keep a scoreboard across the rounds and crown the overall winner at the end.

Are the challenges safe for children?

Yes, with a little care. Clear the playing area, use soft items for throwing games, and give younger players a simpler version or an older partner to help.

Can teams play against each other?

Yes. The Team group is made for that, and any challenge can be played as a relay: split into teams, take turns, and add the scores together.
