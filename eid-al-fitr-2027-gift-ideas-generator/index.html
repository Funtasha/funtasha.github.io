How the gift idea generator works

Choose who the gift is for, choose how generous you want to be, and press New idea. The generator suggests a gift that fits both choices, and tells you why it fits. Press the button again for another idea from the same group, as many times as you like.

Gifts for parents and grandparents

Parents and grandparents are often the hardest people to buy for, because they say they want nothing. The truth is that they want thought, not things: a letter, a photo, a day spent together. The ideas for parents and grandparents on this page lean that way.

A framed family photo, a handwritten letter, or a day out together will please most parents more than anything from a shop. If you do buy something, choose comfort: a warm shawl, a soft blanket, or something that makes an ordinary evening a little nicer.

Gifts for siblings and friends

Siblings and friends are the easiest people to gift, because you know what makes them laugh. A funny card, a favourite chocolate, or a shared playlist all say that you pay attention, and that is what a gift is for.

For a bigger gesture, give an experience you can share: a meal together, a day out, or a project you can do side by side. The gift becomes a memory, and the memory lasts longer than anything wrapped in paper.

Gifts for children

Children are delighted by simple things. A colouring book, a ball, or a small puzzle will keep a young child happy for hours, and a storybook read together becomes a memory as well as a gift. You do not need to spend much to make a child's Eid special.

For older children, a day out planned around what they love beats another toy. Let them choose the day: a park, a museum, a picnic, or a cooking session at home. The day itself is the gift, and they will remember it long after the toys are forgotten.

Gifts for teachers and colleagues

Teachers and colleagues deserve thanks, and Eid is a good time to give it. A handwritten card, a box of sweets, or a small plant are all thoughtful without being too personal. The note matters more than the gift: say what they did, and why it mattered.

Keep workplace gifts modest and equal. If you give to one colleague, give something similar to others in the same group, so nobody feels left out. A gift that the whole desk can share, such as a box of sweets, is always a safe choice.

Small gifts that feel generous

A small gift feels generous when it shows thought. A handwritten letter, a box of homemade sweets, or a plant for a windowsill all cost little, but they say that you took time. Time is the one thing nobody can buy, which is why it means so much.

Homemade gifts are especially good at Eid. Sweets you cooked yourself, a card you wrote by hand, or a photo you printed and framed all carry a flavour that no shop can match. If you can make it, make it: the effort is the gift.

The best gift is time

Ask most people what they want for Eid, and the honest answer is time: time with family, time with friends, time to rest. A day out together, a long lunch, or a quiet afternoon of conversation are gifts that no shop sells, and they are often the ones people remember.

If you are short of money this Eid, give time instead. Offer to cook, to babysit, to help with a job, or simply to sit and talk. A gift of time is never a small gift, and it is never forgotten.

When you are stuck for an idea

Everyone gets stuck on one person each year: the relative who has everything, the friend who is hard to read, the colleague you barely know. When that happens, stop thinking about things and start thinking about the person. What do they enjoy? What do they complain about? What did they mention, just once, months ago?

The generator can help here too. Press New idea a few times and notice which suggestions make you think, that could work. The right idea often arrives as a small feeling rather than a flash of inspiration, and the generator's job is to give that feeling something to catch on.

Wrapping and giving with grace

How you give a gift matters as much as what you give. Wrap it with care, even if the paper is plain, and write the person's name on it by hand. Hand it over with a smile and a few kind words, and let the person open it in their own time.

If someone gives you a gift, accept it graciously, whatever it is. Thank them warmly, and mean it. Eid is a festival of generosity, and generosity runs in both directions: giving gladly, and receiving gladly too.

Budgets here are relative words, not amounts: small, medium and generous mean different things to different families. Give what you can afford, and never stretch yourself for a gift.

Questions people ask

How many gift ideas are there?

There are forty-eight ideas in the generator: two for every combination of eight recipients and three budgets. Press New idea to see another one from the same group.

What do small, medium and generous mean?

They are relative words, not amounts. A small gift is a small gesture, a generous gift is a bigger one, and what those words mean depends on your family and your means. The generator never states a price.

Can I use an idea for someone else?

Yes. The groups are a starting point, not a rule. If an idea for a friend fits your sibling better, use it for your sibling. The best gift is the one that fits the person.

What if the person has everything?

Give time instead of things: a day out, a meal together, or an offer of help. The generous ideas in the generator include several gifts of time, and they are often the ones people remember longest.

Is it all right to give money at Eid?

Yes. Giving money to children at Eid, often called Eidi, is a much-loved tradition in many families. This generator focuses on gift ideas, but a gift of money, given with love, is a fine Eid gift too.

Does the generator work on a phone?

Yes. The generator runs entirely in your browser with no sign-up and no download, and it works on phones, tablets, and computers.
