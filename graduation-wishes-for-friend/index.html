These wishes are starting points, not finished cards. A line lands best when it carries a detail only you two share — a shared lecture, an inside joke, or the all-nighter you survived together.

How to make it your own

A wish becomes yours the moment you add something only you could write. Try one of these before you send it:

  • Name a memory only you two share: a lecture, a library all-nighter, a celebration that went wrong.
  • Mention their degree or their plans, so the wish fits their story and no one else's.
  • Add one line in your own voice — a friend's joke lands better than any polished sentence.

Questions people ask

What should I write in a graduation card for a friend?

Start with a wish from the sweet section, then add one detail only you two share — a lecture, a library all-nighter, or a hope for what comes next. Two or three heartfelt sentences are plenty.

How long should a graduation message be?

Two or three sentences is enough for a card, and one sentence is enough for a text. A short message that is personal beats a long one that could be for anyone.

Can I send these wishes by text message?

Yes. The short wishes are written to fit a text, and the copy button copies the whole wish so you can paste it straight into your message.

What if I want something funny instead of sweet?

Use the funny section — every line is kind and good-natured. If you are writing to someone who loves a joke, pair one funny wish with one sweet line.

How do I make a graduation wish more personal?

Add a shared memory, name their subject or their plans, and sign it the way you always do. The wish gives you the words; the personal detail makes them yours.
