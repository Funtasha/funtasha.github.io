You never asked for thanks, but you deserve a lifetime of it. Happy Mother's Day to the woman who gave me the world and asked for nothing in return.

From lullabies to life advice, you have been there for every chapter of my story. I love you more than words, today and every day.

These wishes are starting points, not finished cards. A line lands best when it carries a detail only you know — a childhood memory, something she taught you, or the meal only she can make.

How to make it your own

A wish becomes yours the moment you add something only you could write. Try one of these before you send it:

  • Name a memory: a childhood meal, a school event she never missed, or a holiday you still talk about.
  • Mention something she taught you — a recipe, a value, or the way she always knew when you needed a hug.
  • Sign it the way you always do — a nickname or a family phrase beats a formal ending.

Questions people ask

What should I write in a Mother's Day card for my mom?

Start with a wish from the sweet section, then add one detail only you know — a favourite memory, something she taught you, or a thank-you for a specific kindness. Two or three heartfelt sentences are plenty.

How long should a Mother's Day message be?

Two or three sentences is enough for a card, and one sentence is enough for a text. A short message that is personal beats a long one that could be for anyone.

Can I send these wishes by text message?

Yes. The short wishes are written to fit a text, and the copy button copies the whole wish so you can paste it straight into your message.

What if I want something funny instead of sweet?

Use the funny section — every line is kind and good-natured. If your mom loves a joke, pair one funny wish with one sweet line.

How do I make a Mother's Day wish more personal?

Add a shared memory, name something she does that you love, and sign it the way you always do. The wish gives you the words; the personal detail makes them yours.
