Easter Egg Hunt Game Generator

By the Funtasha team · Published 19 November 2026 · Updated 19 November 2026

Pick the age of your hunters, how many eggs to hide, and whether the hunt is indoors or outdoors, and the generator builds a printable list of clues and hiding spots. Every list is different, so no two hunts play the same way twice.

Choose your hunters and press the button.

    How the hunt generator works

    Tell the generator who is hunting, how many eggs you want to hide, and whether the hunt is indoors or outdoors. It then builds a list of clues matched to the age group, each paired with a hiding spot that suits the place you chose. Press Make my hunt list as many times as you like — every list is drawn at random, so each one is different.

    When you are happy with the list, press Print the list to print it out or save it. The printed list is your organiser's sheet: it tells you where every egg goes, so you can hide them all before the hunters arrive. Keep it in your pocket during the hunt, and you will always know which eggs are still out there.

    Choosing the right age group

    The age group changes the clues, not just the hiding spots. Little ones aged 2 to 4 get short, simple clues that a grown-up reads aloud, pointing them towards something they can see. Kids aged 5 to 8 get simple rhymes and riddles they can puzzle out with a little help. Older kids aged 9 to 12 get trickier clues that make them think, and teens and adults get wordplay that rewards a clever reader.

    If your hunters span two age groups, choose the younger one — a hunt that is too easy is still fun, but a hunt that is too hard ends in frustration. You can also run two hunts side by side: a short, simple one for the little ones and a longer, harder one for the older children, each with its own set of eggs.

    Clue ideas for little hunters

    Little hunters need clues they can follow with their eyes and feet, not their reading. The best clues for ages 2 to 4 name something familiar — a chair, a shoe, a flowerpot — and hide the egg somewhere they can reach without climbing. Read each clue aloud, walk with them, and let them find the egg themselves, even if it takes a while.

    Keep the hunt short for this age: four to six eggs is plenty, and every egg should be visible from a normal standing position once you are near the spot. Hide the eggs while the little ones are in another room, and resist the urge to make the last egg hard — the joy is in the finding, and a hunt that ends in tears is not a hunt worth repeating.

    Clues that make older kids think

    Children aged 9 to 12 enjoy a clue that makes them stop and think. Riddles work well at this age: a clue that describes a place without naming it, a word that has two meanings, or a rhyme that only makes sense when you say it aloud. The generator's clues for this group are written to be solvable but not obvious — the kind of clue that earns a grin when the answer clicks.

    For teens and adults, raise the game further with wordplay and misdirection. A clue that points to the wrong room first, or a hiding spot that is right in front of them, keeps even the cleverest hunters honest. You can also turn the hunt into a race: split into teams, hand each team the same list, and see who finds all their eggs first.

    Hiding spots indoors and outdoors

    Indoor hunts reward creativity: behind the sofa cushions, inside a shoe, on top of a bookshelf, under a pillow. The best indoor spots are the ones hunters walk past without noticing, at heights they can reach safely. Avoid anywhere hot, sharp or fragile, and keep eggs away from anything that stains.

    Outdoor hunts have more room but more weather. Under a flowerpot, behind the garden gate, at the foot of the biggest tree, inside the wheelbarrow — the garden is full of hiding places that feel like an adventure. If it has rained, choose spots that stay dry, and keep chocolate eggs out of direct sun. And always count the eggs before and after: an egg left in the garden is a mess waiting to happen.

    Keeping the hunt fair and fun

    A fair hunt is a fun hunt. Give every hunter the same number of eggs to find, or the same colour to look for, so that the fastest child does not scoop up everything while the slowest finds nothing. A simple rule — one egg per hiding spot, and no grabbing from another hunter's basket — prevents most arguments before they start.

    For mixed ages, try a head start: let the little ones begin two minutes early, or give them easier clues. For a big group, split the garden or the house into zones and assign one zone to each age group. And have a spare egg or two in your pocket for the hunter who finds the fewest — a little kindness at the end costs nothing and is remembered for years.

    After the hunt: counting, sharing and tidying up

    When the hunting is over, gather everyone together to count the eggs. Counting is part of the fun, and it is also how you check that nothing has been left behind. Compare baskets, trade duplicates, and let the children tell the story of their hardest find — the retelling is often better than the hunt itself.

    Then tidy up together. Collect any empty wrappers, check the hiding spots against your printed list, and make sure no egg is still sitting behind a cushion or under a bush. If you used the printed list, keep it: next year you can run the generator again for a fresh set of clues, and compare the two hunts to see which clues the family still talks about.

    Questions people ask

    How many eggs should I hide?

    Four to six eggs are plenty for little ones aged 2 to 4. Older children enjoy eight to twelve. The generator lets you choose any number from 4 to 12, and builds a list to match.

    Can I run a hunt for mixed ages?

    Yes. Choose the younger age group so nobody is left out, or run two hunts side by side with a different list for each group. Giving little ones a two-minute head start also keeps things fair.

    What if it rains on the day?

    Switch the hunt indoors. The generator has an indoor setting with hiding spots for every room in the house, and an indoor hunt can be just as exciting as one in the garden.

    How do I print the hunt list?

    Press Print the list after you have made your hunt. The list prints from your browser, so you can print it on paper or save it as a file. Keep it with you while you hide the eggs.

    Are the clues different every time?

    Yes. Each list is drawn at random from the clue and hiding-spot banks for the age group and place you chose, so pressing the button again gives you a fresh hunt.

    What should I do with the eggs after the hunt?

    Count them together to check none are missing, then let the hunters trade and share. Collect the wrappers, check every hiding spot against your list, and keep any spare eggs for later.
