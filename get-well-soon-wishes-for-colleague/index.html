Get Well Soon Wishes for a Colleague

Here are 28 original get-well wishes for a colleague, grouped warm, funny and short so you can match the message to your working relationship. Tap Copy on any wish to send it in a message, a card or a team note.

When a colleague is unwell, the right message strikes a friendly balance: warm enough to show you care, light enough to respect their privacy. Choose a wish that fits how well you know them, keep work talk out of it, and let them know the team has everything covered while they rest.

Warm wishes

  • I heard you are unwell, and I wanted to let you know the whole team is thinking of you. Rest properly, and do not worry about a thing at work, because we have it covered.
  • Take all the time you need to recover. Your desk, your coffee mug and your spot at the lunch table will all be waiting whenever you are ready to return.
  • Wishing you a smooth and comfortable recovery. You are missed around the office, and we look forward to having your calm advice and good humour back soon.
  • Rest well, and please do not think about deadlines or inboxes. Your only job right now is to get better, and the team is happy to hold the fort.
  • You are a valued colleague and a kind person to work with, and I hope each day brings you a little more energy and comfort. We are all rooting for you.
  • Sending you warm wishes for a speedy recovery. If there is anything I can help with while you are away, from forwarding a message to watering your desk plant, just say the word.
  • I hope you are resting properly and not sneaking a look at your emails. The work will wait, and your health comes first. Feel better soon.
  • Take it easy and heal well. Your steady presence is missed in our meetings, and we cannot wait to welcome you back when you feel strong again.
  • Wishing you plenty of rest and a gentle recovery. You have always been generous with your time and your help, and now it is our turn to look after things for you.
  • Rest up, and know that your colleagues are thinking of you. We have saved you a seat, a fresh notebook and a very warm welcome for your first day back.

Funny wishes

  • Get well soon. The office printer has already jammed twice without you, the meeting room is suspiciously quiet and nobody knows the Wi-Fi password.
  • I hear rest is the best medicine, which is lucky because you are the only person I know who can nap through a fire drill. Keep up the excellent work.
  • Being ill is the only acceptable excuse for missing the team lunch, and I am still counting the days. Hurry back, because your lunch order is legendary.
  • Get better soon. Your inbox is behaving itself, your chair is gathering dust and the coffee machine is asking where its favourite customer has gone.
  • You are allowed one more day of daytime television and blanket forts, and then I expect you back, armed with snacks and strong opinions about the new project.
  • Rest well. I would bring you soup, but the office kitchen is a mystery to me, so please accept this message and a promise of a proper coffee when you return.
  • Get well soon. The team has voted unanimously: you are banned from checking emails, joining calls and thinking about spreadsheets until further notice.
  • I have hidden your laptop charger so you actually rest, and I have hidden the good biscuits so you have to ask me for them. Feel better soon.
  • You are the only person I know who apologises for being ill. Stop that at once, put your feet up and let the team do the worrying for a while.

Short wishes

  • Feel better soon. The team is thinking of you and missing you.
  • Rest up and recover well. We have everything covered at work.
  • Get well soon. Your desk and your colleagues are waiting for you.
  • Sending you warm wishes for a speedy recovery. Take care.
  • Take it easy and heal well. We look forward to your return.
  • Wishing you a gentle recovery and many restful days ahead.
  • Get well soon. The office is quieter without you in it.
  • Rest, recover and come back soon. We miss you already.
  • Feel better soon. Better days and brighter mornings are coming.
These wishes are friendly encouragement only. They are not health advice, and they never replace a doctor, a nurse or any care your colleague is already receiving. If you are worried about them, encourage them to speak to a health professional.

How to make it personal

A message to a colleague feels genuine when it carries one small detail from your working life together. A few small touches make any wish on this page sound like it came from you.

  • Mention one shared routine, like a coffee break, a weekly meeting or a project you worked on, so the message feels personal without being too private.
  • Offer one concrete kind of help, such as forwarding an urgent message or covering a small task, instead of a vague offer.
  • Match the tone to your relationship: warm and simple for someone you know well, polite and brief for someone newer.
  • Keep work talk out of the message, and say clearly that no reply is needed.
  • If the whole team signs a card, keep each message short so the card stays easy to read.

Questions people ask

What should I write to a colleague who is unwell?

Keep it warm, brief and professional: say you are thinking of them, wish them a good rest and reassure them that work is covered. Avoid asking about their illness or when they will return.

Is it okay to send a funny get-well message to a colleague?

Yes, if you know them well and the illness is mild. Gentle jokes about rest and the office usually land well. For a serious illness or a newer colleague, choose warm and simple words.

Should I mention work in a get-well message?

Only to reassure them that it is handled. Never ask about deadlines, hand over tasks or hint that they should check their inbox. Their only job right now is to recover.

How often should I check on an unwell colleague?

One thoughtful message is enough to start with. A brief check-in after a few days is kind, but let them set the pace and never make them feel they owe you a reply.

What if I do not know my colleague very well?

A short, polite wish is perfect. A simple message wishing them a speedy recovery shows kindness without overstepping, and it will be appreciated.
