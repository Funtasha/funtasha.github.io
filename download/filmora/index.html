What is Filmora, and who makes it?

Filmora is a video editor for Android made by Wondershare, a company that has made video software for many years. The phone version brings the friendly style of the desktop Filmora to a smaller screen: you can cut clips, add effects and music, and shape a polished video without needing editing experience. It is built for social videos — the kind you post on short-video apps, share with friends, or use to show off a product.

Filmora suits people who want their videos to look finished without spending hours on them. Templates and one-tap tools handle the fiddly parts, while the full timeline is there when you want more control. You can start with a quick template video today and learn the deeper tools as you go, all inside the same app.

Filmora has been around for many years, and its phone app benefits from that experience. The tools are arranged in a way that makes sense: the everyday jobs sit on the main screen, and the deeper tools wait one tap away. Help and tips appear inside the app as you work, so you can learn by doing rather than by reading a manual. If you have edited on a computer before, the phone version will feel familiar.

Templates and one-tap video

Templates are Filmora's fastest route to a finished video. Each template sets the timing, the transitions, the music and the text style for you. All you do is add your own photos or clips and change the words. In a few minutes you can turn a set of holiday photos into a lively slideshow, or a handful of clips into a birthday video that looks carefully planned.

One-tap tools go a step further: some can pick your best moments, match them to the beat of a song, and build a video almost by themselves. These tools are a great starting point, and you can still change anything afterwards. Use a template when you are short on time, then adjust the clips, the order and the music until the video feels like yours rather than a ready-made one.

Effects, transitions and stickers

Effects change how a clip looks or feels. A glow effect can make a night scene sparkle, a vintage effect can make new footage feel old, and a speed effect can make a dull moment lively. Filmora includes a large set of effects, and you can preview each one on your own clip before you commit to it. The trick is restraint: one or two effects that fit the mood beat a dozen fighting for attention.

Transitions are the moments between clips. A simple cut is often best, but a soft fade or a slide can signal a change of scene or mood. Stickers add fun: animated shapes, emojis and labels you can drop onto a frame. Use them sparingly and they add charm; use too many and they hide the video underneath. As a rule, if an effect does not help the story, leave it out.

Music and audio tools

Music sets the mood of a video, and Filmora includes a library of tracks you can add to your project. Pick a song that matches the feeling of your clips — upbeat for a celebration, calm for a travel diary — and let it run under your video. You can trim the track to fit, fade it in and out, and set its volume so it supports the pictures rather than drowning them.

Filmora also gives you simple audio tools for the sound already in your clips. You can raise the volume of a quiet clip, lower background noise, or record a voiceover over your video. If you speak in your video, keep the music quiet while you talk, and check the mix with headphones before you export. Good sound is often what separates a video that feels polished from one that feels rough.

Text and titles

Text helps viewers follow your video. A title at the start tells people what they are about to watch, captions can explain each scene, and a closing line can say where to find you. Filmora offers many text styles, from clean and simple to bold and animated, and you can change the colour, size and position of each line.

Keep your words short and easy to read. A title of three to five words works well, and captions should be one line at a time. Make sure the text stays on screen long enough to read, and check that it does not cover the most important part of the picture. Light text on a dark clip, or dark text on a bright clip, reads best — and a plain style usually ages better than a flashy one.

AI tools in plain words

Filmora includes tools that use artificial intelligence, which simply means the app can do clever jobs by itself. One of the most useful is automatic captions: the app listens to the speech in your video and writes the words on screen for you. This helps viewers who watch with the sound off, and it saves you from typing every word by hand.

Other AI tools can pick out the subject of a photo and remove the background, tidy up a shaky clip, or suggest the best moments from a long recording. These tools are helpers, not magic: check their work before you export, and fix anything that looks wrong. Used well, they save time on the boring jobs and leave you free to focus on the creative ones.

Another handy AI helper can tidy up the sound in a clip, lowering background noise so speech is clearer. A different one can pick a short highlight from a long recording, which is useful when you filmed for an hour and only need the best minute. None of these tools replaces your judgement: watch what the app produces, keep what works, and change what does not.

Exporting and quality settings

Exporting turns your project into a single video file you can share. Filmora lets you choose the resolution and frame rate. For most social apps, 1080p at 30 frames per second is a good choice: it looks sharp on a phone and keeps the file size manageable. Higher settings look better on a large screen but create much bigger files and take longer to export.

Before you export, watch your video once through in the preview. Check the cuts, the text, the music level and the ending, because fixing a mistake now is quicker than exporting twice. Also check that you have enough free space on your phone for the finished file. Once you are happy, export and share the video straight to the app where your audience will see it.

Tips for a polished video on a phone

A polished video is mostly about good habits, not fancy tools. Shoot in good light, hold the phone steady, and keep each clip short. Then edit with a clear idea of what the video is about, and cut anything that does not serve that idea. The result will look better than a long video full of effects.

  • Shoot in daylight or bright indoor light for cleaner footage
  • Hold each shot steady for a few seconds before you move on
  • Cut the weak parts first, then add music and effects
  • Use one text style and one filter across the whole video
  • Watch the finished video once before you share it

Questions people ask

Is Filmora free?

Yes. Filmora is free to download and the main editing tools cost nothing. Some extra effects, templates and AI tools are paid options.

How do I install the Filmora APK?

Tap the Download button above to open the official Play Store page, then tap Install. If you use a file from the APKPure archive instead, open the downloaded file and allow your phone to install it when asked, then follow the on-screen steps.

How do I use a template in Filmora?

Open Filmora, browse the templates and pick one you like, then add your own photos or clips and change the text. Adjust the music if you want, then export the video.

Does Filmora add a watermark?

Yes, videos exported with the free version of Filmora carry a small Filmora watermark. The paid plan removes the watermark and adds extra effects and templates.

What Android version does Filmora need?

Filmora needs Android 7.0 or newer. You can check your version in Settings, under About phone.
