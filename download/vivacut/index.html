What VivaCut is

VivaCut is a video editor for your phone that aims higher than the basics. It is made by AIPropulse International Limited, and it is built for people who want more control over their edits: multiple layers of video and image, keyframe animation, chroma key for green-screen work, and a deep library of effects and transitions. If you have outgrown a simple editor and want tools that feel closer to a desktop app, VivaCut is a strong next step.

The app is free to download and use, with an optional paid plan that adds extra features and removes limits. The free version is a real editor, not a demo — you can cut, layer, add effects and export finished videos without paying. The paid plan is there for people who use the full tool set every day and want every feature.

VivaCut suits short-form video especially well — the clips people post on TikTok, Instagram and YouTube Shorts — but it handles longer projects too. Its layer system means you can stack video over video, put text and images on top, and blend them together, which is how the polished videos you see online are actually made.

Effects and transitions

Effects are where VivaCut starts to show its depth. Beyond the usual filters, it has glitch effects, light effects, blur and distortion, and effects that respond to the beat of your music. You can apply an effect to a whole clip or to just part of it, and you can adjust how strong it is. The trick is restraint: one well-chosen effect on the right moment looks far better than five effects fighting each other.

Transitions are the bridges between your clips, and VivaCut has a wide range — fades, slides, zooms, spins and glitch cuts. A transition should feel like part of the video, not a decoration pasted on top. Match the transition to the mood: a soft fade for something gentle, a sharp glitch cut for something energetic. And use the same family of transitions through a video so it feels like one piece of work.

One feature worth learning is the way VivaCut lets you combine an effect with a transition on the same cut. A zoom transition plus a light flash, for example, can make a scene change feel dramatic. Try combinations on a test project first, and keep the ones that feel natural — your viewers should notice the video, not the tricks.

Multi-layer editing and chroma key

Layers are the heart of VivaCut. A simple editor gives you one timeline: clips in a row. VivaCut lets you stack clips on top of each other, so a second video can play over the first, an image can sit in a corner, or text can float above everything. This is how picture-in-picture works, how reaction videos are made, and how you put a logo or a caption over your footage.

Chroma key — the green-screen effect — is the feature that opens the most creative doors. Film yourself in front of a plain green background, then use chroma key to make the green transparent and drop in any background you like: a city, a beach, a cartoon world. It works best with even lighting and a background that is one solid colour, and VivaCut lets you fine-tune how much green is removed so the edges of your subject stay clean.

Layers and chroma key together are how you make videos that look like they were shot in a studio. You can stand in front of a scene you never visited, appear twice in the same shot, or float a product shot over a moving background. It takes a little practice, but the tools are all in the app, and the results are worth the learning curve.

Video with music and text

Music changes everything about a video, and VivaCut makes it easy to add. You can import a song from your phone, trim it to fit your video, and set its volume so it sits under your voice instead of drowning it out. The beat markers help you cut your clips to the rhythm of the music, which is the single easiest way to make a video feel professional.

Text in VivaCut goes beyond simple captions. You can choose from many fonts, add outlines and shadows, animate the text so it slides or pops in, and place it on its own layer so it moves with the video. Text is useful for titles, for jokes, and for the words people need to read when they watch with the sound off.

Voiceover is the third audio layer, and it is simple to record: tap the microphone, speak while your video plays, and VivaCut drops the recording onto the timeline. It is perfect for explaining a tutorial, narrating a travel video, or adding a personal message to a birthday montage. Record in a quiet room, close to the phone, for the cleanest sound.

Templates for a fast start

When you want a good-looking video quickly, VivaCut's templates do the heavy lifting. A template is a ready-made edit with the cuts, effects, transitions and music already chosen. You add your own photos or videos, and the template arranges them into a finished piece. It is the fastest route from camera roll to shareable video.

Templates are also a way to learn. When you find one you like, look closely at how it is built: where the cuts fall, which transitions it uses, how the music lines up with the pictures. Those are the choices an experienced editor makes, and studying templates teaches you to make them yourself. Many VivaCut users start with templates and graduate to building edits from scratch.

Pick a template that fits your footage. A fast, glitchy template suits energetic clips; a slow, gentle template suits a birthday or a holiday film. If a template does not fit your clips, try another — the same photos can look completely different in a different template, so it is worth browsing before you settle.

Exporting and quality

When your edit is ready, exporting turns it into a video file you can share. VivaCut offers a range of resolutions, and for most sharing — messaging apps and social media — 1080p is the right choice: sharp on a phone screen, and a file size that uploads quickly. Higher resolutions are worth it if you filmed in high quality and want the best possible result, but they make larger files and longer exports.

Frame rate affects smoothness. Thirty frames per second is natural for everyday video. Sixty frames per second looks smoother and suits fast action — sport, dance, anything that moves quickly. If you filmed in slow motion, a higher frame rate keeps the slow parts looking clean instead of choppy.

Exporting a long or layered video takes time, and your phone may warm up — that is normal, because rendering is hard work. Keep VivaCut open while it exports and let it finish. When it is done, the video appears in your gallery, ready to share wherever you like.

Smooth playback on older phones

VivaCut's layers and effects ask more of a phone than a simple editor does, so on an older phone it helps to work smart. The biggest win is closing other apps before you open VivaCut — every app running in the background takes memory that your edit could use. If your phone feels slow while editing, this one change often fixes it.

Working with shorter clips also helps. Instead of importing a ten-minute video and trimming it down, trim the footage in your gallery first, or split the project into short scenes and edit them one at a time. Smaller pieces are easier for an older phone to preview smoothly, and they are easier for you to manage too.

If previews stutter, lower the preview quality while you edit — the exported video is not affected, because the export renders at the quality you choose. And free some storage before a big export: a phone that is nearly full cannot render properly. A little space, a closed background app, and short clips will keep VivaCut running well on a phone that is no longer new.

Questions people ask

Is VivaCut free?

Yes, VivaCut is free to download and use, and the core editing tools cost nothing. There is an optional paid plan that adds extra features and removes limits, but you can edit and export videos without it.

How do I install the VivaCut APK?

Tap the Download button above — it opens the official Play Store listing for VivaCut. Tap Install there and the app installs itself. For an older version, the button on its row opens the APKPure version page, where you can download the file and open it to install it.

How do I use chroma key in VivaCut?

Film yourself in front of a plain green background with even lighting, then add the clip to your timeline and open the chroma key tool. Pick the green colour to remove it, then place any video or image on the layer underneath as your new background. Fine-tune the strength until the edges of your subject look clean.

Why is my export slow?

Exports slow down when a video has many layers, effects, or a high resolution, because the phone has to render every frame. Close other apps, free some storage, and try a lower resolution for a faster export — 1080p is plenty for most sharing.

What Android version does VivaCut need?

VivaCut needs Android 7.0 or newer. You can check your Android version in Settings, under About phone.
