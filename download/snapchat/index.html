What is Snapchat?

Snapchat is a messaging app for Android built around photos and short videos called snaps. You send a snap to a friend, and it disappears after they view it, which makes chatting feel quick and light. Snapchat also has stories, chats, video calls and playful camera lenses that add effects to your face and surroundings.

Snapchat is most popular with younger users and friend groups who share everyday moments rather than polished posts. Because snaps disappear, people share more freely: a funny face, a meal, a street scene, a quick thought. It is a casual app, made for the moment rather than for keeping.

Snaps and stories

A snap is a photo or short video you send to one friend or a few friends. When they open it, they can view it once or twice, and then it disappears. You can add text, drawings, stickers and filters to a snap before you send it, which is half the fun. If someone takes a screenshot of your snap, Snapchat tells you.

A story is a snap you share with all your friends at once, and it stays visible for 24 hours before it disappears. You can post several snaps to your story through the day, and friends can watch them whenever they like. Public stories from creators and publishers appear in the Discover section, where you can follow shows, news and people you enjoy.

Spotlight is Snapchat's feed of short videos from people all over the world, shown one after another as you scroll. It works like the short video feeds on other apps: watch, like, and follow creators you enjoy. You can submit your own short videos to Spotlight too, and a video that people like can reach a very wide audience.

Chat and lenses

Chat in Snapchat works like other messaging apps, with one difference: messages disappear after they are read, unless you save them by pressing and holding. You can send text, photos, voice notes and video notes, and you can make voice and video calls to friends. Group chats work the same way, with disappearing messages for the whole group.

Lenses are camera effects that change how you look or what is around you. Point the camera at your face and lenses can add animal ears, change your voice, or turn you into a cartoon. Some lenses work on the rear camera too, adding objects and animations to the world around you. New lenses appear all the time, and you can search for lenses made by other people.

Bitmoji is your personal cartoon avatar on Snapchat, made to look like you. You design it once — face, hair, clothes — and it appears in chats, on the Snap Map, and in stickers you can send to friends. It is a small thing, but it makes Snapchat feel personal, and friends recognise your Bitmoji at a glance.

Snap Map and memories

The Snap Map shows where your friends are on a map, if they have chosen to share their location. You can see friends nearby, explore snaps posted from places around the world, and find out what is happening in a city you plan to visit. If you would rather not share your location, turn on Ghost Mode in the map settings, and nobody can see where you are.

Memories is where Snapchat keeps the snaps you choose to save, instead of letting them disappear. Swipe up from the camera screen to open Memories, where your saved snaps are stored by date, along with photos from your camera roll that you import. You can look back at old memories, share them again, or make a story from them. Anything you do not save is gone once it disappears, so save the moments that matter.

  • Turn on Ghost Mode if you do not want to share your location
  • Save important snaps to Memories before they disappear
  • Use Memories to look back at birthdays, trips and celebrations
  • Share an old memory to your story to relive a moment
  • Keep your My Eyes Only section locked with a passcode for private saves

Setting up Snapchat step by step

Setting up Snapchat is quick. Install the app from the official Play Store page, open it, and tap Sign Up to create an account. Enter your name, pick a username, set a password, and add your phone number or an email address to confirm your account.

  • Install Snapchat from the official Play Store page and open it
  • Tap Sign Up and enter your name and birthday
  • Pick a username and a strong password
  • Add your phone number or email address to confirm your account
  • Add friends by searching for their username or scanning their Snapcode

Your username is how friends find you, so pick one you are happy to share. Snapchat will ask for access to your camera, microphone and contacts — the camera and microphone are needed for snaps and calls, while contacts help you find friends. You can change these permissions later in your phone settings.

Privacy settings

Snapchat gives you control over who can contact you and see your story. In Settings, under Privacy Controls, you can choose who can send you snaps and messages: everyone, or only your friends. Setting it to friends only is the safer choice, and it stops strangers from messaging you.

You can also choose who can see your story and who can see your location on the Snap Map. Ghost Mode hides your location from everyone, which is worth turning on if you do not want friends to see where you are. If someone bothers you, block them from their profile, and report them if they break the rules. Remember that snaps can be screenshotted, so never send anything you would not want shared.

Snapchat on an old phone

Snapchat needs Android 6.0 or newer. On an old phone, the camera and lenses are the parts that struggle most, since they need a decent processor and enough memory. If the camera is slow to open or lenses stutter, try closing other apps first, and make sure your phone has free storage.

If the latest version will not install on your phone, the Play Store will offer the newest version that fits your phone instead. Keep the app updated when you can, since newer versions often run better on older phones. If Snapchat still feels slow, use the chat and story parts, which are lighter than the camera lenses.

Tips for saving data

Snapchat uses a lot of data because snaps, stories and videos load as you browse. Travel Mode helps: open Settings, go to Manage, and turn on Travel Mode, which stops snaps and stories from loading until you tap them. That is the single biggest data saving in the app.

  • Turn on Travel Mode so stories and snaps load only when you tap them
  • Watch stories on Wi-Fi when you can
  • Avoid sending long videos on mobile data
  • Clear the cache in Settings, under Account Actions, to free space
  • Turn off background data for Snapchat in your phone settings if your plan is small

Video calls and long stories use the most data, so save them for Wi-Fi. If your data is running low, stick to text chat, which uses almost nothing, and catch up on stories when you are home.
