Why lite?

Opera Mini is a browser built for small phones and slow connections. At 44.7 MB it is a light install, and it saves data on every page you open: pages are compressed on Opera's servers before they reach your phone, so they load faster and use fewer megabytes. On a slow connection, that compression is the difference between a page that loads and a page that gives up.

Compared with a full desktop-style browser, Mini trims the weight: it keeps browsing, tabs, downloads, bookmarks and video, and leaves out the heaviest extras. It also blocks ads built in, which saves data twice — the ad itself never downloads.

The latest Opera Mini needs Android 9.0 or newer, but older builds run on older phones: the builds in the list above from 2025 and early 2026 need only Android 6.0, and older builds still run on Android 5.0. If your phone cannot run the latest version, an older build still browses the same web.

What is Opera Mini?

Opera Mini is a lightweight web browser from Opera, made for phones with little storage, little memory, or a slow connection. It has been the small browser for a long time — Opera built it for the days when phones were slow and data was expensive, and it still does that job today. It is a 44.7 MB download that opens fast and loads pages fast.

It is a real browser, not a toy. It opens any website, keeps tabs and bookmarks, downloads files, plays video, and blocks ads built in. The difference from a full desktop-style browser is focus: Mini is built to get the page in front of you quickly, using as little data as possible.

Opera Mini is at its best on an old phone or a slow connection. If pages take forever to load in your current browser, or your data runs out before the month does, Mini is the browser made for that problem.

Data saving and how it works

Opera Mini saves data by compressing pages before they reach your phone. When you open a website, the page first passes through Opera's servers, where it is squeezed down — images made smaller, extra code stripped out — and then sent to your phone in a lighter form. Your phone downloads less, so the page loads faster and uses fewer megabytes.

You can choose how much compression you want. The data-saving modes range from light to strong: the strongest mode compresses the most and saves the most data, while the lighter modes keep pages looking closer to the original. Pick the strongest mode when data is tight, and a lighter one when you are on Wi-Fi and want pages at full quality.

Built-in ad blocking saves data too. Ads are some of the heaviest parts of a web page, and Mini blocks them before they download — so the page is lighter, loads faster, and uses less of your data plan. Between compression and ad blocking, Mini uses far less data than a browser that loads every page at full weight.

Browsing and video on a light browser

Browsing in Opera Mini feels like browsing anywhere else. You get tabs, so you can keep several pages open; bookmarks, so your favourite sites are one tap away; and a start page with shortcuts to the sites you visit most. The address bar searches and navigates in one place, and your history is there when you want to go back.

Video works too. Mini has a built-in video player, so videos on web pages play inside the browser without needing another app. On a slow connection, videos may take a moment to start — that is the connection, not the browser — and watching over Wi-Fi is always smoother than watching over mobile data.

Downloads are handled well. Mini can download files in the background and keep going even if you switch to another app, and it has a download manager so you can see what is downloading and what has finished. On a slow connection, background downloads mean you can start a file and come back to it later.

Watching YouTube in the browser on an old phone

If the YouTube app will not install or will not run on your old phone, the browser route still works — and Opera Mini is a good browser for it. Open youtube.com in Mini, sign in with your Google account, and you can watch videos, search, and see your subscriptions, all in the browser. No app needed.

Watching in the browser has real advantages on an old phone. The browser uses less storage than the app, it does not run in the background when you close it, and it works on any phone that can run a browser at all. The video quality adjusts to your connection, so a slow connection still plays — just at a lower quality.

A few tips make it smoother. Use Wi-Fi for longer videos when you can, and keep the number of open tabs small so the phone has memory free for the video. If a video stutters, let it load for a few seconds before pressing play. And if your phone struggles with video in the browser, the YouTube guide on this site covers the other ways to watch YouTube on an old phone.

Opera Mini vs Chrome on an old phone

Chrome is the browser most Android phones ship with, and on a new phone it is excellent. On an old phone, it is heavy: it uses more storage, more memory and more data than Opera Mini, and it can feel slow when the phone is already struggling. That is the gap Mini was built to fill.

The main difference is data. Chrome loads pages at full weight; Mini compresses them first. On a small data plan, that compression is the difference between a month of browsing and a week of it. Mini also blocks ads built in, which saves more data and makes pages load faster.

Chrome still has its strengths — it syncs with your Google account, and some websites are built with Chrome in mind. You do not have to choose one or the other: keep Chrome for the sites that need it, and use Mini for everyday browsing, news and video. On an old phone, Mini as the daily browser and Chrome as the backup is a combination that works well.

Tips for slow data

Opera Mini already saves data, but a few habits stretch a slow connection further:

  • Use the strongest data-saving mode when you are on mobile data, and a lighter mode on Wi-Fi.
  • Keep the number of open tabs small. Each tab uses memory, and a phone with free memory runs faster.
  • Save pages to read offline when you have Wi-Fi, so you can read them later without using data.
  • Watch video over Wi-Fi when you can — video uses more data than anything else you do in a browser.
  • Keep Opera Mini updated. New versions fix bugs and usually run better than the one before.

A slow connection does not have to mean a slow web. Opera Mini was built for exactly this problem.
