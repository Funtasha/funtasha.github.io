What is WhatsApp?

WhatsApp is a messaging app for Android that lets you send text messages, photos, videos and voice notes to anyone with a phone number, anywhere in the world. It is made by Meta, the same company behind Facebook and Instagram, and it is one of the most widely used messaging apps on the planet. Messages travel over the internet, so a chat with someone in another country costs no more than a chat with someone next door, apart from your normal data charges.

WhatsApp works on your phone, and you can also use it on a tablet or a computer, where your chats stay in step with your phone. Everything in a chat is protected by end-to-end encryption, which means only you and the person you are chatting with can read what is sent. For most people, WhatsApp is simply where daily life happens: family groups, school groups, work chats and quick calls.

You can also use WhatsApp on a computer through WhatsApp Web or the desktop app. Your phone stays the main device, and your chats appear on the computer screen so you can type with a real keyboard and send files from your computer. It is handy for long chats and for work, though your phone needs to stay nearby and switched on for the connection to work.

Chats, calls, groups and status

A chat in WhatsApp is a one-to-one conversation with a single contact. You can send text, photos, videos, documents, voice notes and your live location, and you can see when a message has been sent, delivered and read. Voice messages are popular because they are quick to send and easy to listen to, and you can speed them up while you listen if you are in a hurry.

Calls work the same way as chats. Voice calls and video calls are free over the internet, and group calls let several people talk at once. Groups bring many contacts into one shared chat, which is how families, classes, teams and neighbourhoods stay organised. Status is where you share a photo, video or short note that disappears after a day, so friends can see what you are up to without you sending it to each person one by one.

Setting up WhatsApp step by step

Setting up WhatsApp takes only a few minutes, and you need nothing more than your phone and your phone number. Install the app from the official Play Store page, then open it and follow the steps on the screen.

  • Tap Agree and Continue to accept the terms
  • Enter your phone number and tap Next, then wait for the confirmation code by text message
  • Enter the code to confirm your number
  • Type your name and, if you like, add a profile photo
  • Allow access to your contacts so WhatsApp can show who is already using the app

If the code does not arrive, wait a minute and ask for it again, or choose the call option so the code is read out to you by phone. Once your number is confirmed, your chats appear and you can start messaging straight away. Your phone number is your account, so keep your SIM card safe and do not share your confirmation code with anyone, ever.

Backup and restore

A backup is a copy of your chats saved somewhere safe, so you can get them back if you change phones or reinstall the app. On Android, WhatsApp backs up to Google Drive. Open WhatsApp, go to Settings, then Chats, then Chat backup, and turn on automatic backups. Choose how often you want it to happen, and whether videos are included, since videos make the backup much bigger.

To restore your chats, install WhatsApp on the new phone, sign in with the same phone number and the same Google account, and WhatsApp will offer to restore your backup during setup. Tap Restore and wait for it to finish before you start chatting. If you skip the restore step, you cannot go back to it later, so take your time and let it complete on Wi-Fi if your backup is large.

If both phones are nearby, WhatsApp can also move your chats directly from the old phone to the new one during setup, without using a backup at all. Keep both phones charged and on the same Wi-Fi network, and follow the steps on the screen. Either way, check that your chats have arrived before you wipe or give away the old phone.

Privacy settings

WhatsApp gives you simple controls over who sees what. In Settings, under Privacy, you can choose who sees your last seen time, your profile photo, your About text and your status updates: everyone, only your contacts, or nobody. You can also turn read receipts off, so people do not see the blue ticks, though read receipts stay on in group chats.

Disappearing messages make new messages in a chat vanish after a set time, which is handy for chats that do not need to be kept. Two-step verification adds a six-digit PIN that is asked for when your number is registered on a new phone, which stops someone else setting up WhatsApp with your number. If a stranger messages you, you can block and report them from the top of the chat, and you can leave any group at any time.

WhatsApp on an old phone

WhatsApp needs Android 6.0 or newer, so most phones from the last several years can run it. If your phone is older than that, the latest version will not install, and the Play Store will tell you the app is not compatible with your device. In that case, an earlier version from the old versions section above may still work, but remember that old versions can stop working if the developer ends support for them.

On a phone with little free space, WhatsApp can still feel slow, because chats, photos and videos pile up over time. Free space by deleting media you no longer need, clearing large chats, and turning off automatic media downloads. Restarting your phone now and then also helps. If the phone is very old, keep your expectations modest: chats and voice calls will work, but video calls may stutter on a weak connection.

Tips for saving data

WhatsApp is light on data for text, but photos, videos and calls add up. The biggest saving comes from controlling media downloads: open Settings, then Storage and data, and set photos, videos and documents to download only on Wi-Fi. That way nothing large downloads on mobile data unless you tap it yourself.

  • Turn on low data usage for calls in Storage and data settings
  • Download media only on Wi-Fi, and let videos wait until you are home
  • Send photos as documents only when you need full quality, since it uses more data
  • Keep backups on Wi-Fi only so a big backup never eats your mobile data
  • Check Storage and data to see which chats use the most space, and clear the ones you do not need

Voice calls use little data, but video calls use much more, so prefer voice when your data is limited. Status updates and videos in chats also use data as they load, so scroll past them rather than opening every one if you are watching your allowance.

Questions people ask

Is WhatsApp free?

Yes. WhatsApp is free to download and free to use for messages, voice calls and video calls. You only pay your normal internet or mobile data charges.

How do I install WhatsApp?

Tap the Download button above to open the official Play Store page, then tap Install. Open WhatsApp when it finishes, agree to the terms, and enter your phone number to set it up.

Will my chats stay if I reinstall?

Yes, if you back up first. Turn on backup in Settings, under Chats, then back up to Google Drive. When you reinstall and sign in with the same number, WhatsApp offers to restore your chats from the backup.

How do I back up WhatsApp?

Open WhatsApp, go to Settings, then Chats, then Chat backup. Tap Back Up to save your chats to Google Drive now, and turn on automatic backups so it happens on a schedule.

What Android version does WhatsApp need?

WhatsApp needs Android 6.0 or newer. You can check your version in Settings, under About phone.
