What is YouCut?

YouCut is a free video editor for Android made by InShot, the team behind the popular InShot editor. It is built for quick, everyday edits: trimming a clip, joining a few clips together, adding music and sharing the result. The app keeps its tools simple and its screens uncluttered, so you can finish an edit in minutes rather than hours.

YouCut is a good fit for short videos — birthday clips, holiday moments, product demos and social posts. It does not try to be a full studio, and that is its strength: everything you need for a clean, short video is easy to find, and nothing gets in your way. Videos export with no watermark, so your finished clip looks like yours from the first frame to the last.

YouCut keeps its file size small, so it downloads quickly and does not take up much room on your phone. It runs well on modest phones too, which matters if your device is a few years old. Because the app focuses on the basics, it opens fast, exports fast, and rarely gets in the way of a quick job. If you want a simple editor that does the everyday jobs well, YouCut is a solid choice.

Trimming, splitting and merging clips

Trimming is the most useful editing skill, and YouCut makes it easy. Open a clip, drag the handles at each end to keep only the part you want, and the rest is cut away. This is how you remove the shaky start of a clip, the long pause in the middle, or the moment someone walked in front of the camera. A few careful trims can turn a rambling clip into a tight, watchable video.

Splitting cuts one clip into two pieces at the point you choose, so you can remove a section from the middle or put something between the halves. Merging joins separate clips into one video in the order you pick. Together, these three tools cover most everyday editing: trim each clip to its best moments, split out the mistakes, then merge the good parts into a single video that flows.

Filters, effects and text

Filters change the look of your whole video with one tap. A warm filter makes a sunny day feel warmer, a cool filter suits a rainy scene, and a black-and-white filter gives an old-fashioned feel. YouCut includes a set of filters you can try on your clip and adjust until the look feels right. If a filter is too strong, you can lower its strength rather than removing it completely.

Effects and text add character. You can add animated stickers, draw on a frame, or put words on the screen to explain what is happening. Text works best when it is short: a title at the start, a name, or a single line that sets the scene. Keep the letters large enough to read on a phone, and leave each caption on screen long enough for a viewer to read it twice.

Music and voiceover

A video with no sound can feel empty, and YouCut gives you two easy ways to fill it. You can add a music track from your phone and set its volume so it sits nicely under your clips, or you can record a voiceover while the video plays. A voiceover is useful for explaining a recipe, narrating a trip, or adding a personal message to a birthday video.

When you mix music and voice, balance matters. Keep the music quieter than the voice so every word is clear, and fade the music down at the moments where you speak. If your original clip already has sound you like, such as laughter or waves, you can keep it and skip the music entirely. The best soundtrack is the one that fits the moment, not the loudest one.

Speed control and reverse

Speed control changes how fast your video plays. Slow motion stretches a moment out, which suits a jump, a splash or a goal. Fast motion squeezes a long process, such as cooking or a journey, into a few lively seconds. YouCut lets you change the speed of a whole clip or just part of it, so you can keep the important moment at normal speed and speed up the rest.

Reverse plays a clip backwards, which can be funny or surprising: a ball flies back into a hand, or spilled water jumps back into a glass. It works best on short clips with clear movement. Try it on a clip of someone jumping into a pool or dropping something — the reversed version often gets a laugh. As with all effects, a little goes a long way, so use reverse for one moment rather than a whole video.

Exporting without a watermark

Many free video editors stamp a logo onto every video you export. That logo, called a watermark, sits in a corner and advertises the app to everyone who watches your video. YouCut does not do this: videos you export carry no YouCut logo and no watermark, even though the app is free to use.

A clean export matters most when you share your video with other people. Friends, followers and customers see only your content, and the video looks like you made it with care. It also means you can reuse your video anywhere — on another app, in a presentation, or on a bigger screen — without an unwanted logo following it around.

Exporting for social apps

Different apps like different video shapes. A tall vertical video fills a phone screen and suits short-video apps, while a wide video suits a bigger screen and a square video sits neatly in a photo feed. YouCut lets you set the shape of your video before you export, so you can make one version for each place you plan to share it.

Before you export, think about where the video will be watched. For a phone screen, 1080p is sharp enough and keeps the file size sensible. Check that your text and stickers sit inside the frame and are not cut off at the edges, and watch the video once through to check the sound. A minute of checking now saves you from exporting twice.

If you post the same video in more than one place, export a version for each shape rather than stretching one video to fit everywhere. A vertical video cropped to a square loses the edges of the picture, and a square video stretched tall looks soft. It takes an extra minute to export twice, but each version looks as if it was made for the screen it appears on.

Tips for quick edits

YouCut is at its best when you keep things fast and simple. Decide what the video is about before you open the app, then cut ruthlessly until only the good moments remain. A thirty-second video that holds attention beats a three-minute video that loses it.

  • Plan the order of your clips before you start editing
  • Trim first, then add music, then add text last
  • Use one filter for the whole video so it looks consistent
  • Keep text short and on screen long enough to read
  • Export at 1080p for a good balance of quality and file size

Questions people ask

Is YouCut free?

Yes. YouCut is free to download and the main editing tools cost nothing. Some extra effects and filters are paid options, but you can trim, merge and export full videos without paying anything.

Does YouCut add a watermark?

No. Videos you export from YouCut carry no YouCut logo and no watermark, even in the free version.

How do I install the YouCut APK?

Tap the Download button above to open the official Play Store page, then tap Install. If you use a file from the APKPure archive instead, open the downloaded file and allow your phone to install it when asked, then follow the on-screen steps.

How do I change video speed in YouCut?

Open your clip in YouCut, tap the speed tool, and choose a slower or faster speed. You can change the speed of the whole clip or just part of it, and preview the result before you save it.

What Android version does YouCut need?

YouCut needs Android 8.0 or newer. You can check your version in Settings, under About phone.
