Why lite?

Maps Go is Google Maps stripped down to the map itself. The full Google Maps app is a download of around 280 MB; Maps Go is 1.7 MB. On a phone with almost no storage left, that is the difference between having maps and not having them.

To stay that small, Maps Go is built as a light web-based version of Maps. It shows the map, finds places, and gives directions — but it drops the heavier extras of the full app, including downloaded offline areas and the Timeline history. It also needs Chrome on your phone, because the map itself runs through the web.

The latest Maps Go needs Android 10 or newer, but older builds run on much older phones: version 161.1 and 160.1 need only Android 6.0, and version 159.0 runs on Android 4.4. If your phone cannot run the latest version, pick an older build from the list above — the map still works.

Download Maps Go only from the official Play Store listing linked on this page. Never install an app file from a site you do not trust — fake maps apps can misuse your location.

What is Maps Go?

Maps Go is a smaller, lighter version of Google Maps, made by Google for phones with little storage, little memory, or a slow connection. It is a 1.7 MB download — one of the smallest apps Google makes — and it gives you the map, places and directions without the weight of the full app. If your phone struggles with the full Google Maps, Maps Go is the version made for it.

It is a real Google app, not a copy. It is published by Google, it signs in with your normal Google account, and it shows the same map data, the same places and the same roads as the full app. The difference is how it is built: Maps Go is a light web-based version of Maps, so most of the heavy work happens on the web rather than on your phone.

That light design is why the app is so small. Where the full Maps app downloads hundreds of megabytes of maps, voices and features to your phone, Maps Go downloads almost nothing — it shows you the map you need, when you need it, and nothing more.

Maps, directions and places on a light app

The map itself works the way you expect. You can search for a place by name, see it on the map, and get directions to it — by car, on foot, or by public transport where that information is available. The map moves smoothly, places show their names and details, and you can zoom in and out to see the streets around you.

Directions are the heart of the app. Type where you want to go, pick how you want to travel, and Maps Go shows the route with turn-by-turn guidance. On a slow phone this is where the light design pays off: the route loads quickly, the map follows you as you move, and the app does not drain the battery the way the full app can.

Places work too. Search for a restaurant, a shop, a hospital or a landmark, and Maps Go shows what it finds with addresses, opening hours and reviews where they exist. You can save places you visit often, so the next trip is one tap away.

What Maps Go drops vs full Google Maps

A 1.7 MB app cannot carry everything a 280 MB app carries, so Maps Go makes cuts. The biggest cut is offline maps: the full app lets you download an area of map to your phone and use it without a connection, but Maps Go needs a connection to load the map. If you travel where there is no signal, that matters — see the offline section below.

Other extras are trimmed too. The Timeline history of where you have been, the deeper local guides features, and some of the detailed layers of the full app are missing or simpler in Maps Go. The app also needs Chrome on your phone, because the map itself runs through the web — Google's own listing says so plainly.

What stays is the everyday map: search, directions, places and live traffic. For most trips — finding an address, getting across town, checking a route before you leave — Maps Go does the whole job in a fraction of the space. If you need offline maps or the full feature set, the full Google Maps app is still there.

How to install Maps Go step by step

Installing Maps Go takes about a minute, and you do it from the official Play Store listing. Here is the whole process:

  1. Tap the Download button on this page. It opens the official Maps Go listing in the Play Store.
  2. Tap Install, then wait for the 1.7 MB download to finish. On a slow connection this still takes only a few seconds.
  3. Tap Open, then sign in with your Google account if asked and allow location access so the map can show where you are.
  4. Maps Go needs Chrome on your phone to run. If Chrome is not installed, install it from the Play Store first — it is free.

If the Play Store says the app is not compatible with your phone, your Android version is probably older than Android 10. In that case, pick an older build from the old versions list above — version 161.1 and 160.1 need only Android 6.0, and version 159.0 runs on Android 4.4.

Maps Go vs the full Google Maps app

Maps Go and the full Google Maps app share the same map, the same places and the same roads — they are two doors into the same Google Maps. The difference is what each door costs your phone. The full app is a download of around 280 MB, runs offline maps, Timeline and many extras, and uses more storage, data and battery. Maps Go is 1.7 MB, runs the everyday map, and is gentle on all three.

Choose the full app if your phone is new, has plenty of storage, and you need offline maps or the full feature set. Choose Maps Go if your phone is older, storage is tight, your data plan is small, or the full app feels slow. On an old phone, Maps Go is simply the better map: it opens fast, loads routes fast, and never fills the phone up.

You can switch between them at any time. Your saved places live with your Google account, not in the app, so moving from the full app to Maps Go — or back again — loses nothing. Try Maps Go for a week and see which one your phone prefers.

Offline areas and Maps Go

Here is the honest answer to the question every Maps Go user asks: Maps Go cannot download offline areas. Because it is a light web-based version of Maps, it needs a connection to load the map and your route. Turn the connection off, and the map stops loading new areas.

If you need maps without a connection, the full Google Maps app is the answer. It lets you download an area of map to your phone in advance — your town, your route, a whole city — and then navigate inside that area with no signal at all. The download takes storage, which is exactly why Maps Go does not offer it, but for a trip into the countryside or a place with no signal, it is worth it.

A practical middle route works for many people: use Maps Go for everyday trips where you have signal, and before a trip without signal, load the route in Maps Go while you still have a connection and keep the app open. The route you already loaded stays on screen. It is not a true offline map, but it gets you there when the signal drops for a short stretch.

Tips for old phones

Maps Go already does most of the work, but a few habits keep an old phone running well with it:

  • Free space before you install: remove apps you never open and delete old downloads. A phone with free space runs every app better.
  • Keep Chrome updated too — Maps Go runs through the web, so an old Chrome can slow the map down.
  • Load the route before you leave, while you have Wi-Fi, so the map is ready when you go.
  • Keep Maps Go updated. New versions fix bugs and usually run better than the one before.
  • If the map ever feels slow, clear the app's cache in Settings, under Apps — your saved places are safe, the cache is only temporary files.

An old phone does not need the newest apps — it needs the lightest ones. Maps Go is built for exactly that job.
