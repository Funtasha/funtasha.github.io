What InShot is: video and photo in one app

InShot is a video and photo editor for your phone, made by InShot Video Editor. It does two jobs in one app: it edits videos — trimming, merging, adding music and text — and it edits photos, with filters, collage and retouching. If you post both photos and videos, having one app for both saves you learning two tools and keeps your style consistent across everything you share.

The app is free to download and use, with an optional paid plan that adds extra features and effects. The free version is a complete editor — you can trim, filter, add music and export finished videos and photos without paying. The paid plan is there for people who use the full tool set every day and want every feature.

InShot is known for being easy to pick up. The tools are where you expect them, the buttons are clearly labelled, and you can go from opening the app to sharing a finished video in a few minutes. That simplicity is why it is popular with people who want good results without a learning curve — and why it is often the first editor people recommend to a friend.

Trimming, splitting and merging

Every video edit starts with trimming, and InShot makes it simple. Open a video, drag the edges of the clip to cut the start or the end, and the boring parts are gone. If a clip has a dull middle, split it into two pieces and delete the part you do not want. Trimming well is most of what makes a video feel good to watch — short, tight clips hold attention, and long, wandering ones lose it.

Merging is the other half of the job. InShot lets you join several clips into one video, in any order, with a transition between them if you like. This is how you turn a day of short clips — a morning walk, a lunch, an evening out — into a single video that tells the story of the day.

Speed is the third basic tool. Slow a clip down for a dramatic moment, or speed up a long process so it fits in a few seconds. InShot lets you change the speed of a whole clip or just part of it, and it is one of the easiest ways to add variety to a video that would otherwise feel flat.

Filters, stickers and text

Filters change the look of a clip or a photo in one tap — warmer, cooler, brighter, or styled like old film. InShot has a large filter library, and you can adjust how strong a filter is, so a look can be subtle or bold. A light touch usually works best: pick one filter for the whole video so the clips feel like they belong together.

Stickers add fun: emojis, shapes, seasonal decorations and animated stickers that move with your video. They are great for birthday videos, holiday clips and anything light-hearted. As with effects, a few well-placed stickers look better than a screen full of them — save them for the moments that deserve a smile.

Text is the third layer, and InShot treats it well. You can add titles, captions and labels in many fonts, change their colour and size, and animate them so they slide or fade onto the screen. Text tells viewers what is happening, adds jokes, and makes your video watchable with the sound off — which is how many people scroll.

Music and voiceover

Music changes everything about a video, and InShot makes it easy to add. You can import a song from your phone, trim it to fit your video, and set its volume so it sits under your voice instead of drowning it out. Pick music that fits the mood — gentle for a family film, energetic for a sports clip — and keep it low enough that it supports the video instead of competing with it.

Voiceover is simple to record: tap the microphone, speak while your video plays, and InShot drops the recording onto the timeline. It is perfect for explaining a tutorial, narrating a travel video, or adding a personal message to a birthday montage. Record in a quiet room, close to the phone, for the cleanest sound.

InShot also has sound effects — applause, laughter, whooshes, chimes — that you can drop onto the timeline at the right moment. A well-timed sound effect can make a funny moment funnier, or mark a scene change without a visual transition. Use them sparingly, and they add a professional touch.

Photo editing and collage

InShot's photo editor covers the essentials well. You can crop and rotate, adjust brightness, contrast and saturation, apply filters, and add text and stickers — the same tools as the video side, applied to still images. It is a capable photo editor on its own, and having it in the same app as your video editor keeps your workflow simple.

Collage is where the photo side shines. Pick several photos and InShot arranges them into a single image — a grid, a strip, a free-form layout — with borders and backgrounds you can change. Collages are perfect for birthdays, holidays and events, where one photo cannot tell the whole story but five photos side by side can.

One handy trick is to use the same filter on your photos and your video clips for a single event. When the birthday collage and the birthday video share the same warm filter, they feel like parts of the same story. Consistency is a small thing that makes your posts look thoughtful instead of random.

Exporting for Instagram, TikTok and YouTube

InShot is built for sharing, and it knows the sizes each platform wants. Before you export, pick the aspect ratio that fits where the video is going: vertical for TikTok, Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts, square for a feed post, wide for a YouTube video. Getting the ratio right means your video fills the screen instead of floating in a sea of black bars.

For quality, 1080p is the sweet spot for most sharing: sharp on a phone screen, and a file size that uploads quickly. Higher resolutions are worth it if you filmed in high quality and want the best possible result, but the files are larger and take longer to upload. Frame rate matters too — thirty frames per second is natural for everyday video, and sixty suits fast action.

Exporting takes a while on longer videos, and your phone may feel warm — that is normal, because rendering video is hard work for a phone. Keep InShot open while it exports, and do not lock the screen, or the export may pause. Once it is done, InShot offers to share the video straight to your apps, or you can find it in your gallery.

Quick-edit tips

The fastest edits follow a simple pattern: trim first, then add music, then add text, then export. Doing things in that order keeps you from fiddling with details before the shape of the video is right. If you find yourself spending twenty minutes on a filter before you have trimmed a single clip, you are working backwards.

The second tip is to use the canvas tool. InShot lets you set the background of your video — a colour, a blur of the video itself, or a pattern — which is how you make a horizontal video fit a vertical screen without cropping it. A blurred background is the classic choice: it fills the empty space and looks intentional rather than accidental.

Finally, save your projects. InShot keeps your drafts in the app, so you can come back to a video later and keep editing. If you are working on something important — a birthday video, a gift, a school project — export a draft copy early, so you always have a finished version even if something goes wrong later.

Questions people ask

Is InShot free?

Yes, InShot is free to download and use, and the core editing tools cost nothing. There is an optional paid plan that adds extra features and effects, but you can edit and export videos and photos without it.

How do I install the InShot APK?

Tap the Download button above — it opens the official Play Store listing for InShot. Tap Install there and the app installs itself.

How do I remove the InShot watermark?

Basic edits in InShot export clean, with no watermark. A watermark can appear when you use certain paid-plan assets — swap them for a free one, or use the paid plan, and the mark goes away.

Can InShot edit photos too?

Yes. InShot has a full photo editor alongside the video editor: crop, rotate, filters, text, stickers and collage. You can edit photos and videos in the same app and keep the same style across both.

What Android version does InShot need?

InShot needs Android 8.0 or newer. You can check your Android version in Settings, under About phone.
