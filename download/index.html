How to install an APK on Android

Most of the time you never touch an APK file at all: the Play Store installs the app for you when you tap Install. An APK file only comes into it when you pick an older version from an app page. Those old-version buttons go to the APKPure archive, where you download the app file itself and install it by hand.

Installing an APK takes four steps. Download the file from the archive page, then open it from your downloads or your notifications. Android will ask you to allow installs from that source — this is a normal safety check, and you allow it just for the app you are installing from. Tap Install, wait for it to finish, and open the app. If Android warns you about the file, read the warning and stop if anything looks wrong.

Only install APK files from sources you trust. The old-version links on the app pages go to verified archive pages for the official signed files, and each one was checked before it was listed. Never install an APK from a site you found in a search result or a message, and never install a file that promises paid features for free — that promise is always a lie, and the file is often harmful.

How to check you got the right version

After you install an app, it takes a few seconds to check that you got the version you wanted. Open your phone's Settings, then Apps, then the app you just installed. The version number is shown there, near the bottom of the screen. Compare it with the version in the table on the app page here.

If the numbers do not match, the Play Store may have installed an older build for your phone, or a newer one may have come out since this page was checked. Open the app's Play Store listing and tap Update if one is offered. If you installed an older version on purpose, for an old phone, the number should match the older version you picked — if it does not, uninstall the app and start again from the version you wanted.

What to do when an app is not available in your country

Some apps are only released in certain countries. When that happens, the Play Store shows a message saying the app is not available in your country, and there is no Install button. This is the developer's choice, not a problem with your phone, and no setting on the phone changes it.

The honest options are few but real. Check the app's official website — some apps offer a direct download there, or a web version that works in your browser. TikTok, for example, is not available in the Play Store in some countries, and its website is the place to check. Wait, too: apps do come to new countries over time, and a page that says not available today may offer the app next year.

What you should not do is hunt for the app file on a random site. An app that is not in your country's Play Store is one of the easiest things to fake, and the fake copies are often the harmful ones. If you cannot get the real app from its official page, the safe choice is to wait or to use the web version.

Keeping apps updated and why old versions stop working

Apps change all the time. Developers fix bugs, close security holes and add features, and the Play Store delivers those changes as updates. The easiest way to stay current is to let the Play Store update your apps on its own: open the Play Store, tap your profile picture, tap Settings, and turn on auto-update over Wi-Fi. Your apps then update in the background while you sleep.

Old versions stop working for the same reason new ones exist. When a developer changes how an app talks to its servers, or drops support for an old version, that old version may refuse to open, or lose features, or fail to sign you in. This is normal, and it is not something you did wrong. It is also why every app page here lists the latest version first and treats old versions as a fallback for old phones, not as the thing to install first.

If an app you use every day stops working after years of running fine, the first step is always the same: update it. If your phone cannot run the latest version, look for the lite version of the same app, or an older version from the app page here. If none of those work, the phone itself may have reached the end of its useful life — the old-phones guide linked below explains how to tell.

Lite apps and why they keep old phones usable

A lite app is a smaller version of an app, made for phones with little storage and slow data. It keeps the features people use most and drops the rest, so it downloads faster, takes less space and runs better on an old phone. This section lists eleven of them, from Facebook Lite and Instagram Lite to Google Go, Maps Go and Files by Google. The sizes tell the story: Maps Go is 1.7 MB, Facebook Lite is 3.5 MB, Instagram Lite is 3.8 MB and TikTok Lite is 9.5 MB.

Three of the lite apps in this section need an honest note. YouTube Lite never existed as a Google app — the lightweight YouTube app Google actually made was YouTube Go, and Google shut it down in August 2022. Messenger Lite was shut down by Meta in September 2023, and Spotify Lite was removed from the Play Store in October 2025. Their pages here explain what happened and what to use instead, and their buttons open a Play Store search rather than a listing that does not exist.

Two more things are worth knowing before you install a lite app. Gmail Go has not been updated since December 2022, so it still works but it gets no new fixes — the full Gmail app is the safer choice if your phone can run it. And some newer lite apps need a newer Android than you might expect: Google Go needs Android 11, and the latest Maps Go needs Android 10. Always check the version table on the app page before you install, and pick an older version from the same page if your phone needs one.

Official pages only — why this section links nowhere else

Every link in this section goes to one of two places: the app's official Play Store listing, or its official website. There are no links to file-hosting sites, no links to app stores you have never heard of, and no files hosted on this site at all. That is a deliberate choice, not an accident.

The reason is simple. A changed copy of a popular app is one of the easiest ways to spread harmful software. It looks like the real app, it asks for the same permissions, and then it does something the real app never would — reads your messages, steals your sign-in, or signs you up for things you never asked for. The only safe way to get an app is from the people who made it, and that is the only place this section sends you.

If a button on this site ever takes you somewhere that does not look like the official Play Store page or the app's official website, stop and do not install anything. Come back here and check the version table on the app page, or use the Play Store search on your own phone. The real app is always worth the extra minute.

Questions people ask

Is it safe to download an APK?

It is safe when the file comes from a source you can trust. The old-version buttons on the app pages go to verified archive pages for the official signed files, and each link was checked before it was listed. An APK from a site you do not know is not safe — never install one, and never install a file that promises paid features for free.

How do I install an APK on Android?

Download the file, then open it from your downloads or your notifications. Android will ask you to allow installs from that source — allow it just for the app you are installing from, then tap Install. If the button takes you to the Play Store instead, there is no file to install: just tap Install on the Play Store page and the store does the rest.

Where does the download button take me?

To the app's official Play Store listing, or to its official website where there is no Play Store listing. Three apps in this section have no official listing at all — YouTube Lite, Messenger Lite and Spotify Lite — and for those three the button opens a Play Store search instead. Their app pages explain why.

Why does an app say it is not available in my country?

Some apps are only released in certain countries, and the Play Store blocks the install everywhere else. This is the developer's choice, not a fault in your phone. Check the app's official website for a direct download or a web version, and do not install the app file from a random site — fake copies of region-blocked apps are common and often harmful.

How do I update an app?

Open the Play Store, tap your profile picture, then tap Manage apps and device, and update the apps listed there. You can also turn on auto-update in the Play Store settings so your apps update on their own over Wi-Fi. If an update is not offered, your phone may already have the newest version it can run.

What if the download does not start?

Check your connection first, then check your storage — a phone with no free space cannot download anything. Close the Play Store and open it again, or restart your phone. If the button opened a new tab, close it and come back here, then tap the button again. If the Play Store still will not start the download, clear the Play Store cache in Settings, then Apps, then Google Play Store, then Storage.

What is a lite app?

A lite app is a smaller version of an app, made for old phones and slow data. It keeps the features people use most and drops the rest, so it is a much smaller download and uses less storage. Maps Go is 1.7 MB, Facebook Lite is 3.5 MB, Instagram Lite is 3.8 MB and TikTok Lite is 9.5 MB. Not every lite app is still available: Messenger Lite was shut down in 2023 and Spotify Lite was removed from the Play Store in 2025.
