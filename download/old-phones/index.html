Best Apps for Old Android Phones

An old Android phone does not have to feel old. With lite apps, a light browser and a little free space, a phone that is five or six years old can still run the apps you use every day. This guide shows you which apps to switch and how to keep your old phone running.

Download lite apps only from the official pages linked here. Never install an app file from a site you do not trust — a fake lite app can steal your sign-in details and your chats.

Why old phones feel slow

An old phone feels slow for three reasons, and only one of them is the phone itself. The first is storage: when a phone's storage is nearly full, everything slows down, because the phone has nowhere to put temporary files. The second is heavy apps: the apps you installed years ago have grown with every update, and they now want more memory and more storage than your phone has to spare. The third is the Android version: a phone that stopped getting updates years ago cannot run the newest apps, and the apps it can run are often old versions that were never made for it.

The good news is that two of those three reasons are fixable. Freeing up storage and swapping heavy apps for lite versions can make a five-year-old phone feel usable again, sometimes for years more. Only the third reason — an Android version too old for the apps you need — is a hard limit, and even that has workarounds, like older versions of apps and the browser. This guide works through all of it, starting with the easiest win of all: space.

  • Storage nearly full — check in Settings, then Storage.
  • Apps take a long time to open, or close on their own.
  • The phone gets hot doing simple things.
  • Updates fail because there is no room to install them.

What a lite app is and why it runs faster

A lite app is the same service in a smaller package. Facebook Lite is still Facebook, Instagram Lite is still Instagram, and Maps Go is still Google Maps — but each one is a much smaller download, uses less memory while it runs, and sends less data over your connection. The app makers build them for phones with little storage and slow data, which is exactly what an old phone is.

The sizes show how big the difference is. Maps Go is 1.7 MB, Facebook Lite is 3.5 MB, Instagram Lite is 3.8 MB and TikTok Lite is 9.5 MB. Google Go is 23.7 MB and Files by Google is 19.1 MB. A lite app also tends to be simpler inside: fewer animations, fewer background jobs, and no features you never used. That simplicity is what makes it fast on a phone that struggles with the full app.

One honest note before you go through the list below. Not every lite app in this guide is still available. YouTube Lite never existed as a Google app. Messenger Lite was shut down by Meta in September 2023. Spotify Lite was removed from the Play Store in October 2025. Their pages in this section explain what happened and what to use instead, so nothing here will send you looking for an app that is gone.

Free up space before you install anything

Do this first, before you install a single lite app: make room. Open Settings, then Storage, and look at what is using your space. On most old phones the answer is the same: photos and videos, apps you never open, and the downloads folder. Delete the apps you have not opened in months — you can always install them again later. Move photos and videos to a computer or a cloud backup, or delete the ones you do not need.

Files by Google is the best tool for this job, and it is free from Google. It finds junk files, old screenshots, duplicate photos and big files you forgot about, and it lets you delete them a few taps at a time. Install it first, run its clean-up suggestions, and you may free enough space to make the phone feel faster before you change anything else.

Aim to keep some free space at all times. A phone with no free storage runs slowly no matter which apps you install, and updates fail when there is nowhere to put them. If you can keep a gigabyte or two free, the phone has room to breathe — and the lite apps below will have room to install.

Which apps to replace first

Start with the apps you open most. The browser is first: Opera Mini is a light browser that compresses pages to save data, and it runs well on old phones. It is also the best way to watch YouTube on a phone where the YouTube app will not install — more on that below.

Facebook is next. Facebook Lite is the official lightweight Facebook from Meta, and at 3.5 MB it is a fraction of the size of the full app. It keeps the feed, notifications and messages in one small app, so you can delete the full Facebook app and the separate Messenger app and use Lite for both.

Messaging needs a frank word. Messenger Lite was shut down by Meta in September 2023, so it is no longer available to install. Your chats are safe — they live in your Facebook account, not in the app — and you can read them in Facebook Lite, which has messaging built in, or in the full Messenger app if your phone can run it.

Music is the same story. Spotify Lite was removed from the Play Store in October 2025, so new installs are no longer possible. If you already have it, it may keep working for a while, but the safe choice is the full Spotify app, or Spotify in the browser, where your playlists and library are exactly as you left them.

Maps, search and email come next. Maps Go is 1.7 MB and gives you maps, directions and places; the latest version needs Android 10, but older Maps Go builds on its app page run on much older phones. Google Go is the lightweight search app, but it needs Android 11, so on a very old phone the browser is the better choice for search. Gmail Go has not been updated since December 2022 — it still works, but it gets no new fixes, so use the full Gmail app if your phone can run it.

How to keep using YouTube on an old phone

YouTube is the app old-phone owners ask about most, and it needs an honest answer. There is no app called YouTube Lite from Google — Google never released one. The lightweight YouTube app Google actually made was YouTube Go, and Google shut it down in August 2022. Any app that calls itself YouTube Lite today is not from Google, and you should not install it.

What works instead depends on your phone. If the YouTube app still installs and opens on your phone, keep using it — and lower the video quality in the app settings to save data. If the app will not install, or it opens and then closes, the browser route is your friend: open YouTube in Chrome or Opera Mini, sign in, and watch there. Opera Mini plays YouTube well on old phones, and you can add the YouTube site to your home screen so it opens like an app.

The guide linked at the bottom of this page walks through both routes step by step — the app, the browser, and the data-saving settings that make videos play smoothly on slow data. If YouTube is the one app you cannot live without, read that guide next.

Battery and data tips

Lite apps already use less battery and less data than the full apps, but a few habits help more. Turn your screen brightness down — the screen is the biggest battery user on any phone. Turn off background data for apps you rarely open, in Settings, then Apps, then each app, then Mobile data. Do your updates and downloads on Wi-Fi, and leave mobile data for the things that need it.

Restart your phone once a week. It clears out the background clutter that builds up over time, and it is the simplest fix there is for a phone that has gone sluggish. Close apps you are not using instead of leaving them running, and turn off autoplay for videos in your social apps — videos starting on their own are the fastest way to burn through a data allowance.

When an old phone is really done

Lite apps can keep an old phone going for years, but they cannot keep it going forever. The signs that a phone is really done are clear. Apps you need refuse to install because they need a newer Android than your phone has. The battery dies in an hour even when you barely use the phone. The storage is full even after you have deleted everything you can. The phone restarts on its own, or the screen stops responding.

One of those signs on its own is not the end — a new battery can fix the battery, and a memory card can help with storage on phones that take one. But when several of them arrive together, it is time to start looking for a newer phone. Until then, the lite apps in this guide are the cheapest upgrade there is: no new phone, no cost, just smaller apps that let the phone you already own keep doing its job.

Questions people ask

Will lite apps work on my old phone?

Most of them will, but check the Android version each one needs. Facebook Lite and Instagram Lite need Android 8.0 or newer, TikTok Lite needs Android 5.0 or newer, and Files by Google needs Android 8.0 or newer. Google Go needs Android 11.0, and the latest Maps Go needs Android 10.0 — but older Maps Go builds on its app page run on Android 4.4 and up. If your phone is too old for the latest version, the app page lists older versions that may still run.

What Android version do I need?

It depends on the app — each app page shows the Android version it needs in its version table. To find your own version, open Settings, then About phone, then Android version. If your phone is on Android 8 or newer, most lite apps will install. On older versions, check the older builds on each app page.

Is YouTube Lite really from Google?

No. Google never released an app called YouTube Lite. Google's lightweight YouTube app was YouTube Go, and Google shut it down in August 2022. Any app that calls itself YouTube Lite today is not from Google — do not install it. Watch YouTube in the browser instead, or read the YouTube guide linked at the bottom of this page.

Will I lose my chats if I switch to a lite app?

No. Your chats live in your account, not in the app, so switching to a lite version does not delete anything. Messenger Lite is gone, but your messages are still in your Facebook account — read them in Facebook Lite or in the full Messenger app. For WhatsApp, back up your chats first in Settings, then Chats, then Chat backup, before you reinstall anything.

How much space does a lite app save?

A lite app is often only a few megabytes. Maps Go is 1.7 MB, Facebook Lite is 3.5 MB, Instagram Lite is 3.8 MB and TikTok Lite is 9.5 MB. Swapping several heavy apps for lite versions frees a noticeable amount of space on a phone that is nearly full — and the phone runs faster with room to breathe.

Can I still use YouTube on a 5-year-old phone?

Yes. If the YouTube app still installs on your phone, keep using it and lower the video quality to save data. If the app will not install, watch YouTube in the browser instead — Chrome and Opera Mini both play YouTube well on old phones. The YouTube guide linked at the bottom of this page walks through both routes step by step.
