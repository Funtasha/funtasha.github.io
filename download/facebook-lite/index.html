Why lite?

Facebook Lite keeps the Facebook you use every day — your feed, your friends, your groups, your messages — and leaves out the parts that make the full app heavy. The feed loads in a simpler layout, photos open at a smaller size first, and the app does far less work in the background. On a phone with little memory, that is the difference between an app that opens in a second and one that freezes the phone.

The size tells the story. The latest version is 3.5 MB, much smaller than the full Facebook app, and it uses less data because it loads less on every screen. That matters twice over on an old phone: you save storage you do not have, and you save data you pay for.

One honest note: current Facebook Lite versions need Android 8.0 or newer, including the older builds listed above, so a phone on Android 7 or older cannot run them. On those phones the mobile Facebook site in the browser is the working option, and it signs in with the same account.

Download only from the official page linked here, and never install an app file from a site you do not trust. Facebook Lite is free — if a site asks you to pay for it, or for a special version of it, walk away.

What Facebook Lite is

Facebook Lite is the official lightweight version of Facebook, made by Meta Platforms, Inc. for phones with little storage, little memory, or a slow connection. It is the same Facebook — the same account, the same friends, the same groups — in an app built to load fast and use little data. If the full Facebook app makes your phone slow, or the Play Store says your phone cannot run it, Lite is the version Meta made for you.

It is not a copy, a clone, or a third-party remake. It comes from Meta, it is listed on the Play Store under Meta's name, and it signs in with your normal Facebook account. The download button on this page goes to that official listing, and the version table above shows the latest version we checked.

Lite is aimed at the same people who use the other lite apps on this site: anyone on an older phone, a cheap phone, or a connection that drops in and out. It loads a simpler feed, asks for less from your phone, and keeps working where the full app gives up.

What Facebook Lite keeps — and what it drops

Lite keeps the parts of Facebook most people use every day. You can scroll your feed, react to posts, comment, share, follow pages, join groups, and see notifications. You can post text and photos, and you can message friends without a second app. Your profile, your friends list, and your saved posts are all there, because it is the same account.

What it drops is the weight. Video plays with fewer quality options, photos load smaller first, and some of the full app's heavier tools are simplified or missing. The app does less in the background, so it uses less battery and less data when you are not looking at it. Most people do not miss what is gone — the feed still feels like Facebook, just faster.

If you spend your time watching long videos or using the full app's newest effects, you will notice the difference. Lite is built for reading, posting, and messaging, not for the heaviest media. For an old phone, that is the right trade.

How much space and data it saves

The latest Facebook Lite is 3.5 MB. That is the whole app — smaller than a single photo from a modern phone camera. The full Facebook app is much larger, and it grows as it caches photos and videos, so on a phone with 16 GB of storage the difference is the difference between an app that fits and one that does not.

Data is the other saving. Lite loads a simpler feed, which means fewer images and videos arriving before you ask for them. Photos open at a smaller size first, and video does not autoplay at full quality. On a small data plan, that adds up fast — the same half hour of scrolling uses noticeably less data than the full app would.

There is also the saving you do not see: Lite does less in the background. The full app checks for updates, preloads content, and keeps services running even when you are not using it. Lite is quieter, which saves both data and battery over the course of a day.

How to install Facebook Lite

Installing from the Play Store takes a minute, and you do not need to remove the full Facebook app first — though on a phone short on space, removing the heavy app and keeping Lite is usually the right move.

  1. Tap the Download button above. It opens the official Facebook Lite listing on the Play Store.
  2. Tap Install and wait. At 3.5 MB, the download finishes quickly even on a slow connection.
  3. Open Facebook Lite and sign in with your normal Facebook account. Your friends, groups and messages will be there.
  4. If you already have the full Facebook app and your storage is full, uninstall the full app after Lite is working.

If the Play Store says Facebook Lite is not compatible with your phone, your Android version is the usual reason — the latest version needs Android 8.0 or newer. Check your version in Settings, under About phone. If your phone is older than that, the mobile Facebook site in your browser is the way to stay connected, and it needs no install at all.

Facebook Lite vs the full Facebook app

The two apps are the same service with different appetites. The full Facebook app is built for new phones: it loads high-quality photos and video, runs more features in the background, and takes more storage and more data. On a new phone that is fine. On an old phone it means slow scrolling, a hot phone, and a battery that drains fast.

Lite is built for the opposite end. It loads a simpler feed, uses less memory, and asks less of your processor and your connection. It opens fast on a phone where the full app takes ten seconds to show anything, and it keeps working on a weak signal where the full app stalls.

Choose Lite if your phone is more than a few years old, if storage is tight, or if your data plan is small. Choose the full app if your phone is new and you want every feature at full quality. There is no wrong answer — and you can switch between them any time, because both sign in to the same account.

Messaging inside Facebook Lite

One thing that surprises new Lite users is that messaging is built in. The full Facebook app sends you to a separate Messenger app when you want to chat, but Facebook Lite lets you read and reply to messages inside the app itself. For an old phone, that is one less heavy app to install and one less app running in the background.

Your conversations are the same ones you would see in Messenger, because they live in your Facebook account, not in the app. You can send text, photos, and voice messages, and you can see when a friend has read your message. Group chats work too.

If you already use Messenger Lite or the full Messenger app, your chats stay in place — switching to Lite does not delete anything. Many people on old phones end up using Facebook Lite for both the feed and the messages, and removing the separate messaging app entirely.

Tips for slow phones and slow data

Lite helps a lot on its own, but a few habits make an old phone feel faster still. Free up space first: delete apps you do not use, clear old photos and videos, and empty the downloads folder. A phone with no free storage runs slowly no matter which apps you install.

Inside Lite, keep video quality low and turn off autoplay if the app offers it. Read the feed on Wi-Fi when you can, and save the video-heavy scrolling for then. Close apps you are not using instead of leaving them running, and restart your phone once a week — it clears out the background clutter that builds up over time.

Finally, keep Lite updated. Meta releases new versions often — the version table above shows the latest we checked — and updates usually bring small speed fixes. The Play Store updates it by itself if you let it, and at 3.5 MB the update is never a burden.
