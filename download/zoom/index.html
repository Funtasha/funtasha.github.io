What is Zoom?

Zoom is a video meeting app for Android that lets you join and host video meetings, classes and calls from your phone. It is used by schools, offices, families and community groups for anything that needs people to see and hear each other at the same time. You can join a meeting with a link or a meeting ID, without needing an account of your own.

Zoom is known as Zoom Workplace on the Play Store, and it does more than video: it has team chat, screen sharing, a whiteboard and meeting notes. But most people use it for one thing — a simple, reliable video call with one person or a whole group, from wherever they are.

Joining and hosting a meeting

Joining a meeting is easy. Tap the meeting link someone sent you, or open Zoom, tap Join, and enter the meeting ID and passcode. You can choose whether your video is on or off when you join, and you can join with or without signing in. If the host has a waiting room on, you wait until they let you in.

Hosting a meeting means starting one yourself. Sign in, tap New Meeting, and share the meeting link or ID with the people you want to invite. As host, you can mute people, let people in from the waiting room, share your screen, and end the meeting for everyone. Keep the passcode private, so only the people you invited can join.

In a meeting, you can raise your hand to get the host's attention without interrupting, and you can send a quick reaction, such as a thumbs up or applause, to show what you think. The chat panel lets you type a question or share a link while someone else is speaking, which is useful in a large meeting where talking over each other would be messy.

Classes and calls

Zoom is widely used for classes, because a teacher can share a screen, show slides, and talk to a whole class at once. Students join from their phones, raise a hand to ask a question, and answer in the chat. If you are a student, join a few minutes early, keep your microphone muted until you speak, and sit somewhere quiet with good light on your face.

For family and friend calls, Zoom works like a big video call. You can see everyone at once in gallery view, or focus on the person speaking. Group calls on the free plan have a time limit, so for a long family catch-up, plan to restart the meeting or keep it short. One-to-one calls are simpler and run longer.

Breakout rooms split a large meeting into smaller groups for a short time, which teachers use for group work and teams use for discussion. The host sends people into rooms, visits each room to check on them, and brings everyone back together when the time is up. If you are sent to a breakout room, you can ask the host for help from inside the room.

Screen sharing and whiteboard

Screen sharing lets you show everyone in a meeting what is on your phone screen: a photo, a document, a web page or an app. Tap Share in the meeting controls, choose what to share, and everyone sees it. It is the main way teachers show slides and the main way teams review a document together. Remember that everyone sees exactly what is on your screen, so close private messages and notifications first.

The whiteboard is a shared drawing space where everyone in the meeting can write and sketch at the same time. It is useful for brainstorming, for explaining an idea with a quick diagram, or for working through a maths problem together. You can draw with your finger, add text, and save the whiteboard when you are done so nobody loses the work.

  • Share your screen to show slides, photos or documents
  • Close private apps before you share your screen
  • Use the whiteboard for brainstorming and quick diagrams
  • Ask the host to let you share if the button is missing
  • Save the whiteboard before the meeting ends

Setting up Zoom step by step

Setting up Zoom takes a few minutes. Install the app from the official Play Store page, open it, and sign in with an account, or join a meeting straight away without signing in.

  • Install Zoom from the official Play Store page and open it
  • Tap Sign In and enter your account details, or tap Join a Meeting to join without an account
  • Allow access to the camera and microphone when asked
  • Add your name so other people know who you are
  • Test your video and audio in Settings before your first meeting

You can sign in with an email address, or with a Google, Apple or Facebook account. If you only ever join meetings that others host, you may never need to sign in at all — a meeting link and passcode are enough.

Audio and video settings

Good audio matters more than good video in a meeting. In Settings, under Meetings, you can choose to mute your microphone when you join, which is polite in a large meeting, and to turn your video off when you join. Use earphones if you can, since they cut echo and help you hear clearly.

For video, sit facing a window or a lamp so your face is lit, and put the phone steady at eye level rather than holding it in your hand. In Settings you can also choose to use the front or rear camera, and to turn on a virtual background, though backgrounds need a fairly new phone to work smoothly.

Zoom on an old phone

Zoom needs Android 9.0 or newer. On an old phone, video meetings are demanding, because the camera, microphone and screen are all working at once. If Zoom feels slow, close every other app first, plug in your charger, and keep the phone cool — a hot phone slows down.

If the latest version will not install on your phone, the Play Store will offer the newest version that fits your phone instead. Turning your own video off is the simplest way to make a meeting run smoothly on an old phone: you can still see and hear everyone, and your phone has much less work to do.

Tips for slow data

Video meetings use a lot of data, so a slow or small connection needs care. The biggest saving is turning your video off, which cuts data use sharply while you can still listen and speak. You can also ask the host to share only when needed, since shared screens use data too.

  • Turn your video off when your connection is weak
  • Join audio by phone if the app connection keeps dropping
  • Sit close to your Wi-Fi router, or use mobile data if Wi-Fi is weak
  • Close other apps and stop downloads during a meeting
  • Keep meetings short, and use chat for anything that does not need a live call

If your video freezes, it is usually the connection, not the app. Wait a few seconds, and if it does not recover, leave and rejoin the meeting with your video off. A stable audio connection is better than a broken video one.

Questions people ask

Is Zoom free?

Yes. Zoom is free to download, and you can join meetings for free. Free group meetings have a time limit, and a paid plan removes it and adds more features.

How do I install Zoom?

Tap the Download button above to open the official Play Store page, then tap Install. Open Zoom when it finishes and sign in, or join a meeting straight away.

How do I join a meeting?

Tap the meeting link someone sent you, or open Zoom, tap Join, and enter the meeting ID and passcode. You can join with or without signing in.

What Android version does Zoom need?

Zoom needs Android 9.0 or newer. You can check your version in Settings, under About phone.

Can I use Zoom on an old phone?

Yes, if your phone runs Android 9.0 or newer. Close other apps first, keep the phone charged, and turn your video off if the meeting runs slowly.
