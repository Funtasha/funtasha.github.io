Why lite?

Spotify Lite kept the core of Spotify — search for a song, play it, save it, build a playlist — in an app that opened fast and asked little of your phone. It left out the heavier parts of the full app, and it gave you controls the full app does not: a limit on how much data it could use, and a limit on how much storage it could take.

The size was the point. Spotify Lite was much smaller than the full Spotify app, which is 153.5 MB in its current version, and it used less data because it loaded less on every screen and was built for slow connections. On a phone with little storage and a small data plan, that was the difference between music that played and music that buffered.

The trade was features. Lite did not offer offline downloads — that stayed in the full app — and it had fewer audio quality options. But for listening at home or on the move with a small phone and a small plan, it was all most people needed, which is why its removal in October 2025 was felt by so many owners of older phones.

What Spotify Lite was

Spotify Lite was the official lightweight version of Spotify, made by Spotify AB for phones with little storage, little memory, or a slow connection. It played the same music, from the same account, in an app built to load fast and use little data. If the full Spotify app made your phone slow, or your data plan ran out too fast, Lite was the version Spotify made for you.

It was not a copy or a remake. It came from Spotify, it was listed on the Play Store under Spotify's name, and it signed in with your normal Spotify account. Your playlists, your saved songs, and your library were the same as in the full app, because they live in your account, not in the app on your phone.

Spotify wound the app down through late 2024 and 2025, and removed it from the Play Store on 23 October 2025. The last version was 1.9.0.72404, released in August 2024. This page keeps the facts straight: what Lite was, why it is gone, and how to keep listening on an old phone today.

What Spotify Lite kept — and what it dropped

Lite kept the parts of Spotify most people use every day. You could search for songs and artists, play albums and playlists, save songs to your library, and listen to music made for you. Your home screen showed your recent listening and your playlists, and everything synced with your account, so a song saved on your phone was saved everywhere.

What it dropped was the weight. The full Spotify app loads large images, video clips, and extra sections on every screen; Lite loaded a simpler screen with less on it. It also left out offline downloads — in Lite you could only stream, and saving songs to listen without a connection stayed in the full app. Audio quality options were fewer too, which was part of how it saved data.

If you listened mainly at home on Wi-Fi, or on the move with a small data plan, Lite had everything that mattered. If you wanted to download songs for a flight, or hear the highest quality audio, the full app was the one to use.

Data controls and storage limit

Lite's best idea was a pair of controls the full app does not have. You could set a limit on how much mobile data the app was allowed to use, and a limit on how much storage it could take up on your phone. When the app reached the limit, it told you — so there were no surprises at the end of the month and no sudden full-storage warnings.

The app also showed you how much data and storage it had used, in plain numbers, on a screen of its own. That made it easy to see what listening actually cost you, and to change the limit if your plan changed. For anyone on a small prepaid data plan, that visibility was worth more than any feature.

The full Spotify app does not have these limits built in, so if you move to it, you will need to watch your data another way. The section on listening with slow data below has the practical steps.

Why Spotify Lite was shut down

Spotify never announced the shutdown with a big statement. The app simply stopped being updated — the last version, 1.9.0.72404, came out in August 2024 — and through late 2024 and 2025 it faded from the Play Store, until it was removed entirely on 23 October 2025. Phones and connections have improved since Lite launched, and Spotify clearly decided the full app could serve those users now.

Whatever the reason, the practical result is the same: the light app is gone, and the options left are the full Spotify app or the browser player. The sections below cover both, and the tips for listening on slow data still apply.

One thing the shutdown did not do is delete anyone's music. Playlists, saved songs, and your library live in your Spotify account, not in the Lite app, so they are still there when you open the full app or the browser player with the same account.

How to keep listening on an old phone

The first option is the full Spotify app. It signs in with the same account, shows the same playlists and library, and has every feature, including offline downloads. It is larger — 153.5 MB in the current version — and it needs Android 7.0 or newer, so it suits phones that can still run it comfortably.

The second option needs no install at all: open your browser and go to open.spotify.com, sign in, and listen from there. The web player has your playlists, your library, and search, and it works on any phone with a browser. It uses no storage, which matters on a phone that is already full.

If your phone runs Android 5 or 6, the browser player is the way to go, because the full app needs Android 7.0 or newer. If your phone runs Android 7 or newer but storage is tight, try the full app first — and if it feels heavy, the browser player is always there as the lighter fallback.

Spotify Lite vs the full Spotify app

The two apps were the same service with different appetites. The full Spotify app is built for new phones: it loads large images, video, and extra sections, runs more in the background, and takes more storage and more data. On a new phone that is fine. On an old phone it means slow loading and a battery that drains fast.

Lite was built for the opposite end. It was much smaller than the full app, it opened fast on a phone with little memory, and it kept working on a weak signal where the full app buffered. It ran on Android 5.0 and up, while the full app now needs Android 7.0 or newer.

What the full app has that Lite never did is offline downloads, higher audio quality options, and the newest features. If your phone can run it, the full app is the better listen. If your phone struggles, the browser player gives you the music without the weight.

Listening on slow data

Whether you use the full app or the browser player, the audio quality setting is the biggest lever on data use. Set it to low or normal instead of high, and the same hour of music uses far less data. On a phone speaker or cheap earphones, the difference is hard to hear.

A few more habits help. Listen on Wi-Fi when you can, and save the long sessions for then. In the full app, download your favourite playlists while you are on Wi-Fi and play them offline later — that uses no mobile data at all. Close other apps before you listen, so nothing else is using data in the background.

Finally, keep an eye on your storage. The full app caches songs as you play them, and the cache grows over time. Clearing it in the app settings now and then keeps the phone fast, and your playlists and saved songs are safe in your account.

Questions people ask

Is Spotify Lite official?

It was. Spotify Lite was made by Spotify AB, the same company that makes Spotify. Spotify removed it from the Play Store on 23 October 2025, so there is no official listing to download from any more.

Is Spotify Lite free?

Yes, it was free to download and free to use with a free Spotify account, just like the full app. You only pay for the data your connection uses.

How do I install Spotify Lite?

You cannot install Spotify Lite from the Play Store any more — Spotify removed it in October 2025. The full Spotify app is the current option, and it signs in with the same account and shows the same playlists and library.

Does Spotify Lite have my playlists?

It did. Spotify Lite signed in with your normal Spotify account, so your playlists, saved songs and library were the same as in the full app. They are still there in the full app and in the browser player today.

What Android version does Spotify Lite need?

The last version needed Android 5.0 or newer. The full Spotify app needs Android 7.0 or newer, so if your phone runs Android 5 or 6, the browser player at open.spotify.com is the way to keep listening.
