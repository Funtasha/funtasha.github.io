What Alight Motion is and what motion graphics means

Alight Motion is a motion graphics app for your phone, made by Alight Creative. Motion graphics means graphics that move: text that slides in, shapes that grow and spin, logos that assemble themselves, and animations that bring a still design to life. It is the style you see in video intros, social media posts, music videos and animated stories — and Alight Motion puts the tools for making it in your pocket.

The app is free to download and use, with an optional paid plan that adds extra features and removes limits. The free version is a real animation tool — you can build keyframe animations, draw vector shapes, apply effects and export finished videos without paying. The paid plan is there for people who animate every day and want the full set of tools.

Alight Motion is different from a normal video editor. A video editor cuts clips together; Alight Motion animates things. You can still work with video clips in it, but its heart is movement: making a shape travel across the screen, making text bounce in on the beat, making a still image feel alive. If you have ever wanted to make the animated intros and edits you see online, this is the app for it.

Keyframes in plain words

Keyframes sound technical, but the idea is simple. A keyframe is a snapshot of how something looks at one moment in time — where it is, how big it is, how transparent it is. Put two keyframes on the timeline, change the thing between them, and Alight Motion fills in the movement between the two snapshots. That is all animation is: a start point, an end point, and the app drawing the journey between them.

Here is a simple example. Put a circle on the left of the screen and add a keyframe. Move a second down the timeline, drag the circle to the right, and add another keyframe. Play the video and the circle glides from left to right. Add a third keyframe where the circle grows bigger, and it grows as it moves. Every animation in Alight Motion — from a bouncing logo to a full animated scene — is built from these small steps.

The power of keyframes is control. You decide exactly where something goes, how fast it gets there, and what changes along the way. You can animate position, size, rotation, colour and transparency, all on the same object at the same time. It feels like a lot at first, but start with one thing moving in one direction, and the rest follows naturally.

Vector graphics and shapes

Alight Motion lets you draw with vector graphics, which are shapes defined by maths instead of pixels. That matters because a vector shape stays perfectly sharp no matter how big you make it — a circle you draw at the size of a coin can grow to fill the screen without going blurry. It is the same idea professional designers use, and it is built into the app.

You can draw freehand, or build with shapes: circles, rectangles, stars, polygons and lines. Each shape can have its own fill colour, outline and transparency, and each one can be animated with keyframes. Shapes are the building blocks of motion graphics — a few circles and rectangles, animated well, can become a loading spinner, a logo reveal, or a whole animated scene.

You can also import images and graphics you made elsewhere, and animate them the same way. A photo, a logo, a drawing — anything you can put on the timeline can be moved, spun, scaled and faded with keyframes. Start with shapes to learn the tools, then bring in your own images when you are ready to make something personal.

Effects and easing

Effects in Alight Motion change how things look and feel as they move. There are blur effects, glow effects, colour effects, and effects that bend and warp your shapes. You can stack effects on the same object, and each effect has settings you can adjust — and animate. A glow that brightens as a logo lands, or a blur that clears as a scene comes into focus, adds a level of polish that makes an animation feel finished.

Easing is the secret ingredient of good animation, and it is simpler than it sounds. Without easing, an object moves at the same speed from start to finish, which looks stiff and robotic. Easing changes the speed along the way: slow at the start, fast in the middle, slow at the end — the way real things move. A ball does not teleport to full speed; it accelerates. Easing makes your animations feel like real movement instead of a robot sliding across the screen.

Alight Motion gives you easing curves you can pick and adjust for every keyframe. The default smooth curve works well for most things. Try a bouncy curve for playful animation, or a sharp curve for a snappy, punchy movement. Small easing choices make a big difference — two animations with the same keyframes can feel completely different with different easing.

Presets: animation in one tap

Presets are ready-made animations you can apply to any text or shape in one tap. Instead of setting keyframes by hand, you pick a preset — a bounce, a slide, a spin, a fade — and Alight Motion applies the movement for you. They are the fastest way to make text and shapes move, and they are a great starting point when you are learning.

Presets are also a way to study animation. Apply a preset, then look at the keyframes it created: you will see exactly where the object starts, where it goes, and how the easing is set. That is a free lesson in how good animation is built, and you can change any of the keyframes to make the preset your own.

Many creators build a library of their own presets for the moves they use most — a favourite text entrance, a logo reveal, a transition. Once you have made an animation you like, save it as a preset and reuse it in every project. It keeps your videos consistent and saves you setting up the same keyframes again and again.

Exporting animation and video

When your animation is ready, exporting turns it into a video file you can share. Alight Motion can export in several formats, including video files and image sequences, and you can pick the resolution and frame rate. For sharing on social media, 1080p at 30 frames per second is a good default: sharp, smooth, and a file size that uploads quickly.

Frame rate matters more in animation than in ordinary video, because it decides how smooth the movement looks. Thirty frames per second is smooth for most animation. Sixty frames per second is smoother still, and it suits fast, bouncy motion — but the files are larger and the export takes longer. Pick the frame rate that fits the project, not the highest number available.

Exporting an animation with many layers and effects takes time, and your phone may warm up while it renders. Keep Alight Motion open and let it finish. When the export is done, the video appears in your gallery, ready to share — or to drop into a video editor as an intro, a title card, or an animated overlay.

Your first animation project

The best first project is a small one: a five-second animated title for your name or your channel. It teaches you every core skill — shapes, text, keyframes, easing and export — in a single short video, and you end up with something you can actually use.

  • Start a new project and add your name as text in the middle of the screen.
  • Add a keyframe at the start with the text small and transparent.
  • Move one second along, make the text full size and fully visible, and add a keyframe.
  • Add a shape behind the text and animate it sliding in from the side.
  • Pick a smooth easing curve for each keyframe so the movement feels natural.
  • Preview the animation, fix anything that feels off, then export it.

Do not aim for perfection on your first try — aim for finished. A five-second animation that you made yourself, with your own name moving on screen, is a real achievement, and every animator started with something just like it. Make the next one a little bolder, and the one after that bolder still.
