What KineMaster is

KineMaster is a video editor for your phone that has been around for years, made by KineMaster Corporation. It is built for people who want a full editing timeline on a mobile screen: multiple layers of video, image, text and handwriting, precise trimming, chroma key, blending modes and a large library of effects and music. It is one of the most capable editors you can run on a phone.

The app is free to download and use, with an optional paid plan that adds extra features and removes the watermark from exports. The free version is a complete editor — you can cut, layer, add effects and export finished videos without paying. The paid plan is there for people who edit every day and want the full set of tools and a clean export.

KineMaster suits almost any kind of video: short clips for social media, longer vlogs, school projects, tutorials and family films. Its layer system is what sets it apart from simpler editors — once you understand layers, you can build videos that look like they were made on a computer.

Layers: video, image, text and handwriting

Layers are the heart of KineMaster. A simple editor gives you one row of clips. KineMaster lets you stack things on top of your main video: a second video playing in a corner, an image floating over a scene, text that moves with the action, or a handwritten note drawn straight onto the frame. Each layer has its own timeline, so you decide exactly when it appears and disappears.

Video layers are how picture-in-picture works — a reaction video, a facecam over gameplay, a second angle of the same scene. Image layers are how you add a logo, a photo, or a title card over your footage. Text layers carry titles, captions and labels, in many fonts, with animation so the words slide, fade or pop onto the screen.

Handwriting is KineMaster's most personal layer. You can draw directly on the video with your finger — circle something, underline a word, sketch an arrow — and the drawing appears on screen as part of the video. It is perfect for tutorials and explanations, where pointing at something is worth a thousand words.

Chroma key and blending modes

Chroma key — the green-screen effect — lets you film in front of a plain green background and replace it with anything: a city, a beach, a cartoon world, or another video. KineMaster's chroma key tool lets you pick the colour to remove and fine-tune how much is taken away, so the edges of your subject stay clean. It works best with even lighting and a background that is one solid colour.

Blending modes change how a layer mixes with the video underneath it. Instead of simply covering what is below, a layer can lighten it, darken it, add its colours, or let the layers beneath show through in creative ways. A light-leak image set to a brightening blend, for example, adds a warm glow to a scene without hiding it. Blending modes are how editors create looks that feel cinematic rather than pasted on.

Chroma key and blending modes together open up the effects you see in polished videos online: a person standing in a place they never visited, a logo glowing over a dark scene, a double exposure of a face and a landscape. They take a little practice, but the tools are all in the app, and the results reward the effort.

Voice recording and audio tools

Good audio makes a video feel professional, and KineMaster treats audio seriously. You can record a voiceover straight into the timeline while your video plays — perfect for narrating a tutorial, explaining a project, or adding a personal message to a family film. Record in a quiet room, close to the phone, for the cleanest sound.

KineMaster also gives you control over every audio track. You can set the volume of each clip and each music track separately, fade music in at the start and out at the end, and duck the music under speech so voices stay clear. Sudden volume jumps are one of the fastest ways to lose a viewer, and a few minutes spent balancing audio makes a big difference.

The app includes a library of music and sound effects you can use in your videos, which saves you searching for tracks elsewhere. Pick music that fits the mood — gentle for a family film, energetic for a sports clip — and keep it low enough that it supports the video instead of competing with it.

The asset store

KineMaster's asset store is a built-in library of effects, transitions, stickers, fonts, music and templates, all made to drop straight into your projects. Instead of hunting for assets on the web, you browse the store inside the app, download what you like, and it appears in your editing tools ready to use.

The store is updated regularly, so there is usually something new to try — seasonal stickers, trending effects, fresh music. Browsing it is also a good way to get ideas: seeing a transition or an effect you had not thought of can change the direction of a whole video for the better.

A word of advice: download only what you need for the project in front of you. It is tempting to collect dozens of effects, but a video built from three well-chosen effects looks better than one built from thirty. Pick the assets that serve your story, and leave the rest in the store for next time.

Exporting and quality

When your edit is ready, exporting turns it into a video file you can share. KineMaster offers a range of resolutions, and for most sharing — messaging apps and social media — 1080p is the right choice: sharp on a phone screen, and a file size that uploads quickly. Higher resolutions are worth it if you filmed in high quality and want the best possible result, but they make larger files and longer exports.

Frame rate affects smoothness. Thirty frames per second is natural for everyday video. Sixty frames per second looks smoother and suits fast action — sport, dance, anything that moves quickly. If you filmed in slow motion, a higher frame rate keeps the slow parts looking clean instead of choppy.

Exporting a long or heavily layered video takes time, and your phone may warm up — that is normal, because rendering is hard work. Keep KineMaster open while it exports and let it finish. When it is done, the video appears in your gallery, ready to share wherever you like.

Tips for longer videos

KineMaster handles long videos well, but long projects need a different approach from short clips. The most important habit is to work in sections: build your video scene by scene — the opening, each main part, the ending — instead of trying to edit an hour of footage in one pass. Short sections are easier to preview, easier to fix, and easier to keep in your head.

The second habit is to keep your timeline tidy. Name your layers mentally as you add them, keep text on its own layers, and delete clips you have decided not to use instead of leaving them sitting on the timeline. A tidy timeline is a fast timeline, and it makes coming back to a project days later much easier.

Finally, export a draft early. Before your video is finished, export a rough version and watch it on a different screen — a television, a tablet, a friend's phone. Problems you cannot see on your editing screen jump out on another display: a clip that is too dark, a title that is too small, a section that drags. Fix what you find, then export the final version with confidence.

Questions people ask

Is KineMaster free?

Yes, KineMaster is free to download and use, and the core editing tools cost nothing. There is an optional paid plan that adds extra features and removes the watermark from exports, but you can edit and export videos without it.

How do I install the KineMaster APK?

Tap the Download button above — it opens the official Play Store listing for KineMaster. Tap Install there and the app installs itself. For an older version, the button on its row opens the APKPure version page, where you can download the file and open it to install it.

How do I use chroma key in KineMaster?

Film yourself in front of a plain green background with even lighting, then add the clip to your timeline and open the chroma key tool. Pick the green colour to remove it, then place any video or image on the layer underneath as your new background. Fine-tune the strength until the edges of your subject look clean.

Why does KineMaster export with a watermark?

The free version of KineMaster adds a watermark to exported videos. It goes away with the paid plan, and there is no setting in the free version that removes it. If the watermark bothers you, the paid plan is the only official way to export without it.

What Android version does KineMaster need?

KineMaster needs Android 8.0 or newer. You can check your Android version in Settings, under About phone.
