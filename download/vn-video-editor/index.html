What is VN Video Editor?

VN Video Editor is a free video editing app for Android phones and tablets. It is made for people who film on their phone and want to turn those clips into a finished video without a computer. You can trim clips, join them together, add music and text, and change the look of your video with filters. The app is popular with vloggers, students and anyone who posts short videos on social apps.

VN works offline once it is installed, so you can edit on a bus, in a park or anywhere without internet. Your projects stay on your phone until you choose to export them. The editing screen shows your clips on a timeline at the bottom and a preview at the top, so you can see every change as you make it. If you have never edited a video before, VN is a friendly place to start because the main tools are easy to find and the app explains itself as you go.

Editing without a watermark

A watermark is a small logo or piece of text that some free editors stamp onto every video you export. It sits in a corner of your video and tells viewers which app made it. Many free editors add one, and removing it usually means paying. VN is different: videos you export from VN carry no VN logo and no watermark at all, even though the app is free.

That matters if you post videos for other people to watch. A clean video looks more professional and keeps the focus on your content, not on the app that made it. It also matters if you edit videos for a shop, a class or a community page, where a watermark would look out of place. You can export as many watermark-free videos as you like, in the quality you choose, and share them anywhere.

Keyframes and curve speed

Keyframes sound technical, but the idea is simple. A keyframe is a point on your clip where you set a value, such as how big the picture is, where it sits on the screen, or how clear it looks. When you set two keyframes with different values, VN fills in the change between them smoothly. That is how you make a slow zoom into a face, a gentle pan across a landscape, or a smooth fade from one scene to the next.

Curve speed is VN's way of changing speed inside a single clip. Instead of one speed for the whole clip, you can make the start slow, the middle fast and the end slow again, following a curve you shape with your finger. It is the effect behind dramatic slow-motion moments in sports clips and fast, punchy cuts in travel videos. Try it on a short clip first: pick a moment worth slowing down, such as a jump or a smile, and let the rest of the clip play at normal speed.

Templates for a fast start

If you are short on time, VN's templates do most of the work for you. A template is a ready-made video style with the timing, transitions, music and text already set. You open a template, replace the sample clips with your own photos or videos, change the words if you like, and export. In a few minutes you can have a birthday video, a holiday recap or a product showcase that looks carefully made.

Templates are also a good way to learn. Open one you like and look at how it is built: where the cuts fall, how long each clip stays on screen, and how the music lines up with the pictures. You can change any part of a template, so use it as a starting point rather than a finished product. Swap in your own clips, try a different song, and adjust the text until the video feels like yours.

Music, text and stickers

Sound makes a big difference to a video. VN lets you add music from your own phone, adjust how loud it is, and fade it in at the start or out at the end. You can also record a voiceover over your clips, which is useful for explaining what is happening or telling a story. If your clip already has sound, you can lower the music under it so both can be heard.

Text and stickers add personality. You can put a title at the start, add captions that explain each scene, or drop in stickers for fun. Keep text short and large enough to read on a small phone screen, and give it enough time on screen for a viewer to finish reading it. A few well-placed words beat a screen full of text, and a simple style usually looks better than mixing many colours and fonts in one video.

The multi-track timeline in plain words

VN edits on a timeline with more than one track, which sounds complex but is really just layers stacked on top of each other. Think of it like a sandwich: your main video sits on the bottom layer, a second video or picture can sit on the layer above it, and text, stickers and music sit on their own layers too. Each layer plays at the same time, and you can move clips along their layer to change when they appear.

Extra tracks are what let you do picture-in-picture, where a small video plays inside a corner of the main video, or show a photo over a video while a caption runs across the bottom. You do not need to use every track. Start with one video track and one music track, and add a text track when you need a title. As you get comfortable, you can layer more, and VN keeps every track in sync while you work.

Exporting and quality settings

When your video is ready, exporting turns your project into a single video file you can share. VN lets you choose the resolution, which is how sharp the picture looks, and the frame rate, which is how smooth motion looks. For most social apps, 1080p is a good choice: it looks sharp on a phone screen and the file stays a reasonable size. Higher settings look better on a big screen but make much bigger files and take longer to export.

Before you export, check two things. First, make sure you have enough free space on your phone, because a long video at high quality can take hundreds of megabytes. Second, watch your video once through in the preview, checking the sound and the cuts. It is quicker to fix a mistake now than to export, notice it, and export again. Once you are happy, export and share the finished file straight to your favourite app.

Tips for your first project

Your first video does not need to be long or complicated. Pick one idea, such as a day out or a simple recipe, and keep it under a minute. Short videos are quicker to edit, easier to watch back, and more forgiving when something goes wrong. You can always make a longer video once you know the tools.

  • Shoot steady clips and hold each shot for a few seconds longer than you think you need
  • Cut the boring parts first, then add music and text
  • Keep your text short and check it reads well on a phone screen
  • Save your project often so you never lose your work
  • Export a short test clip before you export the full video

Questions people ask

Is VN Video Editor really free?

Yes. VN is free to download and the main editing tools cost nothing. Some extra templates and effects are paid options, but you can edit and export full videos, with no watermark, without paying anything.

Does VN add a watermark?

No. Videos you export from VN carry no VN logo and no watermark, even in the free version. Your finished video shows only your own clips, text and music.

How do I install the VN APK?

Tap the Download button above to open the official Play Store page, then tap Install. If you use a file from the APKPure archive instead, open the downloaded file and allow your phone to install it when asked, then follow the on-screen steps.

What is curve speed in VN?

Curve speed lets one clip change speed partway through — slow at the start, fast in the middle and slow again at the end, for example. You set speed points on a curve and VN smooths the change between them.

How do I use a template in VN?

Open VN, pick a template you like, then replace the sample clips and text with your own. Adjust the music and colours if you want, then export the video.

What Android version does VN need?

VN needs Android 7.0 or newer. You can check your version in Settings, under About phone.
