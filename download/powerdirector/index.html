What is PowerDirector, and who makes it?

PowerDirector is a video editor for Android made by CyberLink, a company that has built video software for decades. The phone version packs in many of the tools you would expect on a computer: a multi-track timeline, colour controls, speed effects, green screen and 4K export. It is one of the most capable editors you can run on a phone.

PowerDirector suits creators who want room to grow. You can start with simple trims and filters, then move on to layered edits, motion tracking and colour grading as your skills develop. The app is free to download, and its deeper tools are there whenever you are ready for them — you do not need to learn everything on day one.

CyberLink has made PowerDirector for computers since the early days of home video editing, and the phone app carries the same ideas in a smaller form. The timeline, the colour tools and the export settings all work the way an experienced editor would expect, which is why many creators use PowerDirector on both their phone and their computer. On a phone, it is best suited to people who enjoy editing and want the extra control.

Timeline editing in plain words

PowerDirector edits on a timeline: a long strip where your clips sit in the order they will play. You drag clips left or right to change their order, drag their edges to make them longer or shorter, and tap a clip to adjust it. The preview at the top shows the video as it will look, so you always know where you are.

The timeline has more than one track, which means you can stack things. Your main video sits on the bottom track, while a second video, a picture, text or a sticker can sit on tracks above it and appear at the same time. Music and voice sit on their own tracks underneath. Think of tracks as layers: each one adds something to the picture, and together they make the finished video.

4K export

4K is a very sharp picture: four times as many pixels as standard high definition. On a large television or a big monitor, a 4K video looks noticeably crisper, with fine detail in faces, text and landscapes. PowerDirector can export your finished video in 4K, provided your phone supports it and your original clips were filmed in high quality.

There is a trade-off. 4K files are much larger than 1080p files, they take longer to export, and they need more storage and more processing power. For videos watched on a phone screen, 1080p usually looks just as good. Save 4K for videos you plan to watch on a big screen, or for clips you may want to crop and reframe later, where the extra detail gives you room to work.

To make a true 4K video, your original clips should be filmed in high quality too. A low-quality clip exported at 4K will not gain detail it never had, so the result looks soft rather than sharp. Film in the highest quality your phone offers when you plan a 4K export, keep the phone steady, and give the export plenty of time. The finished file will be large, so check your storage before you start.

Chroma key and motion tracking

Chroma key is the green-screen effect you see in films and weather reports. You film a person in front of a plain green or blue background, and the app removes that colour so you can put a different picture or video behind them. PowerDirector includes chroma key, so you can film yourself in your room and appear to stand on a beach, in a city, or inside a cartoon world.

Motion tracking follows a moving object in your video. You pick something — a person, a ball, a car — and the app keeps track of where it goes. You can then attach text, a sticker or a blur to that object, and it will follow it around the screen. It is handy for pointing at something in a tutorial, hiding a face or a number plate, or adding a fun label that sticks to a moving subject.

Effects, transitions and titles

Effects change the mood of a clip. PowerDirector includes filters that warm up a scene or cool it down, effects that add glow or grain, and adjustments for brightness, contrast and colour. Try an effect on your clip, adjust its strength, and compare it with the original before you decide. The best effect is often a subtle one that improves the picture without drawing attention to itself.

Transitions join your clips together. A straight cut is the most common choice and suits most videos, while a fade or a wipe can mark a change of time or place. Titles work the same way: a clear title at the start, short captions during the video, and a closing line at the end. Keep text large and simple, and give viewers time to read it before it disappears.

Audio tools and voiceover

Sound deserves as much care as the picture. PowerDirector lets you add music tracks, adjust their volume, and fade them in and out. You can also record a voiceover while your video plays, which is useful for tutorials, reviews and narrated trips. The audio tools let you raise a quiet clip, lower background noise, and balance music against speech.

A good mix keeps the voice clear and the music in the background. If your video has speech, dip the music under it so every word is easy to hear. If your clips already have pleasant natural sound, such as birdsong or waves, consider keeping it and skipping the music. Listen to your finished video with headphones before you export, because speakers on a phone can hide problems that headphones reveal.

Exporting and quality settings

When your video is ready, exporting turns it into a file you can share. PowerDirector lets you choose the resolution, from standard sizes up to 4K, and the frame rate, which controls how smooth motion looks. For most sharing, 1080p at 30 frames per second is a safe choice. Use 4K only when you need the extra sharpness and have the storage and patience for a longer export.

Check two things before you export. First, watch the whole video in the preview and listen to the sound, because it is quicker to fix a mistake now than to export twice. Second, make sure you have enough free space: a long video at high quality can take a lot of room. Once the export finishes, the file is ready to share to any app you like.

Tips for editing on a phone

Editing on a phone is convenient, but the small screen rewards a tidy approach. Work in short sessions, save your project often, and keep your clips organised so you can find them quickly. A phone editor can do a great deal, but it works best when you plan your video before you start cutting.

  • Close other apps before a long export so the phone can focus on the job
  • Keep your phone charged or plugged in during exports
  • Edit in small steps and preview often
  • Use 1080p unless you have a clear reason to export in 4K
  • Back up finished videos so a full phone never costs you a project
