What CapCut is and who makes it

CapCut is a free video editor for your phone, made by Bytedance, the company behind TikTok. It started as a simple mobile editor and has grown into one of the most used video apps in the world, because it puts tools that used to need a computer into an app anyone can open and use. You can trim clips, add music and text, apply filters, and share the result straight to your favourite apps — all without paying anything to start.

What makes CapCut stand out is how much it gives away for free. The core editing tools — cutting, splitting, speed changes, filters, text and music — cost nothing, and you can export finished videos without a watermark on your own edits. There is an optional paid plan that adds extra effects, templates and cloud storage, but the free version is a complete editor on its own, and most people never need more.

CapCut is built for short video first: the kind of clips people post on TikTok, Instagram and YouTube Shorts. That shows in everything from its vertical timeline to its huge template library. But it works just as well for longer videos — a birthday montage, a holiday film, or a school project — because the same tools scale up to whatever you are making.

Trim, cut and arrange: the basics

Every edit in CapCut starts on the timeline, the strip at the bottom of the screen where your clips sit in order. Tap a clip to select it, then drag its edges to trim the start or the end. If a clip has a boring middle, split it into two pieces and delete the part you do not want. It sounds simple, and it is — but trimming well is most of what makes a video feel good to watch.

Arranging clips is just as easy. Long-press a clip and drag it to a new spot on the timeline, and the rest of the clips slide along to make room. This is how you build a story: put your best shot first, keep the pace moving, and cut anything that does not add something. A good rule is that if a clip makes you want to skip ahead, your viewers will feel the same way.

Speed is the third basic tool, and it is more powerful than it looks. Slow a clip down for a dramatic moment, or speed up a long process — a walk, a cook, a build — so it fits in a few seconds. CapCut lets you change the speed of a whole clip or just part of it, and the curve tool lets the speed ramp up and down smoothly instead of jumping.

Templates: a finished video in minutes

Templates are CapCut's most popular feature, and the reason many people install the app in the first place. A template is a ready-made edit: someone has already chosen the cuts, the effects, the transitions and the music. All you do is add your own photos or videos, and CapCut drops them into place. In a couple of minutes you have a video that looks like it took an hour to make.

To use one, open CapCut and tap Templates at the bottom. Browse the trending templates or search for a style you like — birthday, travel, funny, stylish — then tap Use template and pick your clips. CapCut shows you a preview before you commit, so you can see exactly how your photos will look in the finished video. If you do not like it, try another template with the same clips.

Templates are a great way to learn editing, too. When you see a template you like, look at how it is put together: where the cuts fall, when the music changes, how the effects arrive. Those are the same choices a human editor makes, and watching templates teaches you to make them yourself. Start with templates, then try building a video from scratch when you feel ready.

Filters, effects and text

Filters change the look of a whole clip in one tap — warmer, cooler, brighter, or styled like old film. CapCut has a large filter library, and you can adjust how strong a filter is, so a look can be subtle or bold. A light touch usually works best: pick one filter for the whole video so the clips feel like they belong together, rather than a different filter on every clip.

Effects are the moving parts: sparkles, glitches, zooms, light leaks, and dozens more. They sit on top of your clips and bring them to life, and CapCut's effects are easy to add — tap Effects, pick one, and drag it to cover the part of the clip where you want it. Used sparingly, effects make a video feel polished. Used on every second of every clip, they make it feel noisy, so save them for the moments that deserve a lift.

Text is the third layer, and CapCut treats it well. You can add titles, captions and labels in many fonts, change their colour and size, and animate them so they slide, fade or pop onto the screen. Text is useful for more than decoration: it tells viewers what is happening, adds jokes, and makes your video watchable with the sound off — which is how many people scroll.

Auto-captions: words on screen, done for you

Auto-captions are one of CapCut's best features, and they save a huge amount of time. Instead of typing out everything said in your video by hand, CapCut listens to the audio and writes the words on screen for you, timed to match the speech. You can then fix any mistakes, change the style, and choose how the words appear — all at once, or one word at a time as they are spoken.

Captions matter more than most people realise. Many people watch videos with the sound off — on a bus, in bed, or in a quiet room — and captions are the only way they can follow what is being said. Captions also help viewers who speak your language as a second language, and they make your video easier to find, because the words on screen are words people can search for.

To add them, open your project, tap Text, then Auto captions, and pick the language of the audio. CapCut generates the captions in a few moments. Read through them before you export — automatic captions are good, but names and unusual words sometimes come out wrong, and a quick check keeps your video looking professional.

Your first video, step by step

If you have never edited a video before, CapCut is a kind and forgiving place to start. You do not need a plan or a script — a few clips from your camera roll are enough. The steps below take you from nothing to a finished video in about ten minutes.

  • Open CapCut and tap New project, then pick the clips or photos you want to use.
  • Trim each clip by dragging its edges, and delete anything boring.
  • Tap Audio to add music, or Text to add a title at the start.
  • Try one filter on the whole video so the clips match.
  • Preview the video from the start and fix anything that feels off.
  • Tap Export, pick your quality, and share the finished video.

Do not worry about making it perfect. Your first video is for learning, and every editor's first videos are rough. The important thing is to finish one — exporting a finished video, even a short one, teaches you more than hours of watching tutorials. Make the next one a little better, and the one after that better still.

Exporting and picking the right quality

When your video is ready, tap Export and CapCut will render it into a file you can share. The export screen asks you to pick a resolution and a frame rate. For most sharing — messaging apps, social media, family groups — 1080p is the sweet spot: it looks sharp on a phone screen and the file stays a reasonable size. CapCut can export up to 4K at 60 frames per second, which is worth using if you filmed in 4K and want the very best quality, but the files are much larger and take longer to upload.

Frame rate is about smoothness. Thirty frames per second looks natural for everyday video. Sixty frames per second looks smoother, and it is the right choice for fast action — sports, dancing, anything that moves quickly. Higher frame rates also make slow-motion clips look better, because there are more frames to stretch.

Exporting takes a while on longer videos, and your phone may feel warm — that is normal, because rendering video is hard work for a phone. Keep CapCut open while it exports, and do not lock the screen, or the export may pause. Once it is done, CapCut offers to share the video straight to your apps, or you can find it in your gallery.

Tips for clean, smooth edits

The difference between an amateur video and a polished one is rarely the tools — it is the small habits. First, cut on movement: when something is moving in a clip, cut to the next clip mid-movement and the edit feels invisible. Second, keep your clips short. A video made of three-second clips feels alive; the same footage in ten-second clips feels slow.

Third, watch your audio. Music that is too loud drowns out voices, and sudden volume jumps are jarring. CapCut lets you set the volume of each clip and each music track separately — turn the music down under speech, and fade it in at the start and out at the end so it never starts or stops abruptly.

Finally, save your work as you go. CapCut keeps your projects in the app, so you can come back to a video later and keep editing. If you are working on something important — a birthday video, a gift, a school project — export a draft copy early, so you always have a finished version even if something goes wrong later.

Questions people ask

Is CapCut free?

Yes, CapCut is free to download and use, and the core editing tools cost nothing. There is an optional paid plan that adds extra effects, templates and cloud storage, but you can edit and export videos without it.

How do I install the CapCut APK?

Tap the Download button above — it opens the official Play Store listing for CapCut. Tap Install there and the app installs itself. For an older version, the button on its row opens the APKPure version page, where you can download the file and open it to install it.

Why does CapCut need so many permissions?

CapCut asks for the permissions its features need: the camera and microphone for recording, storage and photos for reading your clips, and the network for templates and sharing. You can deny a permission you do not use, and you can change any permission later in Settings under Apps.

How do I remove the CapCut watermark?

CapCut does not put a watermark on your own edits. What you may see is the short default ending clip CapCut adds to new projects — open the project, tap the ending clip on the timeline and delete it before you export. Some templates and effects are part of the paid plan, so swap them for a free one if you see a mark.

What Android version does CapCut need?

CapCut needs Android 6.0 or newer, which covers most phones made in the last several years. You can check your Android version in Settings, under About phone.

How do I use a template in CapCut?

Open CapCut, tap Templates, browse or search for one you like, tap Use template, then add your own photos or videos. The template sets the cuts, effects and music for you — you just swap in your clips and export.
