How Many Helium Balloons Would It Take to Lift That?

Type in a weight, pick a balloon size, and this calculator works out how many helium balloons it would take to lift it. It also shows the total helium volume and the net lift of a single balloon.

This is an estimate — real lift varies with temperature, altitude, inflation, and balloon weight. Never attempt to lift a person or animal with balloons.

The worked example

An 11-inch balloon holds about 11.4 litres of helium, with a gross lift of about 12.1 g and a net lift of about 9.1 g. Lifting a 70 kg person needs about 7,700 of them: 70,000 divided by 9.06 equals 7,726.3, rounded up to 7,727 balloons. The calculator above starts with exactly this example, so you can see the full working before you try your own weight.

How the lift works

A helium balloon floats for the same reason a beach ball floats in a swimming pool. This is Archimedes' principle: a balloon pushes aside a volume of air, and the lift equals the weight of that displaced air minus the weight of the helium inside and the weight of the balloon itself. Because helium is much lighter than air, the difference is positive, and the balloon rises.

The calculator treats each balloon as a sphere. Its volume is pi times the diameter cubed, divided by six. The gross lift is the volume times 1.056 grams per litre — that figure is the weight of air, 1.225 kg per cubic metre, minus the weight of helium, 0.169 kg per cubic metre, at 15 C and sea level. From the gross lift the calculator subtracts the balloon's own weight: the envelope weighs 2.5 g times the square of the diameter divided by 11, and the string weighs 0.5 g. What is left is the net lift per balloon, and the balloon count is the weight divided by that net lift, rounded up.

Fun things to try

The calculator is at its best with everyday objects. Try a phone, a cat, or a bicycle: type in the weight, pick a balloon size, and the answer appears on the spot. Switching balloon sizes for the same weight is the most surprising part — a bigger balloon holds far more helium than a slightly smaller one, so the count drops fast as the size goes up. The result is a fun way to picture a weight, and a reminder of just how light a single balloon's lift really is.

Questions people ask

How does a helium balloon lift anything?

A balloon floats because it pushes aside air that weighs more than the helium inside it. The difference is the lift, minus the weight of the balloon skin and the string.

How many balloons would lift a 70 kg person?

About 7,727 eleven-inch balloons, using the method on this page: 70,000 grams divided by 9.06 grams of net lift per balloon, rounded up. Never attempt to lift a person or an animal with balloons.

Why is the answer only an estimate?

Real lift varies with temperature, altitude, how fully each balloon is inflated, and how much each balloon skin weighs. The calculator uses one standard set of figures, so treat the result as a fun estimate, not an exact count.

Do bigger balloons lift much more?

Yes. A balloon's volume depends on the cube of its diameter, so a small increase in size makes a big difference to the lift. Try the same weight with different balloon sizes in the calculator to see the change.

What can I try in the calculator?

Anything you can weigh: a phone, a cat, a bicycle. Type in the weight and pick a balloon size, and the calculator works out the balloon count on the spot.
