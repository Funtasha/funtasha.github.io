Farewell Speech for a Colleague

Here are three complete farewell speech templates for a colleague — short, heartfelt, and funny — each under 250 words with fill-in blanks you can replace with your own details. Pick the one that suits the occasion, make it yours, and read the delivery tips before you stand up.

A farewell speech works best when it is short, warm, and specific. Say what the person meant to the team, name one memory, and wish them well — that is the whole recipe. Each template below is ready to use as it stands, and the square brackets show exactly where your own details go.

The short farewell speech

Use this one when time is tight or several people are speaking.

Thank you all for coming. I want to say a few words about [Name], who is leaving us after [number] years with the team. [Name] has been the person we all turned to when things got busy, the one who kept us laughing through long weeks, and the colleague who made this place feel like more than just a workplace. I will miss our [shared habit, for example Friday coffee breaks], and I know I am not the only one. [Name], thank you for everything you have given this team. We are sad to see you go, but we are excited for what comes next for you. Please keep in touch, and remember that you will always have friends here. Let us raise a glass to [Name] — to new adventures, and to a colleague we will not forget.

About 139 words — roughly one minute to read aloud.

The heartfelt farewell speech

Use this one when you have worked closely together and want to say something real.

Good [morning/afternoon], everyone. When I first met [Name], I had no idea how much they would change this team — or how much I would enjoy working beside them. Over [number] years, [Name] has taught me what a good colleague looks like: someone who answers the difficult email, stays late when the project needs it, and still finds time to ask how your weekend went. I will always remember [a memory, for example the week we finished the big project together], because that was [Name] at their best — calm, kind, and determined. [Name], you leave behind a team that is better because of you, and friends who will miss you every day. Wherever you go next, take our thanks with you, and know that you will always be welcome here. We wish you every success, and we will be cheering for you from here.

About 145 words — roughly one minute to read aloud.

The funny farewell speech

Use this one when the mood is light and the colleague enjoys a joke.

Thank you, everyone, for gathering to say goodbye to [Name] — or, as I like to think of it, to celebrate the fact that somebody finally escaped the [something funny about the office, for example the printer that never works]. In [number] years here, [Name] has become famous for three things: [a habit, for example arriving with coffee for everyone], [another habit, for example knowing every shortcut in the building], and [a third habit, for example never losing a single meeting note]. We will miss all of it, though we will not miss [a gentle joke, for example their playlist on a Friday afternoon]. [Name], thank you for the laughs, the hard work, and the patience. Your new colleagues have no idea how lucky they are — and when they find out, tell them we said hello. Good luck, keep in touch, and remember that your desk will always be here, probably with your name still on it.

About 158 words — roughly one minute to read aloud.

These templates are starting points, not scripts — the best farewell speech carries a detail only you could give, so replace every bracket with something real before you speak.

Delivery tips

  • Practise out loud twice, standing up if you can — a speech that sounds fine in your head can trip you up when you say it for real.
  • Speak slowly and look up from your notes often; people listen to your face as much as your words.
  • Keep it short: two to three minutes is plenty for a farewell speech, and a short speech that lands is better than a long one that wanders.
  • If your voice shakes, pause, take a breath, and carry on — nobody minds, and it shows you care.
  • End clearly with a thank-you or a toast, so the audience knows when to clap.

How to personalize your speech

Start with the template that suits the occasion, then replace every square bracket with a real detail: the person's name, how long they have been here, a habit everyone knows, and one memory you share. One true detail makes the whole speech sound like yours, so pick the memory that makes you smile when you think of it. Read the speech out loud once with your details in place, and cut any line that does not sound like something you would actually say.

Questions people ask

How long should a farewell speech for a colleague be?

Two to three minutes is plenty — long enough to say what the person meant to the team and wish them well, and short enough to hold the room.

What should I say in a farewell speech?

Say what the person meant to the team, name one memory you share, thank them, and wish them well — that is the whole recipe, and it always works.

Should a farewell speech be funny or serious?

Either works, and the person leaving usually decides — a light, funny speech suits a relaxed colleague, while a heartfelt one suits someone you worked closely with.

What if I get nervous while speaking?

Speak slowly, pause when you need to, and look up from your notes often — a pause feels much longer to you than to the audience, and nobody minds it.

Can I use these templates for a leaving card instead?

Yes — the short template works well in a card, especially with one personal memory added in your own words.
