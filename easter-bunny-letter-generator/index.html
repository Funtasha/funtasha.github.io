How the letter generator works

Type in the child's name — that is the only part you must fill in. Then add as much or as little as you like: their age, their town or village, one good thing they did this year, and their favourite thing about Easter. Read the letter in the preview box. If you like it, press Copy letter to copy it, or Print letter to print it straight from your browser. Print it on nice paper if you have it, fold it up, and hide it with the eggs on Easter morning. The letter is written in a warm, playful voice that suits children of all ages — no scares, no tricks, just a bunny who is very pleased to visit.

What makes a letter feel personal

A letter feels personal when it could only have been written for one child. The name does most of the work, but the small details do the rest: the town where they live, the good thing they did, the thing they love most about Easter. When a child reads their own name beside something they actually did, the letter stops being a piece of paper and becomes a little bit of magic.

That is why the good deed line matters more than any other box on the page. A letter that says "I heard you helped Grandma in the garden" will be remembered long after a letter that says "you have been a good child". Use something real, something recent, and something the child will recognise as their own. If you cannot think of one, ask yourself what they did last week that made you smile — that is the line to use.

Ideas for the good deed line

The good deed does not need to be grand. Small, everyday kindnesses are the ones children are proudest of, because they are the ones they chose to do themselves. Think about the last few months and pick the moment that stands out.

Here are a few ideas to get you started:

  • Helped a younger brother or sister with their reading.
  • Shared their toys without being asked.
  • Helped Grandma in the garden, or Grandpa with the shopping.
  • Looked after a friend who was feeling sad at school.
  • Kept their room tidy all week, without a single reminder.
  • Was brave at the dentist, or patient while waiting for news.

Write it in your own words, in a few words if you can. The generator fits your line into the letter as it stands, so keep it short and simple.

Letters for different ages

The same letter works for many ages, but the details change how it lands. For a little one aged 2 to 4, keep it simple: their name, their favourite thing, and a short line about the eggs. Read it aloud to them on Easter morning, and let them hold the paper — the feel of it matters as much as the words.

For a child aged 5 to 8, the good deed line is the star of the show. They are old enough to read the letter themselves, or to follow along as you read it, and young enough to believe every word. For older children aged 9 to 12, the letter works best with a wink: they may suspect who really wrote it, but they will enjoy the joke and keep the letter anyway. A letter for an older child can be longer and a little funnier — the generator keeps the tone playful for exactly that reason.

Printing and hiding the letter

Print the letter the night before Easter, so Easter morning runs smoothly. Plain paper works fine, but coloured paper or a little drawing in the margin makes it feel special. Fold the letter in half, or roll it up and tie it with a ribbon if you want a flourish. You can also copy the letter by hand onto a card, if your handwriting is neater than the printer's.

Hide the letter where the child will find it with the eggs: tucked into the Easter basket, leaning against the front door, or propped up beside the biggest egg. Some families leave it with a few chocolate footprints — a trail of small eggs leading from the door to the basket. Wherever you put it, make sure it is the first thing they see, so the letter sets the scene for the whole morning.

The Easter Bunny story

The Easter Bunny is a springtime visitor who brings eggs and treats to children on Easter morning. The story is old and simple: while the children sleep, the bunny hops from house to house, hiding eggs in gardens and homes for them to find when they wake. Eggs stand for new life, which is why they belong to a spring festival, and the bunny — quick, soft and full of energy — is spring itself in animal form.

Every family tells the story a little differently, and that is part of the fun. Some say the bunny paints the eggs, some say it hides chocolate ones, and some say it leaves a letter as well. The generator's letter follows the friendliest version of the story: a kind bunny who notices good deeds, loves spring flowers, and is always delighted to visit. You can add your own family details to the letter after you print it, to make the story yours.

Making Easter morning special

Easter morning is about the first ten minutes. The children wake up, find the letter, and the hunt begins. Keep it simple: the letter, the basket, and a handful of eggs hidden in easy spots to start with. A big breakfast can wait — the excitement will not.

After the hunt, slow the morning down. Read the letter aloud together, count the eggs, and let the children tell you about their hardest find. Take a photo of them holding the letter — it is the kind of photo that gets brought out years later. And keep the letter itself: in a drawer, in a scrapbook, or pinned to the fridge for a week. A letter from the Easter Bunny is a small thing, but it is the kind of small thing childhood is made of.

Only use a child's name in a letter with a parent or guardian's permission, and never share a letter that names a child in public. The letter is for one family, one morning, and one very lucky child.

Questions people ask

What do I need to write a letter?

Only the child's name. Everything else — age, town, a good deed and a favourite thing — is optional, but each detail you add makes the letter feel more personal.

Can I print the letter?

Yes. Press Print letter after the letter is written, and it prints from your browser. You can print it on paper or save it as a file, and it looks lovely on coloured paper.

What if the child cannot read yet?

Read the letter aloud to them on Easter morning and let them hold the paper. Little ones love hearing their name in a letter, even if they cannot read the words themselves.

Can I change the letter after it is written?

Yes. Copy the letter and edit it in any text app, or copy it out by hand onto a card. Add a line of your own, a drawing, or a family joke to make it yours.

Is the letter suitable for older children?

Yes. The letter is written in a playful tone that works for younger children and amuses older ones. Children aged 9 to 12 often enjoy the joke and keep the letter anyway.

When should I hide the letter?

Hide it the night before Easter, with the eggs, so it is waiting when the children wake up. Tuck it into the Easter basket or lean it against the front door.
