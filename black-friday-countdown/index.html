What Black Friday is

Black Friday is the day after Thanksgiving in the United States, and it marks the start of the holiday shopping season. Shops open early, websites run their biggest sales of the year, and millions of people go looking for a bargain — some for gifts, some for things they have been waiting all year to buy at a lower price. Over the years the day has grown from a single morning of queues into a whole weekend of deals, stretching from the Friday through to Cyber Monday.

Where does the name come from? The documented origin is Philadelphia in the 1950s and 1960s, where police used the term for the chaotic crowds and heavy traffic that filled the city the day after Thanksgiving, as shoppers poured in and football fans arrived for the weekend game. It was not a cheerful name at first — it described a long, difficult day for everyone working the streets. A second story you will often hear is that shops move from being in the red (making a loss) to being in the black (making a profit) on this day. That is the popular retelling, and it came later; the Philadelphia story is the one the records support.

Whatever the name's history, the day itself is simple: it is the Friday after Thanksgiving, and it is the busiest shopping day of the year in the United States. The countdown above tells you exactly how long is left until the next one.

When Black Friday falls in 2026 — and why the date moves

Black Friday 2026 falls on Friday, 27 November 2026. Thanksgiving that year is Thursday, 26 November, and Black Friday is always the day after it. The next one after that, Black Friday 2027, falls on Friday, 26 November 2027.

The date moves each year because Thanksgiving itself moves. Thanksgiving is not a fixed date: it is the fourth Thursday of November. That Thursday can land anywhere from 22 November to 28 November, depending on where the Thursdays fall in a given year. Black Friday, one day later, therefore always falls somewhere between 23 and 29 November. To find it for any year, open a calendar to November, find the fourth Thursday, and take the next day.

That moving date is exactly why a countdown helps. The day is easy to miss in a busy November — Thanksgiving takes the attention, and Black Friday arrives the very next morning. The timer at the top of this page does the working out for you, and once one Black Friday has passed it rolls forward to the next one automatically.

Prepare before the day: wishlist and budget

The people who get the most out of Black Friday are the ones who decide what they want before the day arrives. Sales are designed to make you buy quickly, and quick decisions are where the regrets live. A short list made in advance turns a hectic day into a simple errand.

  • Make a wishlist. Write down the things you actually need or have been meaning to buy — gifts, household items, the coat you have been putting off. Keep the list short enough to finish.
  • Set a total budget. Decide the most you will spend across the whole day, before you see a single deal. A number decided calmly is worth more than any discount.
  • Put the list in order. The thing you want most goes first. If it sells out or the price disappoints, you still have the rest of the list to work through.
  • Decide what you will not buy. A deal on something you did not want is not a saving. If it was not on the list, it can wait.

With a list and a budget in hand, the day stops being a hunt and starts being a plan. You know what you are looking for, you know what you will spend, and you can walk away from everything else.

Track prices before the day

A discount only means something if you know what the price was before the sale. The single most useful thing you can do in the weeks before Black Friday is to note the ordinary prices of the things on your wishlist. Write them down — the item, the shop, the price, and the date you saw it. A simple note on your phone is enough.

Why does this matter? Because a sale price is only a bargain when it is lower than the price the item actually sold at. Some shops raise a price shortly before a sale and then cut it back, so the discount looks bigger than it is. Your own notes are the defence: if the Black Friday price is the same as the price you wrote down two weeks ago, it is not a deal, whatever the label says.

Price history is also worth checking where a shop or a price comparison site shows it. A price that has been steady for months and then drops on Black Friday is a genuine cut. A price that jumped up last week and then fell back is not. Ten minutes of checking before the day can save you from paying full price while believing you got a bargain.

Online or in store?

Black Friday used to mean queues outside shops before dawn. These days most of the action is online, and the two ways of shopping suit different people and different purchases.

Shopping online is calmer and easier to compare. You can check the same item at several shops in minutes, read the delivery terms, and walk away from a basket without any pressure. The risks are delivery times — sale week is the busiest time of the year for parcels — and the temptation to keep adding things because the basket never gets heavy. If you shop online, check the delivery date before you pay, and remember that a deal is not a deal if it arrives after you need it.

Shopping in store suits things you want to see, touch or try on, and things you want to take home the same day. The trade-off is the crowd and the clock: popular items can sell out, and the atmosphere is designed to hurry you. If you go out, take your list, go early if you can, and give yourself permission to leave empty-handed. The same shops usually have the same offers online, and the weekend is long.

Whichever way you shop, the rules are the same: know your list, know your budget, and know the price you wrote down before the sale.

How to spot a real deal

Not every Black Friday price is a bargain, and the difference between a real deal and a dressed-up ordinary price is usually visible if you look for it.

  • Compare with the price you noted. Your own notes from before the sale are the best test. A real deal beats the price you saw two weeks ago.
  • Ignore the percentage alone. A big percentage off a price that was never charged is not a saving. The only number that matters is what you actually pay.
  • Check more than one shop. The same item often appears at several shops at different prices, even on Black Friday. A quick comparison takes minutes.
  • Read the small print. Some sale prices apply only to a particular colour, size or model, or only while a small stock lasts. Make sure the deal is for the item you actually want.
  • Watch the total, not the item. Delivery charges, extras and add-ons can quietly eat a discount. Work out what you pay in total before you decide.

If a deal passes those checks, it is a real one — and you can buy it knowing the saving is genuine. If it fails them, the best move is the simplest one: close the page and move on to the next item on your list.

After Black Friday: Cyber Monday and beyond

Black Friday is not the end of the sales. The Monday after Thanksgiving weekend is Cyber Monday, and in 2026 it falls on Monday, 30 November. It began as the online answer to Black Friday, and it is still the day when online shops run their own round of deals — often on the same kinds of items, sometimes on different ones.

That matters for two reasons. First, if you miss something on Friday, Monday is a second chance: the same item may appear again, sometimes at a similar price. Second, it means there is no need to panic-buy. The weekend is long, and the shops want your custom on Monday just as much as on Friday.

After Cyber Monday the sales quieten down, but the season does not end: the weeks before Christmas bring their own offers, and the January sales follow. So if Black Friday passes without the deal you wanted, it is not the last chance — it is the first of several. Keep your list, keep your budget, and the right price will come around.

Questions people ask

When is Black Friday 2026?

Black Friday 2026 falls on Friday, 27 November 2026, the day after Thanksgiving (Thursday, 26 November). The countdown at the top of this page shows the time left until it begins.

How many days until Black Friday?

The countdown at the top of this page shows the exact days, hours, minutes and seconds left until Black Friday begins at midnight local time. Once the day has passed, it rolls forward and counts down to the next Black Friday instead.

Is Black Friday always the day after Thanksgiving?

Yes. Black Friday is defined as the day after Thanksgiving in the United States, which is the fourth Thursday of November. Because Thanksgiving moves, Black Friday always falls somewhere between 23 and 29 November.

What time do Black Friday sales start?

It depends on the shop. Many online sales begin days or even weeks before the day itself, while in-store sales typically start early on the Friday morning. Check the shop you plan to buy from, because there is no single start time for all stores.

When does Black Friday end?

Most Black Friday offers run through the Friday itself, and many shops extend them across the weekend until Cyber Monday. Some deals end when stock runs out, so popular items can disappear well before the day is over.

What comes after Black Friday (Cyber Monday)?

Cyber Monday comes next — the Monday after Thanksgiving weekend. In 2026 it falls on Monday, 30 November. It began as the online counterpart to Black Friday, and online shops still run their own round of deals that day.
