What a free tier actually buys you

Searches for AI video generation are up 344% year on year, and the phrase "AI video generator free" alone is searched about 688,000 times a month as of November 2026. That demand has filled the internet with pages that promise a free video generator and deliver nothing — type a prompt, wait, and find that the video only appears after you pay. This page does the opposite: every tool below gives you real clips for free, and every limit is stated next to the gift.

A free tier buys you short clips, not long videos. Most free generators make clips of 5 to 15 seconds, and that is by design: video costs far more computer power to make than a picture, so the free allowance is measured in credits, tokens, or clips per day. Google Flow gives 50 free credits a day, which works out as five 8-second clips made with its Veo 3.1 Lite model. Google Vids gives 60 seconds of AI clips a month on a personal account, which Google phrases as up to three 15-second videos a month. Hailuo gives new users a one-time welcome pack of credits that expires three days after it is granted, with no recurring daily credits. Pika's free plan has no monthly credits at all — you buy credit packs or a paid plan to generate. Leonardo gives 150 tokens a day without asking for a credit card. Those are real amounts, and they are enough to make something — as long as you know what they are.

The second thing a free tier buys you is a watermark, on most tools. Kling, Runway, and PixVerse all stamp their free exports, and that stamp only comes off when you pay. A few tools break the pattern: Flow puts only an invisible SynthID mark on most free clips, with no visible watermark, and Wan 2.2, being open source software that runs on your own computer, stamps nothing at all. The table below shows the watermark for every tool, because it is usually the detail that decides whether a free clip is usable.

The third thing to know is that free does not mean unlimited, and it does not mean forever. Runway's free offer is a one-time trial of 125 credits — when they are gone, they are gone. PixVerse starts you with 60 credits and adds 30 a day. HeyGen gives 3 avatar videos a month. Read each row of the table as a small budget: so many clips, so many seconds, with or without a mark, and then the day or the month resets it — or it does not.

If you make one short clip a day, Flow covers it without costing anything, and the clips look clean enough to post. For no watermark at all, the shortlist is Flow, Google Vids, Wan 2.2, and Leonardo. Flow hides only an invisible SynthID mark. Vids gives 6 free clips a month with no visible watermark. The watermark truth

A watermark is the price of a free tier, not a mistake and not a bug. Video generation is expensive to run, and the companies that offer it for free need something in return. The watermark is that something: every free clip you post advertises the tool that made it. That is why Kling, Runway, and PixVerse keep watermark-free exports for paying users — the watermark is the line between the free tier and the paid one.

Not all marks are equal. A visible watermark sits on the picture where everyone can see it, usually in a corner. An invisible mark, like the SynthID mark on Flow clips, is hidden in the file itself: viewers see a clean clip, while software can still tell that the clip was made by AI. And a tool with no mark at all, like Wan 2.2, leaves nothing — which is why open source is the only route to a truly unmarked free clip on your own terms.

If a watermark ruins a clip, the honest fix is to re-render the clip on a tool that does not stamp it, or to accept the mark and keep the clip for practice. Cropping a watermark out, or covering it with a sticker, is not advised if it breaks the tool's terms — and most free tiers do forbid it. A watermark is a small price for a free clip, and removing it the wrong way can cost you the account that gives you the free clips.

Sora is gone — do not chase it

If you came here looking for Sora, here is the plain truth: Sora is dead. OpenAI announced the shutdown on 24 March 2026. The Sora app and sora.com shut down on 26 April 2026, and the Sora API shut down on 24 September 2026. No successor was named. Videos that people had already exported remain theirs, but there is no Sora to open, no Sora to sign up for, and no Sora free tier.

That matters, since Sora's name still appears all over the internet, and many of those mentions are out of date or deliberately misleading. Any site that offers a Sora download, a Sora free trial, or a Sora account in November 2026 is not offering the real thing — there is no real thing left to offer. The same goes for any page that lists Sora among working free tools: it is either old, or it is a trap.

The tools on this page are the working alternatives, and they are honest about what they give. Flow, Kling, PixVerse, Runway, Pika, Hailuo, Leonardo, HeyGen, Vids, Wan 2.2, and CapCut all exist, all work, and all give you something for free today. Pick one of them, and leave Sora in the past where it belongs.

How to get the most from free credits

Free credits are a small budget, and small budgets reward planning. The single biggest waste is generating the same clip twice because the prompt was vague. Write the prompt carefully first — subject, action, camera move, light, style — and you will get a usable clip on the first try far more often. The prompt builder on the text-to-video page does this work for you, and it costs nothing to use.

Second, match the tool to the clip. Use Flow's daily credits for the clips that must look clean, because they carry no visible watermark. Use Kling for shots with people in them, and PixVerse when the clip needs an effect. Use Vids for short explainers, where 60 seconds of clips a month is plenty. Save Runway's one-time 125 credits for a day when you have a clear plan, because once they are spent, there is no refill.

Third, make short clips on purpose. A 5-second clip that works is worth more than a 15-second clip that falls apart halfway through. Free generators are at their best with one subject, one action, and one camera move per clip. Plan a longer video as a row of short clips, stitched together in CapCut with narration over the top, and your free credits will stretch much further than they would on one long, ambitious generation.
