Your prediction

How the predictor works

The predictor is a simple game in three steps. First, pick the team you think will finish first and the team you think will finish second in each of the two groups. Second, pick a winner for each semi-final from those four teams. Third, pick your champion from the two semi-final winners. Tap the button, and you will see your predicted path to the final written out in full.

The predictor checks your picks as you go. The semi-final lists only fill up once you have picked two different teams in each group, and the champion list only appears once you have picked two different semi-final winners. If a pick is missing or repeated, the predictor tells you what to fix instead of showing a broken result.

Nothing here is a forecast. The predictor does not know who will win, and neither does anyone else: that is what makes a World Cup worth watching. Your prediction is a way to follow the tournament with a personal stake in it, and to compare notes with friends as the real results come in.

The two groups

The group stage has twelve teams in two groups of six. Group A is India, Australia, Pakistan, Afghanistan, Zimbabwe, and Qualifier A, the winner of the Super Series. Group B is New Zealand, South Africa, Sri Lanka, England, Bangladesh, and Qualifier B. Every team plays the other five teams in its group once.

Group A is full of history. India and Australia met in the 2023 final, India and Pakistan is the biggest rivalry in the sport, and Afghanistan have beaten the best teams in the world in recent tournaments. Zimbabwe play in front of home crowds at Harare and Bulawayo, and Qualifier A arrives with nothing to lose.

Group B is just as open. South Africa play at home, New Zealand have reached the final in two of the last three tournaments, and England won the whole thing in 2019. Sri Lanka and Bangladesh both have the players to beat anyone on their day, and Qualifier B will be dangerous precisely because nobody knows much about them yet.

From groups to the Super 7

The predictor simplifies the middle of the tournament, so here is how the real format works. After the group stage, the top three teams in each group go through to the Super 7, joined by the best fourth-placed team across the two groups. Those seven teams play a single round robin of twenty-one matches, and the top four reach the semi-finals.

The predictor asks only for your top two in each group, because those are the teams most likely to reach the last four, and because a game should be quick to play. In the real tournament, a team that finishes third in its group can still win the whole thing, and the best fourth-placed team rule means the group stage stays alive until the very last round.

When you compare your prediction with the real results, remember the Super 7. If one of your picks stumbles in the groups but recovers in the Super 7, they can still reach the semi-finals, and your prediction can still come true. The long format rewards teams that improve as the tournament goes on.

The semi-finals

The two semi-finals are played on consecutive days in November 2027. The first semi-final is in Cape Town on Wednesday 17 November, at Newlands, one of the most beautiful grounds in world cricket, with Table Mountain behind it. The second semi-final is in Centurion on Thursday 18 November, at SuperSport Park, a ground famous for high scores and fast outfields.

Which teams play in which semi-final depends on where they finish in the Super 7, so the predictor does not assign your teams to a particular semi-final: you simply pick the two winners. In the real tournament, the pairings follow the standings, and the team that finishes first in the Super 7 usually gets the kinder draw.

Semi-finals are where World Cups are won and lost. The 2019 semi-finals gave us England's win over Australia and New Zealand's famous win over India, and the 2023 semi-finals gave us India's win over New Zealand and Australia's win over South Africa. Expect the 2027 semi-finals to be just as tense.

The final

The final is on Sunday 21 November 2027, the last day of a seven-week tournament. Two teams, one match, fifty overs each, and a trophy that only six nations have ever lifted. The final is the match every prediction is really about: your champion pick is the name you will be shouting at the screen if they make it that far.

Recent finals show how unpredictable the last day can be. In 2023, Australia beat India in Ahmedabad in front of a crowd that had come expecting an Indian win. In 2019, the final at Lord's was tied, and England won on a boundary count after a super over. In 2015, Australia beat New Zealand in Melbourne, and in 2011, India beat Sri Lanka in Mumbai. Home advantage helps, but it guarantees nothing.

When you pick your champion, think about the whole journey, not just the final. A team must survive the groups, the Super 7, and a semi-final to reach the last day, and the team that looks strongest in October is not always the team that lifts the trophy in November.

Picking like a fan

There is no secret formula for a good prediction, but a few habits help. First, look at balance: a team with strong batting and strong bowling is safer than a team that depends on one star. Second, think about conditions: the grounds in South Africa, Zimbabwe, and Namibia suit different kinds of cricket, and the teams that adapt quickest usually go furthest.

Third, remember home advantage. South Africa and Zimbabwe play in front of their own crowds, on grounds they know well, and that matters in a long tournament. Fourth, do not ignore the underdogs: Afghanistan, Bangladesh, and the two qualifiers have all beaten bigger teams in recent years, and a World Cup always produces at least one shock.

Finally, trust your heart a little. A prediction you believe in is more fun to follow than a prediction copied from someone else, even if it turns out wrong. Pick the team you want to win, follow them through the tournament, and enjoy the ride.

Play with friends

A predictor is best played in a group. Ask each friend to make their own prediction before the tournament starts, write the picks down, and compare them as the results come in. Award a point for each correct group pick, a point for each correct semi-final winner, and two points for the champion, and see who finishes top when the final is over.

Keep the game friendly and free. No money, no stakes, no arguments: the prize can be choosing the snacks for the final, or simply the honour of being right. The fun is in the arguing beforehand and the gloating afterwards, not in anything that costs money.

Revisit your predictions as the tournament unfolds. After the group stage, check how many of your four picks made it through. After the Super 7, check whether your semi-final winners are still alive. By the time the final arrives, you will know whether your prediction was inspired, lucky, or gloriously wrong.

This predictor is a game, not a forecast. It uses the published groups only; no individual match fixtures are listed here, and the real semi-finalists depend on the results.

Questions people ask

Then your prediction is wrong, and that is part of the fun. Compare your picks with the real results as the tournament goes on, and see how far your champion actually gets.

Yes. Group A is India, Australia, Pakistan, Afghanistan, Zimbabwe, and Qualifier A, and Group B is New Zealand, South Africa, Sri Lanka, England, Bangladesh, and Qualifier B, as published for the 2027 tournament.

When are the semi-finals?

The first semi-final is in Cape Town on 17 November 2027, and the second is in Centurion on 18 November 2027. The final follows on 21 November 2027.

Yes. Change any pick and tap the button again to see your new predicted path. The predictor always uses your latest picks.
