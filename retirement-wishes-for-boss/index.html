These wishes are starting points — a real achievement or a genuine thank-you makes any message stronger, so add one detail of your own before you send it.

How to personalize your message

A retirement message to a boss works best when it names something real. Add one achievement, one quality you admired, or one time their guidance helped you, and keep the tone warm and respectful. If you worked closely together, a short personal memory is welcome; if you knew them less well, a simple thank-you and a happy wish is perfect. Sign with your name and, if you like, your team.

Questions people ask

What should I write to a retiring boss?

Thank them for their leadership, name something they did well, and wish them a happy retirement — respectful and sincere beats long and formal.

How formal should a retirement message to a boss be?

Match the relationship you had — a warm, respectful tone suits most bosses, and a short personal memory makes it feel genuine.

Should I mention their achievements?

Yes, one or two real achievements mean more than general praise — mention a project, a value they held, or the team they built.

Can a retirement message to a boss be funny?

A gentle, light-hearted line is fine if your boss enjoys humour — keep it warm, never teasing, and never about their age.

Is it better to write a card or send a message?

A handwritten card feels more personal for a boss's retirement, but a thoughtful message sent on their last day is also welcome.
