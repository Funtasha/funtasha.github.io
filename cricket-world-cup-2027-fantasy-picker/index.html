How the picker works

The picker is simple. You have a budget of 120 points and a pool of twenty-four famous players, each with a role, a team, and a points cost. Tick the boxes next to the eleven players you want, watch the running total as you go, and tap the button to check your team. If you have eleven players within budget, your team is ready.

The points are game points, not money. The biggest stars cost twelve points, the next group costs eleven, most players cost ten, and the value picks cost nine. You cannot pick all the stars: the eleven most expensive players in the pool cost 122 points between them, which is two over budget, so every team needs at least one smart saving somewhere.

The cheapest possible team costs 106 points, so there is room to build in different ways. You can spend heavily on batting and save on bowling, or spread your points evenly and pick a balanced side. The live counter shows your picks and your points as you go, so you always know where you stand.

Building a balanced team

A good fantasy team needs balance. In real cricket, a team of eleven usually has five or six batters, one wicketkeeper, and four or five bowlers, with one or two all-rounders who can do both jobs. The same balance works here: a team of eleven batters would score plenty of runs and take no wickets, and a team of eleven bowlers would take wickets and score very few.

Start with the roles, not the names. Pick a wicketkeeper first, because there are only four in the pool and they go quickly in a group game. Then pick your bowlers, because a fantasy team needs at least four, and then fill the batting around them. All-rounders are the most flexible picks: they bat and bowl, so they cover two jobs at once.

Check the role breakdown when you finish. A team with five batters, one wicketkeeper, three bowlers, and two all-rounders is a classic shape, but there is no single right answer. If your team has a clear weakness, swap one player and see whether the balance improves.

Gill, Smith, and Head cost ten each, which makes them the value batting picks: strong players at a lower price, leaving points for your bowlers.

Bowlers, keepers, and all-rounders

Bumrah costs twelve points, Rashid and Cummins cost eleven, and the other three cost ten each. A mix of fast bowling and spin gives your team variety, which matters on different grounds.

The four wicketkeepers are Jos Buttler of England, KL Rahul of India, Heinrich Klaasen of South Africa, and Alex Carey of Australia. Buttler costs eleven points, Rahul and Klaasen cost ten, and Carey costs nine, which makes him the cheapest keeper in the game. Every team needs at least one keeper, and most teams pick exactly one.

The six all-rounders are Hardik Pandya and Ravindra Jadeja of India, Glenn Maxwell and Mitchell Marsh of Australia, Aiden Markram of South Africa, and Wanindu Hasaranga of Sri Lanka. Pandya, Maxwell, and Jadeja cost ten points each, and Marsh, Markram, and Hasaranga cost nine, which makes the last three the cheapest players in the whole pool.

Spending your points wisely

The budget is the heart of the game. With 120 points for eleven players, the average cost is just under eleven points per player, so a team of all stars is impossible and a team of all value picks leaves points unused. The best teams mix a few stars with a few smart savings.

One good approach is to pick two or three stars you really want, then fill the rest of the team with ten-point players and one or two nine-point value picks. The nine-point players, Alex Carey, Mitchell Marsh, Aiden Markram, and Wanindu Hasaranga, are all established internationals, so saving points on them does not mean picking weak players.

Another approach is to spend evenly: eleven players at ten or eleven points each, with no twelve-point stars at all. That team has no weak link, and it is often the team that wins a group game, because it never depends on one player having a great day. Try both approaches and see which one you prefer.

The tournament your team plays in

Your fantasy team is built for the Cricket World Cup 2027, which runs from Saturday 2 October to Sunday 21 November 2027 in South Africa, Zimbabwe, and Namibia. Fourteen teams play fifty-seven matches across twelve venues, and the format has four stages: the Super Series, the group stage, the Super 7, and the knockouts.

The groups are already known. Group A is India, Australia, Pakistan, Afghanistan, Zimbabwe, and Qualifier A. Group B is New Zealand, South Africa, Sri Lanka, England, Bangladesh, and Qualifier B. The semi-finals are in Cape Town on 17 November and Centurion on 18 November, and the final is on 21 November 2027.

Australia are the defending champions, after beating India in the 2023 final in Ahmedabad, and they have won the tournament six times. Your fantasy players come from across the groups, so whichever teams reach the final, your team will have someone to cheer for.

Play with friends

Fantasy picking is best played in a group. Ask each friend to build their own eleven within the same 120-point budget, write the teams down, and compare them. Award points for the real tournament as it unfolds: agree your own scoring before the first match, such as a point for every run or wicket your players score in the real games, and see whose team finishes top.

Keep the game friendly and free. No money, no stakes, no arguments: the prize can be choosing the snacks for the final, or simply the honour of picking the best team. The fun is in the arguing beforehand, when everyone defends their picks, and in the watching afterwards, when every run and wicket matters twice as much.

Revisit your teams as the tournament goes on. If one of your players is not in their real squad, swap them out and agree the change with your group. The game is about enjoying the World Cup together, not about getting every pick right on the first day.

Questions people ask

How many players do I pick?

You pick exactly eleven players, the same as a real cricket team. The checker will tell you if you have picked too few or too many, and how many to add or remove.

What is the budget?

Your budget is 120 points. The biggest stars cost twelve points each, most players cost ten or eleven, and the value picks cost nine. Your eleven players must cost 120 points or fewer in total.

Can I pick two players from the same team?

Yes. There is no limit on how many players you can pick from one country, so you can build a team full of your favourite side if you want to.

Are these the real squads for 2027?

No. Squads for 2027 are not named yet. The players in this pool are famous international names chosen for the game, not a prediction of who will play in the tournament.

What do the points mean?

Points are game points, not money. They are a way to balance the game: famous stars cost more points, so you cannot pick all of them, and every team needs a mix of stars and value picks.

Can I change my team?

Yes. Untick a player and tick another one, then check your team again. The live counter shows your picks and your points as you go, so you always know where you stand.
