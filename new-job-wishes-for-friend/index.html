These wishes are starting points, not finished cards. A line lands best when it carries a detail only you know — the role they wanted, the interview they worried about, or the day the offer arrived.

How to make it your own

A wish becomes yours the moment you add something only you could write. Try one of these before you send it:

  • Name a detail: the role they wanted, the interview they worried about, the day the offer arrived.
  • Mention what you admire about them — their patience, their humour, their steady work.
  • Add one line in your own voice — a friend's encouragement lands better than any polished sentence.

Questions people ask

What should I write to a friend starting a new job?

Start with a wish from the warm section, then add one detail only you know — the role they wanted, the interview they worried about, or the day the offer arrived. Two or three sentences are plenty.

How long should a new-job message be?

Two or three sentences is enough for a card, and one sentence is enough for a text. A short message that is personal beats a long one that could be for anyone.

Can I send these wishes by text message?

Yes. The short wishes are written to fit a text, and the copy button copies the whole wish so you can paste it straight into your message.

What if my friend is nervous about the new job?

Choose a warm wish and add a line of encouragement — remind them of a time they handled something hard. Confidence grows faster when a friend says it out loud.

How do I make a new-job wish more personal?

Add a detail about their journey, name what you admire about them, and sign it the way you always do. The wish gives you the words; the personal detail makes them yours.
