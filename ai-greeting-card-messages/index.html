AI Greeting Card Messages for Every Occasion

By the Funtasha team · Published 18 November 2026 · Updated 18 November 2026

This page gives you thirty original greeting card messages, five for each of six occasions: birthday, thank-you, congratulations, get-well, new baby, and new home. Every message is short, warm, and ready to copy into a card, and each one is easy to personalise with a name or a memory.

A blank card can be surprisingly hard to fill. You know what you feel, but the words come out stiff or flat, and the moment passes. That is where a ready-made message helps: it gives you a starting point, and you add the personal touch that makes it yours.

The messages below are written to sound like a real person, not a greeting card factory. They are short enough to fit on a card, warm enough to mean something, and plain enough to suit almost anyone. Pick one, add a name or a small memory, and your card is done.

A copied message works best with one personal line added to it, such as a shared memory or a detail only you would know. Without that, even a lovely message can read as generic, so take a moment to make it yours.

How to use these messages

Each message below is written to stand on its own, so you can copy it straight into a card, a message, or an email. If the card is small, pick the shortest message in the group. If you have room, add a line of your own after it, such as a memory, a joke, or a wish for the year ahead.

When you personalise a message, small details beat grand ones. Mentioning the cake someone baked, the walk you took together, or the book they lent you will mean more than any flowery phrase. One real detail turns a ready-made message into something only you could have written.

If you are writing to someone you do not know well, such as a colleague or a distant relative, the plainer messages in each group are the safest choice. Save the warmest, most personal ones for close friends and family, where they will land best.

Birthday messages

Birthday messages should feel like a small celebration in words. Keep them bright, keep them kind, and if you know the person well, add a line about the year ahead.

  1. Happy birthday! May your year be full of good company, good food, and days worth remembering.
  2. Wishing you a birthday as kind and generous as you are. Enjoy every bit of your day.
  3. Another year, another reason to celebrate you. Happy birthday, and here is to all the good things ahead.
  4. Happy birthday! I hope your day is loud with laughter and quiet enough for cake.
  5. On your birthday, I just want you to know how glad I am that you are in my life. Have a wonderful day.

Thank-you messages

A good thank-you names what you are grateful for. These messages work for gifts, help, kindness, and all the small things people do that deserve a word.

  1. Thank you so much. Your kindness meant more than you know, and I will not forget it.
  2. I am so grateful for your help. You made a hard week feel much lighter, and I truly appreciate it.
  3. Thank you for the lovely gift. I have already used it, and it made me smile each time.
  4. Thank you for being there when it mattered. Your support carried me further than words can say.
  5. A small note to say a big thank you. You have a gift for making people feel cared for.

Congratulations messages

Congratulations messages work best when they name the achievement. Add the job, the exam, the house, or the milestone, and the message comes alive.

  1. Congratulations! All your hard work has paid off, and nobody deserves this more than you.
  2. What wonderful news! I am so proud of you, and I cannot wait to see what you do next.
  3. Congratulations on this big step. You earned it with patience, effort, and a lot of heart.
  4. Bravo! This is the result of years of quiet dedication, and it is lovely to see it recognised.
  5. Congratulations! May this success be the first of many, and may you enjoy every moment of it.

Get-well messages

Get-well messages should be gentle, hopeful, and free of pressure. A short note that says you are thinking of someone is often exactly what they need.

  1. Wishing you a restful recovery and brighter days ahead. Take all the time you need.
  2. I am thinking of you and sending all my best wishes for a smooth and speedy recovery.
  3. Rest well, and let others take care of you for a while. We will be here when you are ready.
  4. Hope each day brings you a little more strength and a little more comfort. Get well soon.
  5. Sending you warm thoughts and a gentle reminder that you are loved and missed. Feel better soon.

New baby messages

New baby messages celebrate the arrival and welcome the little one. Keep them warm and simple, and congratulate the parents too.

  1. Welcome to the world, little one! Congratulations to the whole family on this joyful arrival.
  2. Congratulations on your beautiful baby. May your days be full of tiny fingers, soft yawns, and big love.
  3. What wonderful news! Wishing your new family health, happiness, and plenty of sleep.
  4. A brand-new person has arrived, and the world is better for it. Congratulations and so much love.
  5. Congratulations on your little bundle of joy. Enjoy every cuddle, every giggle, and every sleepy moment.

New home messages

New home messages wish the household well in its next chapter. Mention the home itself, the garden, or the fresh start, and the message feels grounded.

  1. Congratulations on your new home! May it be full of laughter, good meals, and happy memories.
  2. Wishing you all the best in your new place. May every room feel like home from the very first day.
  3. Happy new home! Here is to fresh paint, full shelves, and many warm evenings ahead.
  4. Congratulations on the move! May your new home bring you comfort, calm, and wonderful neighbours.
  5. A new home, a new chapter. Wishing you joy in every corner and peace in every room.

Choosing the right tone for each occasion

The right tone depends on who you are writing to and how well you know them. For close friends and family, warmth and humour land well. For colleagues, neighbours, and people you know less well, a simple, sincere tone is best, and a joke can wait until you know them better.

Occasion matters too. Birthday and congratulations messages can be bright and playful, while get-well and sympathy notes should be gentle and measured. A new baby card can be joyful and light, and a new home card can be warm and hopeful. When in doubt, choose the kinder, simpler wording.

Length matters as well. A short message on a small card is better than a long one squeezed into the margins. If you have a lot to say, write the message on the card and save the longer story for a letter or a visit.

Turning a short message into a longer note

If you want to write more than a line or two, build outward from the message. Start with the ready-made line, then add a memory, then a wish for the future. Three short parts make a note that feels complete without becoming an essay.

For example, a birthday note might begin with a happy birthday line, continue with a memory of something you did together, and end with a wish for the year ahead. A thank-you note might start with thanks, describe the help or gift, and close with a promise to return the kindness.

Handwriting adds warmth to any card, even if your handwriting is not perfect. A few honest lines in your own hand will always beat a long printed message, because the reader can feel that you sat down and took the time.

Questions people ask

Can I copy these messages into a card as they are?

Yes, every message is written to stand alone. For the best result, add one personal line, such as a name, a memory, or a detail about the person.

What should I write if I do not know the person well?

Choose the simplest message in the group and keep it short. A plain, sincere line is always welcome, while a very personal message can feel out of place.

How long should a card message be?

Short is usually best. One or two lines fit most cards, and three short parts, a greeting, a memory, and a wish, make a full note without running out of room.

What is a safe message for a get-well card?

Keep it gentle and hopeful, with no pressure to reply or recover quickly. Wishing someone rest and brighter days ahead is kind, simple, and safe.

Can I change the wording of a message?

Of course. These messages are starting points. Swap a word, add a name, or combine two lines to make the message sound like you.
