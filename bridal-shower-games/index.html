Bridal Shower Games

Choose from 16 complete bridal shower games across Icebreakers, Guessing, Active and Keepsake, each with what you need, setup and how to play. Prize and timing tips help you plan a shower everyone enjoys.

All 16 games are family-friendly and gentle. Let guests join in at their own pace, and keep the active games light-hearted so everyone can play comfortably.

How to plan the games

Three to five games is plenty for a two-hour shower. Open with an icebreaker while guests arrive, play a guessing game or two over refreshments, add one active game for laughter, and close with a keepsake game the bride takes home. Leave generous time for food, conversation and gift opening.

Prize and timing tips

  • Keep each game to ten minutes or less so the party keeps its pace.
  • Small prizes work best: candles, chocolates, hand cream or a pretty notebook.
  • Have a prize ready for every game, plus one extra for a surprise tie.
  • Let the bride judge the creative games — it is her celebration.
  • Set up the keepsake games on a side table so guests can join in at any time.

Icebreakers

Two Truths and a Lie, Bride Edition

What you need: Paper, pens.

Setup: Each guest writes down two truths and one lie about themselves.

How to play: Guests read theirs aloud and the group guesses the lie. The bride goes first.

Who Knows the Bride Best?

What you need: Printed question sheets, pens.

Setup: Write ten questions about the bride, such as her favourite film or her dream holiday.

How to play: Guests answer the questions, then the bride shares the correct answers. The highest score wins.

Bridal Bingo

What you need: Printed bingo cards with wedding words, pens.

Setup: Hand out a card to each guest.

How to play: Guests mark words as they hear them during the shower. The first full row wins.

The Purse Game

What you need: A list of everyday items, pens.

Setup: Prepare a list of items people might carry in a bag.

How to play: Guests score a point for each item on the list they have in their bag. The highest score wins.

Guessing

Guess the Love Song

What you need: A playlist of love songs, paper, pens.

Setup: Prepare short clips of well-known love songs.

How to play: Play a few seconds of each song. Guests write down the title. The most correct wins.

How Well Do You Know the Couple?

What you need: Question cards about the couple.

Setup: Ask the couple for their answers in advance.

How to play: Guests answer questions about the couple's story. The most correct answers wins.

Guess the Honeymoon Destination

What you need: Clue cards, paper, pens.

Setup: Write clues about the honeymoon destination on the cards.

How to play: Read the clues one at a time. The first guest to guess the destination wins.

The Newlywed Quiz

What you need: Questions answered by the groom in advance.

Setup: Ask the groom ten questions before the shower and note his answers.

How to play: The bride answers the same questions aloud. Guests score a point for each answer that matches.

Active

Wedding Dress Relay

What you need: Rolls of toilet paper, one per team.

Setup: Split guests into teams and choose a model in each team.

How to play: Teams wrap their model in a wedding dress made of toilet paper in five minutes. The bride judges the best dress.

Bridal Charades

What you need: Slips of paper with wedding words.

Setup: Write words like "cutting the cake" or "throwing the bouquet" on the slips.

How to play: Players act out the word while their team guesses. The team with the most correct guesses wins.

Ring Toss

What you need: Rings and a bottle or stand.

Setup: Set up the stand a few steps from a throwing line.

How to play: Players toss the rings and score for each one that lands on the stand. The highest score wins.

Musical Bouquets

What you need: A music player and a bouquet.

Setup: Guests sit in a circle with the bouquet.

How to play: Pass the bouquet while the music plays. When it stops, the guest holding the bouquet is out. The last one left wins.

Keepsake

Advice Cards for the Bride

What you need: Cards, pens, a keepsake box.

Setup: Place the cards and pens on a table with the box.

How to play: Guests write marriage advice or a favourite memory. The bride keeps the box.

Recipe Card Shower

What you need: Blank recipe cards, pens.

Setup: Give each guest a recipe card and a pen.

How to play: Guests write a favourite recipe for the couple. The cards become a cookbook for their new home.

Date Night Ideas Jar

What you need: A jar, slips of paper, pens.

Setup: Place the jar and paper on a table.

How to play: Guests write date-night ideas on the slips. The couple draws one whenever they need inspiration.

Wishes for the Couple Quilt

What you need: Fabric squares, fabric markers.

Setup: Lay out the squares and markers on a table.

How to play: Guests sign or decorate a square. The squares are sewn into a keepsake quilt for the couple.

Questions people ask

How many games should we play at a bridal shower?

Three to five games is plenty for a two-hour shower. Open with an icebreaker, add one or two guessing games, one active game, and close with a keepsake game.

What prizes work best for bridal shower games?

Small, simple prizes work best: candles, chocolates, hand cream or a pretty notebook. Have one prize per game, plus an extra for a surprise tie.

Do the games work if guests do not know each other well?

Yes. The icebreakers are made for exactly that — Two Truths and a Lie and Who Knows the Bride Best help guests meet each other through the bride's story.

Can the games be played at a co-ed shower?

Yes. Every game here works for mixed groups, and the guessing and active games are easy favourites with everyone.

What if the bride is shy about being the centre of attention?

Keep the bride's role light: let her judge the creative games and answer the quiz questions, but let the guests carry the games themselves.
