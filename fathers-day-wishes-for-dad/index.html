These wishes are starting points, not finished cards. A line lands best when it carries a detail only you know — a childhood memory, something he taught you, or the joke only he tells.

How to make it your own

A wish becomes yours the moment you add something only you could write. Try one of these before you send it:

  • Name a memory: a trip together, a skill he taught you, or a joke only he tells.
  • Mention something he does that you love — a habit, a hobby, or the way he looks after you.
  • Sign it the way you always do — a nickname or a family phrase beats a formal ending.

Questions people ask

What should I write in a Father's Day card for my dad?

Start with a wish from the sweet section, then add one detail only you know — a favourite memory, something he taught you, or a thank-you for a specific kindness. Two or three heartfelt sentences are plenty.

How long should a Father's Day message be?

Two or three sentences is enough for a card, and one sentence is enough for a text. A short message that is personal beats a long one that could be for anyone.

Can I send these wishes by text message?

Yes. The short wishes are written to fit a text, and the copy button copies the whole wish so you can paste it straight into your message.

What if I want something funny instead of sweet?

Use the funny section — every line is kind and good-natured. If your dad loves a joke, pair one funny wish with one sweet line.

How do I make a Father's Day wish more personal?

Add a shared memory, name something he does that you love, and sign it the way you always do. The wish gives you the words; the personal detail makes them yours.
