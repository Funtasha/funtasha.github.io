How the date is worked out

Saint Patrick's Day falls on the same date every year: 17 March. Unlike holidays that follow a weekday rule, there is nothing to work out — the countdown simply counts down to the next 17 March at midnight, in your local time.

Once 17 March has passed, the countdown rolls forward to the 17 March of the following year, so the page is always counting down to the next celebration, never to one that has already happened.

The day is named for Saint Patrick, the patron saint of Ireland, and it is celebrated in Ireland and by Irish communities around the world with parades, music and gatherings.

Family-friendly ways to celebrate

Saint Patrick's Day is easy to celebrate at home, and children can join in with almost everything. The theme is simple: green, cheerful and together.

  • Wear green. A green scarf, hat or jumper is all it takes — the whole household can join in, pets included.
  • Cook something green. A green salad, green vegetables, or a green-topped dessert brings the theme to the table.
  • Play some Irish music. A playlist of Irish folk tunes or a family sing-along sets the mood in minutes.
  • Make shamrock crafts. Paper shamrocks, green paper chains and hand-drawn rainbows are simple, cheap and fun to display.
  • Tell a story. Share a tale about Ireland, look at photographs of its green hills, or learn a few Irish words together.
  • Go for a walk. A spring walk is a fitting way to spend 17 March — look for the first green shoots of the season.

Parade and party planning

Parades are the heart of St. Patrick's Day in many towns and cities, and a little planning makes the day much more enjoyable. If you are going to watch a parade, arrive early to find a good spot, dress for the weather, and bring something to sit on if you will be standing for a while. March weather can be changeable, so layers and a waterproof jacket are sensible whatever the forecast says.

For younger children, a parade is best enjoyed in short bursts. Choose a spot near the start of the route, where the excitement is high and the wait is short, and have a plan for a warm drink and a rest afterwards. A small flag or a green hat to wave makes the experience feel personal, and a photograph of the day becomes a keepsake.

If you are hosting a small gathering instead, keep it simple. Green decorations, a playlist of Irish music, and a table of food are all you need. A gathering at home has its own charm: everyone can relax, children can play, and the celebration can last as long as you like. Whether you go out or stay in, the countdown gives you time to plan without rushing.

Irish traditions to enjoy

St. Patrick's Day is a celebration of Ireland, and its traditions are warm and welcoming. Music is at the centre of the day: fiddles, flutes and songs that have been passed down through generations fill pubs, halls and homes. You do not need to know the tunes to enjoy them — listening, clapping along and tapping your feet are participation enough.

The shamrock is the best-known symbol of the day. One well-known story says that Saint Patrick used the three leaves of the shamrock to explain a point of faith, and ever since, the little green plant has stood for Ireland itself. Wearing a shamrock, drawing one, or making paper versions with children is a simple way to join in the tradition.

Storytelling is another Irish tradition that fits the day perfectly. Ireland has a long history of tales told aloud — stories of heroes, of the sea, of small villages and big adventures. Reading an Irish story together, or telling one from memory, is a quiet way to celebrate that suits the whole family, and it costs nothing at all.

Food and drink ideas

Food is a happy part of any St. Patrick's Day, and Irish cooking offers plenty of family-friendly ideas. A slow-cooked stew, made with lamb or beef and plenty of vegetables, is a classic choice for the day, and it fills the house with a wonderful smell as it cooks. Soda bread, warm from the oven with butter, is a simple pleasure that children can help to make.

Green food brings the theme to the table without much effort. A green salad, peas, green beans, or a dessert topped with green fruit all fit the day. For younger children, a green smoothie — made with banana, spinach and apple juice — is a fun and healthy way to drink something green, and making it together is part of the entertainment.

The day is for the whole family, so the table should be too. Keep the focus on food, music and company rather than on drinks for grown-ups, and everyone can enjoy the celebration together. A pot of tea, a jug of something green, and a table full of people are all the refreshments the day needs.

Classroom uses for the countdown

Teachers find the St. Patrick's Day countdown a useful classroom tool. Counting down the days to March 17 gives children a real date to follow, and each day of the countdown can carry a small activity: a fact about Ireland, a new Irish word, a craft to make, or a song to learn. The countdown turns a single celebration into a week or two of learning.

Geography fits naturally into the countdown. Looking at a map of Ireland, finding its counties and cities, learning about its rivers and coastline, and talking about its weather and landscape all build naturally from the celebration. Children can compare Ireland with their own country, and discover what is similar and what is different.

Crafts and art are the easiest classroom activities of all. Paper shamrocks, green paper chains, rainbows and maps can be made with simple materials, and the finished work makes a cheerful classroom display. By the time March 17 arrives, the classroom is already decorated, the children already know the story of the day, and the celebration feels like something they built together.

Questions people ask

When is St. Patrick's Day?

St. Patrick's Day is on 17 March every year. The countdown at the top of this page shows the time left until the next one begins.

Why do people wear green?

Green is linked with Ireland, its green countryside and the shamrock. Wearing green on 17 March is a simple, cheerful way to join in the celebration.

What time does the countdown end?

The countdown ends at midnight local time at the start of 17 March, so it reaches zero just as the day begins.

What can children do to celebrate?

Children can wear green, make shamrock crafts and paper chains, help cook a green meal, and listen to Irish music. Every idea on this page is suitable for the whole family.

Do I need to be Irish to join in?

No. St. Patrick's Day is celebrated by Irish communities and their friends all over the world, and everyone is welcome to enjoy the music, the food and the fun.
