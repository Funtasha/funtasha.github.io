AI Video Generator With No Watermark

By the Funtasha team · Published 20 November 2026 · Updated 20 November 2026

A few free AI video tools really do leave your clips unmarked — Google Flow hides only an invisible mark, and Wan 2.2 stamps nothing at all. This page names the four honest options and explains what each one costs you instead.

A watermark is the price of a free tier, not a mistake. Never pay a stranger to remove a watermark, and never crop or cover one if the tool's rules forbid it — re-render the clip on a tool that does not stamp it instead.

What "no watermark" really means

"No watermark" sounds simple, but it covers three different things, and knowing which one you are getting changes everything. The first is a visible watermark: a logo or a name stamped on the picture where everyone can see it, usually in a corner. Kling, Runway, and PixVerse all put a visible watermark on their free exports. It is the most honest kind of mark — you can see exactly what you are getting, and exactly what paying would remove.

The second is an invisible mark. Google Flow puts an invisible SynthID mark on most free clips: viewers see a clean picture with no stamp, while software can still tell that the clip was made by AI. This is the best deal most people will find — a clip that looks clean to the eye, with the mark hidden where only a machine can read it. When this page says Flow has "no visible watermark", this is what it means.

The third is no mark at all. Wan 2.2 is open source software that runs on your own computer, so there is nobody to stamp your clips and no mark to remove. It is the only route on this page to a truly unmarked free clip on your own terms — and the price is that you need a computer with a graphics card to run it.

When a page promises "no watermark" without saying which kind it means, be careful. Some pages mean "no visible watermark" and deliver exactly that. Others mean "we will help you strip the watermark off someone else's tool", which is a different promise entirely — and one that can break the tool's rules. This page only names tools that leave your clips unmarked by design.

Google Flow: daily free clips with no visible mark

Google Flow is the strongest free answer to the watermark problem. It gives 50 free credits a day, which works out as five 8-second clips made with its Veo 3.1 Lite model, and most free clips carry only the invisible SynthID mark — no visible watermark. For someone making one short clip a day, Flow covers the whole habit for free, and the clips look clean enough to post anywhere.

The limits are the usual ones: 8 seconds per clip, and a daily credit budget. Five clips a day is generous for a free tier, but it is still a budget — plan each clip before you generate it, and you will rarely run out. The clips are short, so Flow suits single shots: one subject, one action, one camera move. Longer videos come from stitching several Flow clips together, which CapCut does for free.

Flow is also the most searched tool on this page — "google flow" is searched about 1.0 million times a month as of November 2026 — which means it is well tested, well documented, and easy to learn. If you only try one tool from this page, try Flow first.

Google Vids: 60 seconds of free clips a month

Google Vids is the quiet option on this list. It gives 60 seconds of AI clips a month free on a personal account, up to 15 seconds per clip, with no visible watermark. Sixty seconds a month is a small allowance next to Flow's five a day, but Vids is built for a different job: short explainers, announcements, and clips that sit inside a longer video you are already making.

Vids suits the person who needs one good clip a week rather than five a day. A shop owner announcing a new product, a teacher showing a quick idea, a creator adding one moving shot to a talking-head video — for those jobs, 60 seconds of clips a month is plenty, and the absence of a watermark means the clip drops straight into the finished video without apology.

The trade is volume. If you make clips every day, Vids alone will not carry you — pair it with Flow, and use Vids for the clips where its style fits best. The two tools together give you a daily allowance and a monthly one, both unmarked.

Wan 2.2: truly no watermark — on your own computer

Wan 2.2 is the only tool on this page with no watermark of any kind, visible or invisible. It is open source software, released under the Apache 2.0 licence, and it runs on your own computer's graphics card. There is no account, no credit balance, no daily limit, and nobody to stamp your clips — because the software is yours to run.

The price is the computer. Wan 2.2 needs a graphics card (GPU) that can run it, and the stronger the card, the faster your clips render. On a modest card, a short clip can take a while; on a strong one, it is quick. There is no number to quote here, because the speed depends entirely on the machine in front of you — but the clips themselves cost nothing, and there is no limit on how many you make.

Wan 2.2 suits the person who makes a lot of clips and wants full control: no terms of service deciding what a clip may show, no watermark to plan around, no monthly reset. It also suits anyone who is uncomfortable uploading their ideas to a stranger's server. The trade is setup and patience — open source tools ask more of you than a website does, and the first render is the slowest.

Leonardo: free, but your creations are public

Leonardo gives 150 tokens a day free, with no credit card required, and no visible watermark on what you make. That is a generous free tier, and the lack of a card requirement makes it the easiest tool on this page to start with — sign up, and you are making things within minutes.

The trade is privacy. Free creations on Leonardo are public, which means other people can see what you make. For practice clips, for experiments, and for anything you were going to post anyway, that costs nothing. For a surprise, a gift, or a product you have not announced yet, it matters — do not make anything on a free Leonardo account that you need to keep secret.

Leonardo suits the beginner who wants to try AI video without committing to anything: no card, no watermark, a daily token budget that refills. Try it for a week, learn what a good prompt looks like, and then decide whether you need one of the other tools on this page for the clips that matter.

The watermarked tier — and when it is still worth using

The tools that watermark their free exports are not bad tools. Kling, Runway, and PixVerse all stamp free clips, and all three are worth using — because the watermark is a trade, not a punishment, and sometimes the trade is worth it.

Kling is the strongest free option for realistic people: people walking, moving, and behaving like people, with camera moves that feel like a real camera. If your clip needs a person in it, Kling's free daily credits are worth the watermark — and paid plans start from about $7–10/mo if you later want the mark gone. PixVerse is the effects pick: 60 starter credits plus 30 a day, with free clips up to 540p, and a watermark that marks the clip as a free-tier test. Runway offers a one-time trial of 125 credits, watermarked, with paid plans from $12–15/mo — a good way to try a tool properly before deciding whether it is worth paying for.

The watermarked tier is also the right place to practice. Every clip you make while learning costs credits somewhere, and learning on a watermarked free tier costs nothing but the mark. Practice on Kling or PixVerse, find the prompt that works, and then spend your clean Flow credits on the final version. That way the watermark never appears on the clip you actually post.

If a watermark ruins your clip

Sometimes the clip is perfect except for the mark. The watermark sits in the corner, the clip is exactly what you wanted, and the temptation is to get rid of the mark by any means. There are honest ways to do that, and dishonest ones, and the difference matters.

The honest fix is to re-render the clip on a tool that does not stamp it. Take the same prompt to Flow, where most free clips carry only the invisible SynthID mark, or to Wan 2.2, where nothing is stamped at all. The clip will not come out identical — every tool reads a prompt differently — but a clean clip that is close is worth more than a perfect clip with a mark you cannot use.

The dishonest fix is to crop the watermark out, cover it with a sticker, or blur it away. That is not advised if it breaks the tool's terms, and most free tiers do forbid removing the mark. It can also cost you the account that gives you the free clips in the first place. A watermark is a small price for a free clip, and the tools on this page give you clean alternatives — use them, and leave the mark where it belongs.

Questions people ask

Is there a truly free AI video generator with no watermark?

Google Vids and Leonardo also leave no visible watermark, with smaller allowances and their own trade-offs.

What is SynthID?

SynthID is an invisible mark that Google puts inside AI-made media. On Flow clips, it is hidden in the file itself: viewers see a clean clip with no stamp, while software can still tell that the clip was made by AI. It is the reason Flow clips look clean while still being honestly labelled as AI-made.

Why do Kling, Runway, and PixVerse watermark free clips?

Because the watermark is the line between the free tier and the paid one. Making video costs real computer power, so these tools stamp their free exports and keep watermark-free exports for paying users. Kling's paid plans start from about $7–10/mo, and Runway's from $12–15/mo. The watermark is the price of trying the tool for free.

Does Google Vids watermark its free clips?

It is one of the four honest no-watermark options on this page, and it suits short explainers and announcements where a minute of clips a month is enough.

Do I need a powerful computer for Wan 2.2?

You need a computer with a graphics card (GPU) that can run it — Wan 2.2 is open source software that renders on your own machine, not on a website. The stronger the card, the faster your clips render, and the weaker the card, the longer each clip takes.
