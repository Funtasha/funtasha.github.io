Preparing for Ramadan in the weeks before

A good Ramadan rarely happens by accident. The weeks before the month begins are the best time to clear small jobs out of the way, so the month itself can be about fasting, prayer and family rather than errands. Many people use the countdown as a gentle nudge: each time they glance at the number of days left, they pick one small task to finish.

Start with the simple things. Sort out your work diary, plan a few easy suhoor and iftar meals, and set aside a quiet corner at home for reading and prayer. If you plan to give charity during the month, decide in advance how much you can give and where it will go. Small steps now make the first fast feel calm instead of rushed.

Daily good deeds to build the habit

The good deed of the day on this page changes every morning. Each idea is deliberately small: call a relative you have not spoken to for a while, help a neighbour with a heavy bag, share a meal with someone who eats alone, or spend ten quiet minutes reading. The point is not to do something grand, but to do something kind every single day.

Habits grow the same way in Ramadan as they do at any other time of year. One small deed, repeated daily, becomes part of who you are, and by the time the month arrives you are already living the way you want to live in it. If you miss a day, do not worry: pick up the next idea tomorrow and carry on.

Making the most of the first days

The first two or three fasts are usually the hardest part of the month. Your body is adjusting to a new rhythm, your sleep is changing, and the days can feel long. Go gently with yourself. Drink plenty of water between iftar and suhoor, keep meals balanced, and give yourself permission to rest in the afternoon.

It also helps to have a small plan for each day before the month begins. Decide when you will pray, when you will read, and when you will rest, and write the plan somewhere you will see it. A simple plan, followed loosely, is worth more than an ambitious one that falls apart on the second day.

Ramadan with family and children

Ramadan is a family month, and the countdown is a lovely thing to share with children. Let them watch the numbers fall, and give them their own small role: choosing the deed of the day, helping to lay the iftar table, or saving a little of their pocket money for charity. Children who feel included remember the month fondly for years.

Young children are not expected to fast the whole day, and nobody should push them to. Many families let little ones try a half-day fast, or fast until lunchtime, and celebrate the effort with praise and a special treat at iftar. The goal is a happy memory, not a test of endurance.

A gentle routine for the month

A routine makes fasting easier, because your body knows what is coming. Wake for suhoor in good time, eat slowly, and drink water before the fast begins. Work or study in the morning while your energy is high, and keep the afternoon for lighter tasks. A short rest after the midday prayer can make the last hours before iftar much easier.

Evenings in Ramadan are for gathering. Break the fast with family if you can, pray together, and then enjoy the night without rushing. Some people read a little each evening; others visit friends or help at the mosque. Whatever you choose, keep it steady: a routine you can hold for the whole month beats a busy first week followed by exhaustion.

Questions people ask

When does Ramadan 2027 start?

Ramadan 2027 is expected to begin on Monday 8 February 2027. The date depends on the sighting of the new moon, so the confirmed date may move by a day. Check with your local mosque as the month approaches.

Why might the date move by a day?

Ramadan follows the Islamic lunar calendar, and each month begins with the sighting of the new moon. Clouds, location, and local practice can all shift the confirmed start by a day, which is why dates are always given as expected until the moon is seen.

Does the countdown use my local time?

Yes. The countdown reads the clock on your own device, so the days, hours, minutes and seconds are counted in your local time zone. If your device clock is wrong, the countdown will be wrong too, so keep your clock set correctly.

What is the good deed of the day?

It is a small, kind idea that changes every morning, drawn from a short list on this page. Each idea takes only a few minutes, and the aim is to build a daily habit of kindness in the run-up to Ramadan and through the month itself.

What happens after Ramadan begins?

The countdown changes to a short message saying Ramadan has begun, and the good deed of the day keeps changing each morning. You can keep using the page through the whole month for a fresh idea every day.

Can I use this countdown on my phone?
