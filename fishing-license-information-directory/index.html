This guide explains fishing licences in plain English: who needs one, the kinds of licence, where to get one, and what happens without one. It is written for anyone planning to fish, whether for the first time or in a new place, and it is general information only, not legal advice. Who needs a licence

In many places, anyone fishing with a rod needs a licence, and in some places, only some people do. The answer depends on where you fish, what you fish for, and how old you are. Sea fishing and freshwater fishing often have different rules, and in some places one needs a licence and the other does not. Some waters are private, and fishing there needs the owner's permission as well as, or instead of, a licence. Some places exempt children below a certain age, and some exempt older anglers. No single rule covers every place.

The only safe approach is to check before you fish. Find the official authority for the waters you plan to fish, and ask whether you need a licence, what kind, and what it covers. Do not rely on what a friend told you, or on what the rules were last year, or on what the rules are in a different place. Rules change, and the rules for one river can differ from the rules for the river next to it. A few minutes of checking saves a fine and a spoiled day.

  • Sea and freshwater fishing often have different rules.
  • Private waters may need the owner's permission as well.
  • Age exemptions differ by place, so check for yours.
  • Always check the official authority before you fish.

The licence types

Where licences exist, they usually come in a few kinds, and the right one depends on how you fish. A short licence covers a day or a few days, and suits a holiday or a first try. A season licence covers a whole season, and suits anyone who fishes regularly in one place. Some places offer a licence for one kind of fish, and a different one for another, and some offer a licence that covers a region, a river, or a single stretch of water. The licence you need is the one that covers the waters, the fish and the time you plan to fish.

Read what the licence covers before you buy it, because a licence for the wrong water, the wrong fish or the wrong dates is the same as no licence at all. Check whether it covers the whole region or only part of it, whether it covers the fish you want, and whether it includes any catch limits or closed seasons. And keep the licence with you while you fish: in many places, you must be able to show it when asked, and a licence left at home does not help.

  • Short licences cover a day or a few days; season licences cover a season.
  • Some licences cover one kind of fish, and others cover a region or a stretch.
  • Check that the licence covers your waters, your fish and your dates.
  • Keep the licence with you while you fish.

Where to get one

Licences are usually sold by the official authority for the waters, and often through more than one channel. Many authorities sell licences online, through their own website, which is usually the quickest way. Some sell through local shops, post offices or fishing clubs, which suits anyone who wants to ask a question in person. Some waters are managed by a club or an owner, and the licence or permit for those waters comes from them directly.

Buy from the official source, and be wary of anyone selling a licence through an unofficial channel. A licence bought from the wrong seller may not be valid, and a forged licence is worse than none. Ask for a receipt, and keep it with the licence. If you are unsure which licence you need, ask the seller: the official seller is the one place where the answer is reliable. And buy before you fish, not after you arrive at the water, because some licences take time to arrange.

  • Buy from the official authority for the waters, usually online or in person.
  • Local shops, post offices and fishing clubs often sell them too.
  • Club and private waters sell their own permits directly.
  • Buy from the official source, and keep the receipt.
  • Buy before you fish, not after you arrive.

The rules that surprise people

Fishing rules hold surprises even for experienced anglers, and the surprises are usually about place, not about fish. The first is that the rules change at a boundary you cannot see: a river can have different rules on its two banks, and a lake can have different rules at its two ends. The second is that a licence for one region does not cover the next one, so fishing across a boundary without the second licence is fishing without a licence. The third is that some waters are closed at certain times of year, and fishing there in the closed season is not allowed even with a licence.

The fourth surprise is catch limits: many places limit how many fish of a kind you may keep, and some require you to return fish below or above a certain size. The fifth is that some methods are not allowed in some waters, such as certain baits or certain numbers of rods. And the sixth is that the rules for children differ: in some places children need no licence, in others they need one, and in others they need one only above a certain age. Check all of these with the official authority for the waters you plan to fish.

  • Rules can change at a boundary you cannot see.
  • A licence for one region does not cover the next one.
  • Some waters are closed at certain times of year.
  • Catch limits and size limits are common, and they differ by place.
  • Some methods and baits are not allowed in some waters.
  • Rules for children differ by place, so check for yours.

What happens without one

Fishing without a licence where one is required can lead to a fine, and the fine can be larger than the price of the licence by a long way. In some places, the fishing gear can be taken as well, and in serious cases, such as fishing in a closed season or taking protected fish, the penalties are heavier still. The exact penalty depends on the place and on what happened, and this page states no penalty for any specific place as fact.

The practical point is simpler: a licence is cheap compared with a fine, and checking takes minutes. Buy the licence before you fish, keep it with you, and follow the rules for the waters you are on. If you are checked, show the licence and answer honestly. If you realise partway through the day that your licence does not cover where you are, stop fishing and sort it out before you carry on. An honest mistake, fixed at once, is a different matter from fishing on knowingly without one.

  • Fishing without a licence where one is required can lead to a fine.
  • Gear can be taken in some places, and penalties are heavier for serious cases.
  • A licence is cheap compared with a fine.
  • If your licence does not cover where you are, stop and sort it out.

Common mistakes and how to avoid them

The most common mistake is assuming the rules are the same everywhere. They are not, and the angler who fishes a new place by the old place's rules is fishing blind. The second mistake is no licence for the second region: a day's fishing that crosses a boundary needs the licence for both sides, and the second licence is the one people forget. The third is fishing a closed season, because the season was not checked. The fourth is keeping fish that should have been returned, because the size or catch limit was not known.

The fifth mistake is buying from an unofficial seller and finding out the licence is not valid. The sixth is leaving the licence at home, and being unable to show it when asked. And the seventh is assuming children need no licence, when in that place they do. Every one of these is avoided the same way: check the official authority for the waters you plan to fish, before you fish, and keep the licence with you while you do.

  • Never assume the rules are the same everywhere.
  • Crossing a boundary may need a second licence.
  • Check the season, the catch limits and the size limits.
  • Buy from the official source, and keep the receipt.
  • Keep the licence with you while you fish.
  • Check the rules for children in that place, not in general.

Questions people ask

Do I need a fishing licence?

In many places, yes, and in some places, no. It depends on where you fish, what you fish for, and how old you are. Sea and freshwater fishing often have different rules, so check the official authority for the waters you plan to fish, before you fish.

Where do I get one?

Licences are usually sold by the official authority for the waters, often online through their own website, and sometimes through local shops, post offices or fishing clubs. Club and private waters sell their own permits directly. Buy from the official source, and keep the receipt.

How much does it cost?

Licence prices vary by place, by the kind of licence, and by how long it lasts, so no single figure is useful. A short licence for a day costs less than a season licence. The official seller will tell you the price for the licence you need.

What happens if I fish without one?

Where a licence is required, fishing without one can lead to a fine, and the fine can be much larger than the price of the licence. In some places, gear can be taken as well. The exact penalty depends on the place, so check the official authority.

Do children need a licence?

It depends on the place. In some places children need no licence, in others they need one, and in others they need one only above a certain age. Check the rules for children with the official authority for the waters you plan to fish.

Are the rules the same everywhere?

No: rules differ by place, sometimes sharply, and they can change at a boundary you cannot see. A licence for one region does not cover the next one. Always check the official authority for the waters you plan to fish, before you fish.
