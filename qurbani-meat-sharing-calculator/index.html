How the calculator works

Start with the total weight of the meat, in kilograms or pounds — whichever you weighed it in. Then choose how to divide it. The simplest choice is equal thirds, one third for each of the three shares. If your family is large, the family-heavy split keeps half at home and divides the rest equally. If you would rather give more away, the giving split sends half to people in need. And if none of those fit, choose your own shares and type in three percentages that add up to 100.

Press the button and the calculator shows all three shares at once, in the unit you chose. If you tell it how many families will share the third part, it also works out how much each family gets. The numbers are rounded to two decimal places, and the shares always add up to the total you entered — the calculator adjusts the largest share by a fraction if the rounding leaves a tiny difference.

The three shares of Qurbani meat

The well-known way to share Qurbani meat is to divide it into three parts. One part stays with the family, for the meals of Eid and the days that follow. One part goes to relatives and friends, as a gift that keeps the bonds of family and friendship strong. And one part goes to people in need, so that everyone in the community can enjoy the festival, not only those who can afford a sacrifice of their own.

The three parts do not have to be exactly equal. Families differ: a large household may keep more, a small one may give more away, and someone with many relatives nearby may share more widely. The calculator lets you choose the split that fits your own situation, while keeping the three-part idea at the centre. What matters is that the sharing happens, and that the part for people in need is a real share, not an afterthought.

A worked example

Suppose the total weight is 90 kilograms and you choose equal thirds. Each share is 90 divided by 3, which is 30 kilograms: 30 for the family, 30 for relatives and friends, and 30 for people in need. If the third part is shared between 10 families, each family receives 30 divided by 10, which is 3 kilograms.

Now suppose the total is 120 kilograms and you choose the family-heavy split: half, quarter, quarter. The family keeps 120 times 50 percent, which is 60 kilograms. Relatives and friends receive 120 times 25 percent, which is 30 kilograms. People in need receive the same, 30 kilograms. The three shares add up to 60 plus 30 plus 30, which is 120 — the full total, with nothing left over and nothing missing. The calculator checks this arithmetic for you on every split, including your own custom percentages.

Choosing different shares for your family

Equal thirds are a good starting point, but they are not a rule. Think about who is around your table this Eid. A household with many children and guests may need a bigger family share. A family with relatives living nearby may want a bigger friends share, so that no visit leaves empty-handed. And a family that knows people in need in the neighbourhood may choose to give the biggest share away.

The custom option is there for all of these cases. Type three percentages that add up to 100 — for example 40, 30 and 30 — and the calculator does the rest. If your percentages do not add up to 100, it will tell you, so you can fix them before you start packing. There is no wrong split as long as the three shares are all real and the giving part is honoured.

Sharing with people in need

The share for people in need is the part of Qurbani that reaches furthest. It is the part that turns a family celebration into a community one. If you know the families yourself, pack their share in bags of equal weight so that everyone receives the same. If you give through a mosque or a charity, ask how they would like the meat packed and when they need it by.

Think about dignity as well as weight. Pack the meat cleanly, label the bags if it helps, and deliver it promptly so it arrives fresh. A bag of meat handed over with a smile and an Eid greeting is worth more than a larger bag handed over carelessly. If you are unsure who near you could use a share, your local mosque will usually know.

Storing and packing the shares

Once the shares are worked out, pack them straight away. Keep the meat cool while you work, and get each share into its bag or container as soon as it is weighed. Label the bags if more than one share is going to the same place, so nothing gets mixed up. The family share can go into the fridge or freezer in meal-sized portions, which makes cooking through the Eid days much easier.

Pack the shares for relatives, friends and people in need in sturdy bags that will not leak on the way. If a delivery will take a while, keep the bags cool on the journey. A little care at this stage means the meat arrives in good condition and the sharing feels like the gift it is meant to be, rather than a chore at the end of a long day.

The sacrifice usually takes place after the Eid prayer, and the sharing follows over the coming days, so there is no need to rush everything into a single afternoon.

Plan the practical side in advance: who is coming for the meal, which relatives and friends will receive a share, and how the part for people in need will reach them. Work out the shares with the calculator before the day, so that when the meat is ready you can pack and share without stopping to do sums. A little planning leaves more of the day free for what matters: the prayer, the family, and the feast.

Questions people ask

What is the three-part division of Qurbani meat?

The well-known division splits the meat into three parts: one for the family, one for relatives and friends, and one for people in need. The parts are often equal, but they do not have to be — the calculator lets you choose the split that fits your family.

Can I use my own percentages?

Yes. Choose My own shares and type three percentages that add up to 100, such as 40, 30 and 30. If they do not add up to 100, the calculator will ask you to fix them before it works out the shares.

Does the calculator work in pounds?

Yes. Choose Pounds from the unit menu and enter the total weight in pounds. All three shares are then shown in pounds, and the per-family amount is in pounds too.

How does the per-family amount work?

If you enter the number of families sharing the third part, the calculator divides that share by the number of families and shows how much each family receives. Leave it blank if you do not need it.

Why do the shares always add up exactly?

The calculator rounds each share to two decimal places and then adjusts the largest share by any tiny rounding difference, so the three shares always add up to the total weight you entered.
