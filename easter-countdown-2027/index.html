What Easter Sunday 2027 brings

Easter Sunday in 2027 falls on 28 March, near the end of the month and well into spring. Good Friday, the Friday before Easter, falls on 26 March, and Easter Monday follows on 29 March. Together they make a long weekend that many families use for a break, a gathering, or simply a slower few days at home.

Easter is a moveable feast, which means its date changes every year. It always falls on a Sunday in spring, and the exact day is set by the moon and the season rather than by a fixed date on the calendar. That is why some years Easter arrives in late March and other years it waits until April. In 2027 it arrives early, on the last Sunday of March, just as the days are getting properly long again.

Easter traditions around the home

Easter has a gentle set of traditions that suit a home celebration. Eggs are at the centre of most of them: chocolate eggs hidden around the garden, painted eggs on the table, egg hunts for the children and egg-and-spoon races for anyone willing to look silly. Spring flowers — daffodils, tulips and hyacinths — make the house feel like the season has turned.

Food plays its part too. Hot cross buns belong to Good Friday, and many families sit down to a special lunch on Easter Sunday, often with roast lamb and spring vegetables. Simnel cake, a fruit cake topped with marzipan, is the traditional Easter cake in Britain. None of these traditions needs to be done perfectly. The point is the gathering, the table, and the feeling that winter is over.

Planning an Easter weekend

The Easter weekend in 2027 runs from Good Friday, 26 March, to Easter Monday, 29 March. Four days is enough for a proper break without going far: a visit to family, a day out in the countryside, or a slow weekend at home with an egg hunt on the Sunday morning. If you are travelling, book early — Easter is one of the busiest weekends of the year for trains, roads and places to stay.

Plan the weekend around one or two things that matter, and leave the rest loose. An egg hunt on Sunday morning and a family lunch in the afternoon is a full day for most children. Easter Monday is a good day for a walk, a picnic if the weather allows, or simply a rest after the feast. The countdown above tells you how long you have to get ready.

Easter with kids

Children love Easter because so much of it is theirs: the hunt, the eggs, the bunny, the chocolate. The countdown itself is part of the fun — a child who checks the clock every morning is building the excitement day by day. Let them count the days on their fingers, mark the calendar, and guess what the fact of the day will be.

Keep the celebration at their size. A short egg hunt with a handful of eggs beats a long one that ends in tears. A small basket each beats a mountain of chocolate. And a story about spring and new life, told simply, will stay with them longer than any egg. Making the countdown part of the season

A countdown works best when it becomes a small daily habit. Check it over breakfast, read the fact of the day together, and talk about one thing you are looking forward to at Easter. In the last week, the days tick down fast enough to feel exciting, and on the final day the hours and minutes take over.

You can pair the countdown with your own preparations: buy the eggs when the countdown reaches two weeks, plan the hunt when it reaches one week, and bake or buy the treats in the last few days. By the time the clock reaches zero, the house is ready, the children are excited, and Easter morning can start the moment everyone wakes up.
