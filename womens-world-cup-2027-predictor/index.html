How the predictor works

The predictor builds your path to the final in four stages. In Stage 1, you pick the winner and the runner-up of each of the eight groups — not by team name, but by group slot, because the teams in each group are not known until the final draw on 11 December 2026. In Stage 2, you choose four semi-finalists from the sixteen slots that reached the knockout rounds. In Stage 3, you narrow those four down to two finalists. And in Stage 4, you pick your champion — both the slot and the team name, chosen from the eighteen teams that have already qualified.

At the end, the predictor shows your full path on one screen: your group winners, your semi-finalists, your finalists, and your champion. Save it, share it with friends, or write it down and compare it with the real thing when the tournament ends on 25 July 2027. The predictor never pretends to know the future — it is a game, and the fun is in seeing how close you get.

Why the predictor uses group slots

The groups for the 2027 Women's World Cup do not exist yet. They are formed by the final draw on 11 December 2026, at the Museum of Modern Art in Rio de Janeiro, when the 32 teams are drawn into eight groups of four. Until that draw, no team can be placed in a group, and any predictor that shows you a group table with team names in it is guessing.

That is why this predictor works with slots. Each group has four slots — A1, A2, A3 and A4 for Group A, and so on through Group H. You predict which slot finishes first and second, and after the draw you can look up which teams actually hold those slots. Your prediction then becomes a real prediction about real teams, without ever having invented a group that did not exist. It is the honest way to predict a tournament whose groups are still to be drawn.

Picking your group winners and runners-up

For each group, pick one slot as the winner and a different slot as the runner-up. The predictor will not let you pick the same slot twice — a slot cannot finish first and second at the same time. There is no right way to choose: some people always back the first slot, some spread their picks across the numbers, and some choose by gut feeling alone.

Think about what the slots mean. In most World Cups, the top seeds tend to land in the first slots and the lower-ranked teams in the later ones, but upsets happen in every tournament — that is half the fun. A predictor that picks all eight first slots to win their groups is playing it safe. A predictor that backs a fourth slot to reach the semi-finals is playing for glory. Both are valid, and only July will tell which was wiser.

Choosing your semi-finalists

Once your sixteen knockout slots are set, Stage 2 asks you to pick four of them as your semi-finalists. Choose four different slots — the predictor checks that none is repeated. This is where your prediction starts to take shape: the four slots you choose are the teams you believe will still be standing in the last week of the tournament.

A good approach is to mix the safe with the bold. Two or three of your group winners are natural semi-final picks, but a runner-up that catches fire in the knockout rounds can go all the way — it has happened before, and it will happen again. If you want a prediction you can defend to your friends, pick the four slots you would genuinely back with your reputation. If you want a prediction that makes people laugh, pick the four least likely slots and enjoy the ride.

Naming your champion

The final stage is the one everyone remembers: naming your champion. First pick the winning slot from your two finalists, then pick the team name from the eighteen teams that had already qualified as of mid-November 2026. The list includes the hosts Brazil, the defending champions Spain, Germany, France, Japan, Australia and thirteen more — but not the USA or England, who had not yet qualified when the list was made.

Your champion pick is the heart of your prediction. Spain arrive as champions and favourites. Brazil play at home, in front of their own fans, with all the pressure and all the joy that brings. Germany and Japan have won this tournament before and know what it takes. And every World Cup produces a team nobody saw coming. Pick the team you believe in, not the team you think you should believe in — a prediction you do not believe is not worth making.

What we know about the tournament so far

The Women's World Cup 2027 is played in Brazil from 24 June to 25 July 2027, with 32 teams playing 64 matches across eight host cities: Belo Horizonte, Brasilia, Fortaleza, Porto Alegre, Recife, Rio de Janeiro, Salvador and Sao Paulo. The opening match is Brazil at the Maracana on 24 June, and the final is at the Maracana on 25 July. The format is eight groups of four, with the top two in each group reaching the round of 16, then quarter-finals, semi-finals, a third-place match and the final.

Eighteen teams had qualified as of mid-November 2026, and the other fourteen places are decided by March 2027 through the European play-offs, the Concacaf championship and a final inter-confederation play-off. The draw on 11 December 2026 fills in the groups. Everything else — the fixtures, the kick-off times, the paths to the final — follows from there. Your prediction is a snapshot of what you believe before any of that is known.

Questions people ask

Why do I pick slots instead of teams for the groups?

The groups are not set until the final draw on 11 December 2026. Until the draw, no team can be placed in a group, so the predictor uses group slots — A1 to A4, B1 to B4, and so on — and you look up the team names after the draw.

Which teams can I pick as champion?

The eighteen teams that had already qualified as of mid-November 2026: Brazil, Denmark, France, Germany, Spain, Argentina, Colombia, Australia, China PR, Japan, Korea DPR, Korea Republic, the Philippines, Algeria, Cameroon, Malawi, Morocco and New Zealand.

Why are the USA and England not in the champion list?

Neither team had qualified as of mid-November 2026. The USA were still playing in the Concacaf championship and England were still playing in the European play-offs. The list holds only teams that had already booked their place.

Can I change my picks after I have made them?

Yes. Change any pick at any stage and press the stage button again to rebuild the later stages. Your path updates to match your latest picks.

How is the tournament structured?

Thirty-two teams play in eight groups of four. The top two in each group reach the round of 16, followed by quarter-finals, semi-finals, a third-place match and the final at the Maracana on 25 July 2027.

When is the final draw?

The final draw takes place on Friday 11 December 2026 at the Museum of Modern Art in Rio de Janeiro. Four pots of eight teams are drawn into eight groups of four.
