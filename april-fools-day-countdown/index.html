Harmless-prank ground rules

The best April Fools' pranks are the ones people laugh about afterwards — including the person they were played on. A few ground rules keep the day friendly for everyone.

  • Kind first. The joke should be on the situation, never on someone's feelings, appearance or worries.
  • Reversible. Anything you change should be easy to undo in a minute or two, with no damage and no mess for someone else to clean up.
  • Never scary. No fake emergencies, no sudden shocks, and nothing that could frighten a child, an older relative or a pet.
  • Know your audience. A prank that delights one person can annoy another. If you are not sure they will laugh, choose a gentler idea.
  • Own up quickly. Reveal the joke soon after it lands, so nobody spends the morning confused or worried.
  • No food tricks. Never swap or disguise food or drink — you cannot know what someone must avoid eating.

Easy ideas that stay friendly

Friendly pranks are usually small and silly rather than elaborate. A note under a pillow, a breakfast served in a funny way, or a clock turned backwards by an hour can all raise a smile without costing anyone anything.

Paper is your friend: a trail of paper footprints, a drawing taped inside a cupboard door, or a lovingly silly certificate for a family award are all harmless and easy to tidy away. The same goes for a harmless mix-up, like swapping two family members' mugs or serving dessert before the main course, just this once.

The real secret is timing. Play the joke early, reveal it quickly, and let the rest of the day be ordinary. That way the laughter is the memory, not the mess.

A short history of April Fools' Day

Nobody knows for certain where April Fools' Day began, and that uncertainty is part of its charm. The story most often told takes us back to France in the 1500s, when the new Gregorian calendar moved the start of the year from late March to 1 January. People who kept celebrating the new year in late March, either because they had not heard the news or because they refused to change, were teased as fools, and the teasing settled on 1 April. It is a good story, and it is the one most histories repeat, but the truth is that spring festivals of mischief and role-swapping existed long before calendars changed, in many different countries.

What we do know is that by the 1600s and 1700s, 1 April was firmly established in Britain and France as a day of light-hearted tricks. In France the day became known for the poisson d'avril, the April fish: children would try to stick a paper fish onto someone's back without being noticed, and the joke was to see how long the fish stayed there. The fish is still part of the day in France today, and it shows what April Fools' Day has always been at its best: a joke you can see, a laugh everyone shares, and nothing that leaves a mark.

The day travelled with settlers and sailors and became a shared tradition across the English-speaking world. Newspapers and, later, radio and television joined in with harmless hoaxes, and in recent years brands and websites have done the same. The spirit has stayed the same through all of it: a day when the ordinary rules bend a little, when a small surprise is welcome, and when the best jokes are the ones that make the person who fell for them laugh hardest of all.

More harmless prank ideas for home

The best home pranks need almost nothing: a pen, some paper, and a sense of timing. A favourite is the backwards morning: set the table for dinner at breakfast time, serve the meal in the wrong order, and act as if nothing is unusual. Another is the travelling object: move one small thing, a favourite mug, a cushion, a plant, to a slightly different spot every hour, and wait to see when someone notices. The joke is gentle, it costs nothing, and it gives the whole household something to laugh about together.

Paper pranks are easy to tidy away, which makes them ideal for families. A paper trail of footprints leading to the front door, a drawing taped inside a cupboard, or a silly certificate naming a family member the winner of a made-up award are all harmless and take minutes to prepare. A note under a pillow or inside a lunchbox works the same way: the surprise is the joke, and the joke is kind. The rule to remember is that the fun should last a minute and the tidying should take no longer.

Timing matters as much as the idea. Play the joke early in the day, reveal it quickly, and then let the rest of the day be ordinary. A prank that drags on stops being funny and starts being annoying, and a prank that leaves someone confused for hours is not a joke at all. The happiest April Fools' mornings are the ones where everyone has laughed by breakfast, and the story gets retold at dinner.

April Fools' fun at school and work

In a classroom, April Fools' Day is a chance to play with words and ideas rather than with people. A teacher might write a silly sentence on the board and ask the class to spot the trick, or hand out a quiz where every question has a playful twist. Children can try sticking a paper fish on a willing teacher's back, or writing harmless joke notes for their friends. The best classroom pranks are the ones the whole class is in on together: a shared joke, not a joke at one child's expense.

Offices can join in too, with the same rules. A desk decorated with paper streamers, a meeting agenda with one joke item slipped in, or a cheerful fake announcement that is revealed within minutes can all lift the mood. The key is to keep it short and to choose ideas that nobody has to clean up. Anything that wastes a colleague's time, touches their work, or could embarrass them in front of others is not worth it, however funny it seems at first.

Wherever you play the day, the same test applies: would the person you are joking with laugh if the joke were played on you? If the answer is yes, go ahead. If you are not sure, choose a gentler idea. April Fools' Day is only a day, but the way a joke makes someone feel lasts much longer, and the kindest jokes are the ones people remember fondly years later.

Questions people ask

When is April Fools' Day?

April Fools' Day is on 1 April every year. The countdown at the top of this page shows the time left until the next one begins.

What time does the countdown end?

The countdown ends at midnight local time at the start of 1 April, so it reaches zero just as the day begins.

What makes a prank harmless?

A harmless prank is kind, reversible and never scary. It should be easy to undo, leave no mess for someone else, and leave everyone laughing, including the person it was played on.

What should I never prank about?

Never fake an emergency, a breakage, an illness or a job or school problem. Never disguise food or drink, and never play a prank that could frighten a child, an older relative or a pet.

Can children join in?

Yes, with gentle ideas. Paper jokes, silly breakfasts and harmless mix-ups are perfect for children, and they learn the best lesson of the day: a good joke is kind to everyone in it.

Is April Fools' Day a public holiday?

No. 1 April is an ordinary working day in most places, which is part of the fun: the jokes happen at school, at work and at home, in the middle of an ordinary day.
