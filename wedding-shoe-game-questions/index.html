How to play the shoe game

Seat the couple back to back, each holding one of their own shoes and one of their partner's. The host reads a question aloud, and on the count of three both partners raise the shoe of the person the question describes. Matching answers get a cheer, and mismatched answers get the biggest laughs of the night.

Play for about ten to fifteen minutes, using ten to fifteen questions. The Love Story questions warm the room up, Daily Life gets everyone nodding along, Funny Habits brings the laughter, and Future Plans ends the game on a sweet note.

Tips for the host

  • Read each question slowly and clearly so the whole room can hear.
  • Count to three before the couple raise their shoes, so neither can copy the other.
  • Skip any question that does not fit the couple — the generator makes it easy to draw another.
  • Keep the mood light: the game is a celebration, not a test.

Questions people ask

How does the wedding shoe game work?

The couple sit back to back, each holding one of their own shoes and one of their partner's. The host reads a question, and both partners raise the shoe of the person the question describes. Matching answers get a cheer and mismatches get a laugh.

How many questions should we use?

Ten to fifteen questions is ideal for a reception, which takes about ten to fifteen minutes. Start with the Love Story group, mix in Daily Life and Funny Habits, and finish with Future Plans.

What do we need to play?

Two chairs, the couple's own shoes, and a host to read the questions. A microphone helps in a large room so every guest can hear.

Can we skip questions that do not fit the couple?

Yes. Every question here is kind and family-safe, but if one does not fit the couple, just draw another. The generator never repeats a question until all of them have been used.

When is the best time to play the shoe game?

After the meal and before the dancing works well, when guests are relaxed and settled. It also works beautifully at a rehearsal dinner or an engagement party.
