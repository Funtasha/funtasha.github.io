Digital Product Delivery Platform

Digital delivery means a buyer pays and receives files instead of a parcel. This guide explains how delivery works, how to choose a platform, and how to handle licences and refunds.

This page teaches you how to set up digital delivery. It is not a store, it sells nothing, and it cannot deliver a product to you or to anyone else.

What digital product delivery means

A digital product is something a buyer can download instead of waiting for a parcel: an ebook, a template, a music file, a set of photos, a course, or a piece of software. Digital delivery is the journey that product takes from your computer to the buyer's, starting the moment they pay and ending when the files are safely on their device.

The whole point of digital delivery is that it happens without you. A buyer in another time zone should be able to pay at three in the morning and get their files at once, with no email from you and no waiting. If delivery needs you to be awake and at your desk, it is not really set up yet.

The journey from payment to download

Every digital sale follows the same short journey. The buyer chooses a product, pays through a payment page, and the system checks that the payment went through. Then the buyer gets access to the files, either through a download link, a download page, or an account library. A confirmation message tells them what they bought and where their files are.

Each step can fail, so each step needs a plan. If the payment succeeds but the link never arrives, the buyer will write to you. If the link arrives but the file will not open, they will ask for a refund. A good delivery setup handles the common failures on its own: the link can be resent, the file can be downloaded again, and the buyer can see what they bought without asking you.

Delivery methods compared

There are four common ways to get files to a buyer. The right one depends on what you sell and how much setup you want to do. A single ebook needs very little, while a course with ten lessons needs a place where buyers can come back and watch them in order.

Most sellers start with email links or platform delivery, because both work with almost no setup. An account library is worth the extra work when you sell courses or products that you update, because buyers can always get the newest version from one place.

Choosing where to sell

You have three broad choices: a marketplace, a hosted store, or your own website. A marketplace, such as Etsy for digital downloads, brings buyers to you, but you follow its rules and its layout. A hosted store builder, such as Shopify, gives you your own shop without building it yourself. Your own website gives you full control and the most work.

Course platforms are a fourth choice for sellers whose product is teaching. Platforms such as Teachable and Podia are built around lessons, student progress and course libraries. If your product is a course, a course platform saves you from building lesson pages yourself. If your product is a file, a simple store or marketplace is usually enough.

There is no single best choice. A seller with one ebook and no audience may do best on a marketplace, where buyers are already browsing. A seller with a mailing list and a growing catalogue may do best with their own store, where every sale builds their own brand. Start small, learn what your buyers need, and move when the setup you have starts to hold you back.

Licences: what buyers actually get

A licence is the set of rules that comes with a digital product. It says what the buyer may do with the files: use them for themselves, use them in their own work, or share them with a team. Most small sellers use a simple personal licence, which means one buyer, personal use, no sharing and no reselling. A commercial licence costs more and allows business use.

Write your licence in plain words on the product page, before the buyer pays. A buyer who finds out after paying that they cannot use a template for client work will feel misled, and that feeling turns into refund requests and bad reviews. A short licence in plain language prevents most of those problems.

You do not need a lawyer to write a simple licence, but you do need to be honest about what you promise. If you say a buyer may use a file forever, mean it. If you say updates are included, include them. The licence is part of the product, and keeping its promises is part of the job.

Pricing without guessing

Pricing a digital product feels hard because there is no cost of materials to start from. Start from the value to the buyer instead. A template that saves someone three hours of work is worth more than a template that saves ten minutes. A course that teaches a skill someone can charge for is worth more than a course that is only interesting.

Look at what similar products sell for, and place yours honestly among them. A first product at a low price, for example 5 or 10 in your own currency, is a reasonable way to get your first sales and reviews. As you add products and learn what buyers want, you can raise prices on the products that sell best. Changing a price is easy with digital products, so you do not need to get it perfect on the first day.

Avoid pricing by panic. Cutting a price in half because of one slow week teaches buyers to wait for the next cut. A steady price, with the occasional honest discount for a launch or a holiday, builds more trust than a price that jumps around.

Refunds and unhappy buyers

Digital products cannot be returned in the usual way, because a buyer who downloads a file still has it after a refund. That is why a clear refund policy matters more for digital products than for physical ones. Write it before your first sale, put it on the product page, and keep it short enough that people actually read it.

Many sellers offer a refund within a short window, such as 7 or 14 days, when the product does not work as described or the buyer cannot open the files. Some sellers offer no refunds at all, which is allowed in some places and not in others, so check the rules where your buyers live before you choose. Whatever you choose, apply it the same way to everyone.

When a buyer is unhappy, reply quickly and calmly. Most unhappy buyers want one of three things: the file to work, an explanation, or their money back. Fix the file if you can, explain clearly if the product was misunderstood, and refund without an argument when the product genuinely failed. A fast, fair reply often turns an unhappy buyer into a repeat customer.

Protecting your files from sharing

Be honest with yourself about this: you cannot fully stop a buyer from sharing a file. Anyone who can open a file can send it to a friend. The goal is not perfect protection, which does not exist, but making honest buying easier than sharing. A fair price, instant delivery and good support make buying the easy choice for most people.

There are still sensible steps worth taking. Use download links that expire after a few days or a few downloads, so one link does not spread forever. Keep your files in a place buyers reach through your store, not in a public folder anyone can guess. Add your name or business name inside the product, so a shared copy still carries your name. These steps slow casual sharing without punishing honest buyers.

Do not punish honest buyers to catch dishonest ones. Heavy locks, passwords on every file and limits that stop a buyer opening their own purchase on a second device create support messages and refunds. Most buyers are honest. Build your delivery for them, and accept that a small amount of sharing is part of selling digital products.

Your launch checklist

  1. Finish the product and test every file by downloading it yourself on a phone and a computer.
  2. Write the product page: what it is, what it contains, who it is for, and the licence in plain words.
  3. Write the refund policy and put it on the product page before the first sale.
  4. Test the whole journey: buy your own product with a test payment and follow the link as a buyer would.
  5. Write the confirmation message buyers receive, with a clear link to their files.
  6. Prepare answers for the three most likely questions: how to download, how to open the files, and how to ask for help.
  7. Tell your first buyers the product exists, and ask the earliest ones what they thought.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Selling a product before testing the download yourself on a phone.
  • Hiding the licence and refund policy where buyers cannot find them.
  • Pricing by panic, with cuts and jumps that teach buyers to wait.
  • Locking files so tightly that honest buyers cannot open their own purchase.
  • Forgetting that buyers live in different time zones, so delivery must work while you sleep.
  • Never updating a product after launch, so early buyers feel forgotten.

Questions people ask

What counts as a digital product?

Anything a buyer downloads instead of receiving by post: ebooks, templates, music, photos, software, fonts, printables and online courses. If it can be delivered as a file or through an account, it is a digital product.

Do I need a website to sell digital products?

No. Marketplaces let you list a product without a website of your own, and hosted store builders give you a shop without building one. A website of your own gives you more control, but it is not required to start.

How do buyers get their files?

Usually by a download link sent by email, a download page shown after payment, or an account library where their purchases live. The method depends on the platform you choose, and all three should work without you being online.

What is a licence for a digital product?

A licence is the set of rules that comes with the files. It says whether the buyer may use the product for themselves only, for business work, or share it with a team. Write it in plain words on the product page before the buyer pays.

Should I offer refunds on digital products?

A clear refund policy builds trust, even if it is a short window for products that do not work as described. Rules about refunds differ by country, so check the rules where your buyers live before you decide.

How do I stop people sharing my files?

You cannot fully stop sharing, and honest sellers accept that. Expiring download links, files kept behind your store, and your name inside the product all help. The best protection is a fair price and instant delivery, which make buying easier than sharing.
