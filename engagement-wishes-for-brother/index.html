These wishes are starting points, not finished cards. A line lands best when it carries a detail only you know — a memory of the two of them, the proposal story, or the moment you knew they were right together.

How to make it your own

A wish becomes yours the moment you add something only you could write. Try one of these before you send it:

  • Mention the proposal: where it happened, how he asked, or the look on her face when she said yes.
  • Name a memory of the two of them together, so the wish fits their story and no one else's.
  • Sign it the way you always do — a nickname or a family joke beats a formal ending.

Questions people ask

What should I write in an engagement card for my brother?

Start with a wish from the sweet section, then add one detail only you know — the proposal, a memory of the couple, or a hope for their future. Two or three heartfelt sentences are plenty.

How long should an engagement message be?

Two or three sentences is enough for a card, and one sentence is enough for a text. A short message that is personal beats a long one that could be for anyone.

Can I send these wishes by text message?

Yes. The short wishes are written to fit a text, and the copy button copies the whole wish so you can paste it straight into your message.

What if I want something funny instead of sweet?

Use the funny section — every line is kind and good-natured. If you are writing to someone who loves a joke, pair one funny wish with one sweet line.

How do I make an engagement wish more personal?

Add a shared memory, name the couple's story or their plans, and sign it the way you always do. The wish gives you the words; the personal detail makes them yours.
