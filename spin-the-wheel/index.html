Spin the Wheel: Random Picker Wheel

Type your options into the box, press SPIN, and the wheel picks one at random. Every option gets an equal chance, and the wheel always lands exactly where the pick says it should.

Press SPIN to pick a winner.

The wheel is only as fair as the options you type in. If the same option appears twice, it gets two segments and twice the chance, so check your list before you spin.

How to use the wheel

Start by typing your options into the box, one per line. Press Update wheel to draw them on the wheel, then press SPIN and watch it slow down onto one winner. The winner's full name appears below the wheel, even if a long name was shortened to fit its segment.

Use the wheel whenever a group cannot decide: pick a classroom volunteer, draw a raffle winner, choose tonight's film, settle who does the washing up, or pick a game for game night. Press Shuffle to mix the order of the segments, and press Remove winner and spin again to take the winner out and pick another — handy for drawing several names in a row.

How the wheel stays fair

The result is decided before the wheel moves. When you press SPIN, the page first draws the winning segment with a uniform random pick, so every option has exactly the same chance, and only then animates the wheel to land on it. The long, slowing spin is just for show — it cannot change the result.

That fairness depends on the list you type in. The wheel gives one segment to each line, so a repeated line gets a bigger slice of the wheel and a better chance. If you want a truly even draw, make sure every option appears once.

Wheel ideas for classrooms, parties and giveaways

The wheel is at its best when a group needs a fair pick and everyone can see it happen. In a classroom, it picks the next reader, the next presenter, or the group that goes first — and because the choice is visibly random, nobody can feel singled out. Teachers use it for quick quizzes too: put the topics on the wheel and let it choose what the class revises next.

At a party, the wheel turns small decisions into entertainment. Put the games on the wheel and let it choose what to play next, or put the guests' names on it for a raffle draw. A wheel draw at a birthday party feels special in a way that pulling a name from a hat never quite matches, because the whole room watches the wheel slow down. For a family game night, put the evening's activities on the wheel and let chance plan the night.

For giveaways, the wheel is a simple, honest way to pick a winner in front of everyone. Type the entrants' names in, spin once, and the winner is chosen where everyone can see it. If there are several prizes, use Remove winner and spin again to draw the next name — the wheel keeps going until the prizes run out. The public, visible spin is what makes it fair: nobody has to take your word for it, because they watched it happen.

The wheel also works for smaller groups and quieter moments. Two friends deciding what to watch, a family choosing tonight's dinner, a team picking who brings the snacks — the wheel settles these in seconds and turns a small argument into a small laugh. The best wheel lists are short, clear, and made together, so everyone feels the result belongs to the group. Some lists to try:

  • Film night: comedy, adventure, mystery, animation, documentary, musical.
  • Dinner picks: pasta, soup, stir-fry, salad, curry, sandwiches.
  • Party games: charades, quiz, dancing, bingo, treasure hunt, karaoke.
  • Classroom jobs: board cleaner, book collector, line leader, time keeper.
  • Weekend plans: picnic, museum, park, baking, board games, bike ride.
  • Prize draw: put every entrant's name on the wheel, one per line.

Using the wheel to settle everyday decisions

Some decisions are too small to argue about and too annoying to leave open, and the wheel is made for exactly those. What to have for dinner, which film to watch, who does the washing up, where to go on a Sunday walk — put the options on the wheel, spin once, and accept the answer. The wheel does not make the decision better, but it makes it faster, and it ends the back-and-forth that turns a small choice into a long debate.

The key is to agree before the spin that the result stands. A wheel only settles a decision if everyone accepts the outcome, so make the rule clear: one spin, one answer, no re-spins because you did not like the result. If the result genuinely does not work — the wheel picked a walk and it is raining — then spin again with the impossible option removed, and say so openly. Honesty about the rules is what keeps the wheel fun rather than frustrating.

The wheel is also a good way to break a tie. When two people want different things and neither will budge, a spin gives both sides an equal chance and nobody has to give in. The loser of a spin has nothing to resent, because the choice was chance, not a person. That is why the wheel works so well with children: a random pick feels fair in a way that a grown-up's decision sometimes does not.

Fairness notes: getting an honest spin

A fair spin starts with a fair list. Every option should appear once, because a repeated line gets two segments and twice the chance. Before you spin, read the list out loud and check it together — it takes ten seconds, and it means nobody can wonder afterwards whether the list was loaded. If the wheel is picking a winner for a prize, let the group see the list before the spin, so the result is beyond question.

The wheel's result is decided by a random draw before the animation begins, and the spin itself is only for show — so the only way to make a spin unfair is to mess with the list. That is worth remembering when the stakes feel high: the wheel cannot be talked into a result, and no amount of hoping changes the odds. Each option has the same chance on every spin, whatever happened last time.

One more fairness note: do not re-spin to change an answer you dislike. If the group agreed to accept the result, accept it — re-spinning until you get the answer you wanted is not a random draw, it is a rigged one. If the first spin feels wrong to everyone, the honest fix is to change the list and start again openly, not to spin quietly until the wheel agrees with you. A wheel that is used honestly stays fun for everyone; a wheel that is used to force a result stops being a game at all.

Questions people ask

How many options can the wheel hold?

The wheel holds 2 to 12 options. Type one option per line in the box and press Update wheel. If you type fewer than 2 or more than 12, the page will ask you to fix the list before spinning.

Is the pick really random?

Yes. Each spin starts with a uniform random draw that picks the winning segment, so every option has exactly the same chance. The wheel then animates to land on the segment that was picked.

What happens if I type the same option twice?

That option gets two segments on the wheel, so it has twice the chance of being picked. Remove the duplicate line if you want every option to have an equal chance.

Can I remove the winner and spin again?

Yes. Press Remove winner and spin again after a spin, and the winner leaves the wheel. You can keep removing winners until fewer than 2 options are left.

Does the wheel work on a phone?

Yes. The wheel runs entirely in your browser with no sign-up and no download, and it works on phones, tablets, and computers.
