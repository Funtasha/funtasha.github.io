Every question here is written to be funny or flattering, never embarrassing. Anyone can skip a question they do not enjoy — just draw another one and keep the game kind.

How to play Most Likely To

Gather your group and draw a question. Everyone points at the person they think the question describes best — or shouts a name — and the fun is in the debate that follows. There are no right or wrong answers, and no scores are needed, though you can keep count of who gets the most votes if you like.

The generator shows each question once before repeating any, so a long game night never runs dry. Pick a group to suit your players, or play with all groups mixed together for the widest variety.

Tips for a kind game

  • Keep it light: every question here is written to be funny or flattering, never embarrassing.
  • Let anyone skip a question they do not enjoy — just draw another one.
  • Mix groups so everyone gets a turn in the spotlight.
  • With children, use the School and Everyday Life groups first.

Questions people ask

How do you play Most Likely To?

Draw a question and everyone points at the person they think it describes best. There are no right answers — the fun is in the debate, the stories, and the surprises.

Will the same question come up twice?

Not until every question in your chosen group has been shown. The generator shuffles the questions and only starts a fresh round once all of them have been used.

Are the questions family-safe?

Yes. All 85 questions are written to be funny or flattering, never embarrassing, and they work for family gatherings, classrooms and parties with mixed ages.

How many questions are there?

There are 85 questions across five groups: Funny, Family, Friends, School and Everyday Life. Use the group menu to play one group or mix them all together.

Can I keep score?

You can if you like — give a point to the person with the most votes each round. Most groups play just for laughs and skip the scoring entirely.
