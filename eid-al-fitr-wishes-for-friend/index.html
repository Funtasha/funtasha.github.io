These wishes are starting points, not finished cards. Blessings here are worded generally, so a line lands best when it carries a detail only you know — a memory you share, a plan for the day, or the reason this friend matters to you.

How to make it your own

A wish becomes yours the moment you add something only you could write. Try one of these before you send it:

  • Name a memory: a shared meal, a long evening, or an Eid you celebrated together.
  • Mention their plans for the day, so the wish fits their story and no one else's.
  • Sign it the way you always do — a nickname or a private joke beats a formal ending.

Questions people ask

What should I write in an Eid message for a friend?

Start with a wish from the warm section, then add one detail only you know — a shared memory, their plans for the day, or a hope for their year ahead. Two or three heartfelt sentences are plenty.

How long should an Eid message be?

Two or three sentences is enough for a card, and one sentence is enough for a text. A short message that is personal beats a long one that could be for anyone.

Can I send these wishes by text message?

Yes. The short wishes are written to fit a text, and the copy button copies the whole wish so you can paste it straight into your message.

What if I want something festive instead of warm?

Use the festive section — every line celebrates the day itself, from the food to the gathering. Pair one festive wish with one warm line for a full card.

How do I make an Eid wish more personal?

Add a shared memory, name their plans for the day, and sign it the way you always do. The wish gives you the words; the personal detail makes them yours.
