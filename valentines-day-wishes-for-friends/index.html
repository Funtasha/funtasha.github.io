These wishes are starting points, not finished cards. A line lands best when it carries a detail only you know — a shared memory, an inside joke, or the time your friend showed up when it mattered.

How to make it your own

A wish becomes yours the moment you add something only you could write. Try one of these before you send it:

  • Name a memory: a trip together, a late-night talk, or the time they showed up when it mattered.
  • Mention an inside joke or a habit you love — the small things say more than grand words.
  • Sign it the way you always do — a nickname or a phrase only the two of you use.

Questions people ask

Is it normal to send Valentine's wishes to friends?

Yes. Valentine's Day is a day for all kinds of love, and a warm message to a friend is a lovely way to say you value them. These wishes are written for friendship only.

What should I write in a Valentine's card for a friend?

Start with a wish from the warm section, then add one detail only you know — a shared memory, an inside joke, or a thank-you for something they did. Two or three sentences are plenty.

Can I send these wishes by text message?

Yes. The short wishes are written to fit a text, and the copy button copies the whole wish so you can paste it straight into your message.

How do I keep a Valentine's message friendly, not romantic?

Stick to friendship words: gratitude, laughter, and shared memories. Every wish on this page is written that way, with no romantic wording.

How do I make a Valentine's wish more personal?

Add a shared memory or an inside joke, and sign it the way you always do. The wish gives you the words; the personal detail makes them yours.
