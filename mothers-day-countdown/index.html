How the countdown works

Mother's Day falls on the second Sunday of May. That is the rule, stated plainly: find the first Sunday of May, and Mother's Day is the Sunday after it. The date moves from year to year, but the rule never changes.

This page works the date out itself, in code. It finds the first Sunday of May in the current year, adds seven days to reach the second Sunday, and counts down to midnight at the start of that day in your local time. It updates every second while the page is open.

When Mother's Day arrives, the countdown shows that the day is here. Once the day is over, it rolls forward on its own to the second Sunday of the following May, so the page never needs updating and never goes out of date. The countdown runs entirely in your browser, using the clock on your own device.

Planning a Mother's Day surprise

The best Mother's Day surprises are the ones that show you were thinking of her, not just of the day. A few ideas:

  • Start the day with breakfast in bed, made with her favourite things.
  • Write a short handwritten letter about something she did that you still remember.
  • Cook her favourite meal, and let her sit down while you do the washing up.
  • Pick a photo from the year and have it printed, with a note on the back.
  • Spend time together doing what she loves, with no phones and no hurry.
The countdown runs on your device clock, so it is only as accurate as the time on your phone or computer. If your device clock is wrong, the countdown will be too.

Mother's Day gift ideas for every budget

A good Mother's Day gift does not need to cost a lot — it needs to show that you noticed something about her. The best gifts start from what she actually enjoys, not from a shop display. Think about her week: what does she reach for every morning, what does she talk about wanting to try, what would make an ordinary day a little easier or a little brighter? A gift that answers one of those questions will always beat a grand gesture chosen in a hurry. Mother's Day falls on different dates in different countries, and this page counts down to the second Sunday of May, the date used in many countries — so check the date where your mum lives if she is in another country.

A small budget can go a long way when the gift is personal. A handwritten letter, a framed photo with a note on the back, a playlist of songs she loves, or a homemade treat from her favourite recipe all cost little and say a great deal. The effort is the gift: the time you spent choosing the photo, writing the letter, or baking the cake is exactly what makes it feel special.

A medium budget opens up experiences as well as things. A meal out together, a bunch of her favourite flowers, a book by an author she loves, or a small plant for the windowsill are all thoughtful choices. The trick is to pair the gift with time — a gift handed over in a rush feels like an errand, while the same gift given over a slow cup of tea feels like a celebration.

A larger budget is best spent on something she would never buy for herself. That might be a day out somewhere she has always wanted to visit, a class in something she has talked about learning, or a treat for the house she has had her eye on. Whatever the size of the budget, the rule stays the same: the gift should fit the person, not the price tag.

Breakfast in bed and a card she will keep

Breakfast in bed is the classic Mother's Day start for a good reason: it costs little, it takes real effort, and it says "today, you rest" more clearly than any gift. Keep it simple and make it hers — her favourite tea or coffee, the breakfast she actually likes rather than the one that looks best in a photograph, and a small flower on the tray if you have one. The point is not a perfect hotel breakfast; it is waking up to find someone else has taken care of the morning.

The card matters as much as the breakfast, and it is the part most people rush. A card bought at the last minute with a single line inside is easy to forget; a card with three or four honest sentences is kept for years. Write about something specific: a moment from the year when she made a difference, a habit of hers you admire, or a small thing she does every day that nobody else notices. Specific beats grand every time — "thank you for the soup when I was ill" means more than "you are the best mum in the world" because only you could have written it.

If children are making the card, let them make it themselves, however wobbly the drawings. A handprint, a spelling mistake, a drawing of the family where everyone is the wrong size — these are the cards that get kept in a drawer for decades. Grown-ups can take the same lesson: a card that sounds like you, in your own words, is worth more than a perfect verse copied from anywhere else.

How to make the whole day special

The secret to a special Mother's Day is not one big moment but a day shaped around her. Start by asking yourself what a perfect ordinary day looks like for her, then remove the chores from it. If she loves a lie-in, protect it. If she loves a walk, plan the route. If she loves a quiet afternoon with a book, guard it from interruptions. The day should feel like her day, not like a schedule of treats she has to keep up with.

Share the day if you can. A phone call from a child who lives far away, a video call with the grandchildren, or a message from someone she has not heard from in a while can be the highlight of the whole day. If the family is spread out, agree a time for everyone to call, so the day has a thread of voices running through it. And if Mother's Day is a difficult day for someone in the family — because a mother is far away, or no longer here — make room for that too. A quiet moment of remembrance, a flower laid somewhere meaningful, or simply saying her name out loud can be the kindest part of the day.

End the day the way it started: with her feet up. Cook the evening meal, or order in her favourite food, and take care of the washing up without being asked. The countdown on this page tells you how long you have to plan — use it. A day planned a week ahead, with the card written and the breakfast ingredients bought, is a relaxed day for everyone, and a relaxed day is a happy one.
