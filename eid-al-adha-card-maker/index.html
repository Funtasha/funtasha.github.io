Eid al-Adha Card Maker

By the Funtasha team · Published 19 November 2026 · Updated 19 November 2026

Design a personal Eid al-Adha card in a minute: pick a design, add a name and a message, and download it to share. Everything is drawn in your browser, so your words stay private.

The card downloads as a picture file you can send by message or print at home.

How the card maker works

The card maker draws your card on the page as you type. Pick one of the four designs, write the name of the person the card is for, and choose a message from the list or write your own. The preview updates at once, so you can see exactly what the finished card will look like.

When you are happy with it, press Download the card. The picture is saved to your device as a file you can send by message, share online, or print at home. You can make as many cards as you like, one for each person, and each one can have a different design and message.

Choosing a design

There are four designs to choose from. Desert sunset is warm and golden, with a low sun over rolling dunes. Lantern glow is deep green with hanging lanterns. Olive branch is light and peaceful, with olive branches on soft green, and Starry night is deep blue with a bright crescent.

There is no right choice. Pick the one that suits the person you are sending it to: a warm design for someone who loves colour, a calm one for someone who likes simple things. If you cannot decide, make two and send the one you like best.

Writing a message for Eid al-Adha

Eid al-Adha is a festival of sacrifice, sharing, and gratitude, and the messages on this page reflect that spirit. They speak of accepted sacrifice, of tables shared with family and neighbours, and of thankfulness for what we have.

A personal line makes any message better. Mention something real: a kindness the person showed, a meal you shared, or a hope for the year ahead. Keep it short and sincere, and read it out loud before you download the card: if it sounds like you, it is right.

If you are writing to someone who has had a hard year, a gentle message means the most. A few words of comfort, a wish for easier days, and the knowledge that someone was thinking of them: that is a card worth receiving, whatever the design around it.

Downloading and sharing your card

The Download button saves your card as a picture file on your device. From there you can send it in a message, attach it to an email, or share it on a family group chat. The picture is 600 pixels wide and 400 tall, which looks clear on a phone screen and prints neatly on a home printer.

If you plan to print it, use card or thick paper if you have it, and cut along the edges with care. A printed card, handed over in person with a smile, is still the nicest way to give one, even in a year when most cards travel by phone.

Cards for family far away

Eid al-Adha is a time when families are often spread across cities and countries, and a card is a small way to close the distance. A personal card, with a name and a message written for one person, says more than a forwarded greeting ever can.

If you are sending cards to several relatives, make each one a little different: change the design, change the message, or add a line about something you shared. It takes only a minute per card, and the person who receives it will know it was made for them alone.

For relatives you rarely see, a card can be the start of a longer conversation. Follow it with a phone call, or a message asking how their Eid went. The card opens the door; the conversation is what walks through it.

Making cards with children

Children love making cards, and this page is an easy way to start. Let a child pick the design and the message, and type the name of a grandparent, an aunt, or a friend. Younger children can dictate a message for an adult to type.

Print the finished card and let the child decorate the back with a drawing, or stick it on the fridge until Eid morning. A card a child helped to make is often the one grandparents keep longest, long after the bought cards have been put away.

A note on names and photos

Use only names you have permission to share on a card. A card for a family member is a lovely gift; a card that names someone who did not agree to it, or that pokes fun at a real person, is not. Keep every card kind, and keep it about celebration.

This card maker does not use photos: the designs are drawn on the page, and nothing you type leaves your browser. That keeps your words private, and it means you can make a card anywhere, without uploading anything at all.

Eid al-Adha 2027: when to send your card

Eid al-Adha 2027 is expected on Sunday 16 May 2027. The exact date depends on the sighting of the moon, so it may move by a day, but the festival itself is certain: a day of prayer, sharing, and family, when meat is shared with relatives, friends, and people in need.

The best time to send a card is the evening before Eid, or early on Eid morning, so it is waiting when the person wakes. If you are printing cards, make them a day or two earlier, so there is time to write a line by hand on the back.

Use only names and words you have permission to share, and never use the card to mock a real person. The card is drawn fresh in your browser; nothing you type is sent anywhere.

Questions people ask

How do I download my card?

Press the Download the card button under the preview. The card is saved to your device as a picture file, which you can send by message, share online, or print at home.

Can I use my own message?

Yes. Choose Write my own message from the message list, then type your words in the box below it. The preview updates as you type, so you can see how the message looks on the card.

What size is the card?

The card is 600 pixels wide and 400 pixels tall. That looks clear on a phone screen and prints neatly on a home printer, on plain or thick paper.

What is the difference between this and the Eid al-Fitr card maker?

The two card makers work the same way, but this one has designs and messages written for Eid al-Adha, the festival of sacrifice and sharing, while the other is written for Eid al-Fitr at the end of Ramadan.

Can I make more than one card?

Yes, as many as you like. Change the design, the name, or the message, and download again. Each card is drawn fresh, so you can make a different one for every person.

Does the card maker work on a phone?

Yes. The card maker runs entirely in your browser with no sign-up and no download, and it works on phones, tablets, and computers.
